Google Ads for dentists: a complete guide for UK practices

By Cristian Savulescu, Founder · Published 26 October 2026

How to set up, run and measure Google Ads for a dental practice, from keywords and budgets to landing pages, compliance and tracking every new patient.

Google Ads, often called PPC or pay-per-click advertising, can put a dental practice at the top of Google within days of launch, in front of patients who are searching for a dentist right now. Run well, it is one of the most predictable ways to win new patients. Run badly, it is one of the quickest ways to spend a marketing budget with nothing to show for it. This guide covers how Google Ads for dentists works, how to set up and structure a dental Google Ads account, what it costs in the UK, and how to measure whether your advertising is paying for itself.

Why Google Ads works so well as advertising for dentists

Most advertising interrupts people. Google Ads reaches patients at the moment they are searching for dental services, such as "emergency dentist near me" or "dental implants Sheffield". These searchers already have a need and are usually close to booking, so the leads tend to be higher quality than those from most other channels.

Four things make Google Ads a good fit for a dental practice:

  • Speed. Search engine optimisation (SEO) builds visibility over months. A Google Ads campaign can produce enquiries in the first week.
  • Control. You decide which treatments to promote, which areas to target, when your ads run and how much you spend each day.
  • Measurability. With the right tracking, you can see what every campaign and keyword costs per enquiry and per new patient.
  • Value. One dental implant or Invisalign case can be worth thousands of pounds, so a campaign only needs a handful of extra patients a month to deliver a strong return on investment (ROI).

If you are still weighing up whether paid search is right for your practice, our honest look at whether Google Ads is worth it for dentists covers the trade-offs, and our comparison of dental SEO vs PPC explains how the two channels work together.

How to set up and structure a dental Google Ads account

Most underperforming dental Google Ads accounts share the same problem: a single campaign holding dozens of unrelated keywords, all pointing at the homepage. Google can't match the right ad to the right search, quality drops and costs rise. A well-structured Google Ads account separates your services so each search meets a relevant ad and a relevant page.

Campaigns by treatment

Create a separate campaign for each treatment you want more of, for example dental implants, Invisalign, cosmetic dentistry and emergency appointments. Each ad campaign gets its own budget, so a high-value treatment never runs out of money because general dentistry searches used it up.

Ad groups by intent

Inside each campaign, group tightly related keywords together. An implant campaign might have one ad group for single-tooth implants and another for full-arch or "teeth in a day" searches. Every ad group gets ads written for those searches, which lifts your click-through rate and your Quality Score.

Search campaigns first

Search campaigns capture patients who are actively looking for a dentist, so they should carry most of the budget. Once search is profitable, remarketing and Performance Max can extend your reach, but they rarely make sense on their own for a dental clinic.

Choosing the right keywords for dental Google Ads

Keyword selection decides whether your ad spend reaches potential patients or disappears on curiosity searches. The best keywords combine a treatment with a place or a strong buying signal:

  • Treatment and location: "dental implants Doncaster", "Invisalign Rotherham"
  • Urgent need: "emergency dentist open now", "same day dentist"
  • Comparison and cost: "dental implant cost", "Invisalign price near me"
  • Practice searches: "private dentist", "dentist accepting new patients"

Use phrase and exact match for your core terms so you stay in control of which searches trigger your ads. Our guide to dental Google Ads keywords goes deeper on which searches are worth bidding on.

Negative keywords protect your budget

Negative keywords stop your ads showing for searches that will never become patients. Typical dental negatives include jobs, courses, training, salary, NHS-only, free and DIY. Review the search terms report every week in the first month and add new negatives as they appear. Our list of negative keywords for dental Google Ads is a good starting point.

Targeting local patients

Patients rarely travel far for a dentist, so location targeting matters more for dental advertising than for most sectors. Set your campaigns to target people in your catchment, using a radius or a list of postcodes and towns, and choose "presence" targeting so you only reach people who are actually in or regularly in that area. Location assets also add your address to your ads and can show your practice on Google Maps when people search for dental services nearby. Our guide to geo-targeting dental ads explains the settings.

Ad scheduling matters too. If nobody answers the phone after 6pm, call ads running at 9pm waste money. Scheduling your ads around opening hours keeps spend focused on times when enquiries can be handled.

How much do Google Ads cost for dentists?

There are two costs to plan for. Ad spend is paid to Google for the clicks themselves. Management fees are paid to whoever builds and runs the campaigns. In the UK, dental keywords are competitive, and the cost per click for implant and orthodontic searches is usually well above that of general dentistry terms.

As a guide, most practices in moderately competitive towns need around £800 to £1,500 a month in ad spend to compete properly for general dentistry and one or two treatments. Practices focused on implants or orthodontics often spend £2,000 or more. Budgets below about £500 a month rarely buy enough clicks for a campaign to learn and improve. Our full breakdown of what Google Ads cost for a UK dental practice covers the detail, and there are practical ways to lower your cost per click once a campaign is running.

The better question is what a new patient is worth. If an Invisalign patient brings in £3,000 and the campaign costs £200 per new patient, the maths works. Thinking in terms of lifetime patient value stops a practice from cutting a profitable campaign because the clicks look expensive.

Writing dental ad copy that gets clicks

Effective dental ads are specific. Generic ad messages such as "quality dental care for all the family" give patients no reason to choose you over the practice above. Strong ad copy for dentists usually includes:

  • The treatment named in the search, ideally in the headline
  • A concrete reason to choose you, such as evening appointments, finance options or a free consultation
  • Your location, so local patients know you are close
  • A clear call to action, such as "Book an appointment online today"

Ad assets (previously called ad extensions) make your ads bigger and more useful, with call buttons, your address, extra links and price details. Our guide to the ad assets every dental practice should switch on lists the ones that matter.

Landing pages that turn clicks into bookings

Sending every click to your homepage is one of the most expensive mistakes in dental advertising. Someone searching for Invisalign wants to land on a page about Invisalign, with prices or finance information, before-and-after examples that meet the rules, reviews and a simple way to book. A focused landing page improves your conversion rate, which lowers your cost per new patient without raising your budget. Our guide to dental landing pages that convert covers what to include.

Tracking conversions and measuring return on investment

Without tracking, you can't tell which keywords bring patients and which only bring clicks. At a minimum, track:

  • Phone calls from your ads and from your website, using call tracking
  • Contact and booking form submissions
  • Online bookings, if you use a booking system

Set these up as conversions in Google Ads and Google Analytics 4. Our step-by-step guide to conversion tracking for dental Google Ads walks through it. The metrics that matter most are cost per enquiry, enquiry-to-patient rate, cost per new patient and return on investment. Click-through rate and Quality Score are useful signals, but they are means to an end.

Not everyone books on the first visit. Remarketing lets you show ads to people who visited your treatment pages without enquiring, which is especially useful for implants and orthodontics where patients take time to decide.

Advertising rules every dental practice must follow

Dental advertising is regulated. The ASA applies the CAP Code to paid search ads and landing pages, and the GDC expects registrants to advertise honestly. In practice that means no unsubstantiated claims such as "best dentist in Sheffield", no misleading prices, no guaranteed results and care with testimonials and before-and-after images. Anti-wrinkle injections can't be advertised by name because botulinum toxin is a prescription-only medicine. Google's own healthcare policies also apply to some treatments. Our guide to GDC and ASA compliant dental advertising sets out what you can and can't say.

Common Google Ads mistakes dentists make

  • Broad match without negatives. Budget leaks to job seekers and people looking for free treatment.
  • One campaign for everything. General dentistry eats the budget meant for high-value treatments.
  • Homepage as the landing page. Patients can't find what they searched for, so they leave.
  • No conversion tracking. Decisions get made on clicks instead of patients.
  • Set and forget. Search terms and competitors change every week, and an unmanaged account slowly gets more expensive.
  • Turning off too early. A campaign needs a few weeks of data to optimise, so judging it after seven days usually gives the wrong answer.

Should you manage Google Ads yourself or use a specialist?

A practice owner can run a simple search campaign, and Google's tools make it easy to launch. The difficulty is in the ongoing work: reviewing search terms, adjusting bids, testing ads and landing pages, keeping every claim compliant and tying enquiries back to booked treatments. That takes a few hours every week and some experience of how dental patients search. It also works best as part of a wider digital marketing plan, where your ads and your SEO support each other.

A specialist dental PPC agency already knows which keywords convert and what the ASA will challenge. Google Ads for dentists: frequently asked questions

Do Google Ads work for dentists?

Yes, when the campaign is built around high-value treatments and measured properly. Dental searches have strong buying intent, so well-managed Google Ads campaigns regularly produce new patient enquiries within days.

How long does it take to see results from dental Google Ads?

Ads can show within hours of launch and enquiries often start in the first week. Expect four to eight weeks of optimisation before the cost per enquiry settles.

Which treatments are best to advertise on Google?

High-value treatments such as dental implants and Invisalign usually give the best return, because a single new patient covers the cost of many clicks. Emergency dentistry also performs well because searchers want an appointment today.

Should dentists use Google Ads or social media ads?

Google Ads captures people already searching, while Meta ads on Facebook and Instagram create demand from people who weren't looking yet. Most practices should start with Google Ads. Our comparison of Meta Ads vs Google Ads for dentists explains when each one wins.
