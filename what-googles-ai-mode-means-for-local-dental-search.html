What Google's AI Mode means for local dental search

By Cristian Savulescu, Founder · Published 17 November 2026

Google's AI Mode is reshaping how patients discover dental practices online, and the old rules of local SEO no longer tell the whole story. This article explains what's changing and what practice owners can do about it.

If you have been keeping an eye on how your practice appears in Google search results lately, you may have noticed something unfamiliar at the top of the page. Before the map pack, before the standard blue links, there is now sometimes a generated summary pulling information together from multiple sources. This is Google's AI Mode in action, and for dental practices competing for local visibility, it represents a genuine shift in how patients find and choose a provider. The question is not whether this will affect your practice. For many, it already is. The question is what you can do about it.

What Google's AI Mode actually is

Google has been rolling out AI-generated features across its search results for some time now. AI Overviews, formerly known as Search Generative Experience, were the first widely visible sign of this. AI Mode goes a step further. Rather than simply appending a generated summary above organic results, AI Mode is a dedicated search experience where Google's large language model handles the entire query, synthesising information from across the web into a conversational, structured response.

For informational queries, this has been visible for a while. For local queries, including searches like "dentist near me accepting new patients" or "best Invisalign provider in Sheffield," the implications are more significant. Google is beginning to serve AI-generated responses that may recommend specific practices, summarise reviews, highlight services, or answer questions about treatment costs, all without the user ever visiting your website.

If you want a broader view of how AI tools are reshaping patient search behaviour more generally, the article Will AI search replace Google for finding a dentist? covers the wider landscape in useful detail.

Why local dental search is particularly affected

Local search has always been competitive for dental practices. You are not just competing with other practices in your town. You are competing for a small number of high-intent searches from people who are ready to book. Google's AI Mode introduces a new layer of complexity to this because it does not simply rank websites. It draws on a wide range of signals to decide what information to present and which practices to surface.

The challenge is that AI-generated results can compress what used to be a diverse first page into a single synthesised answer. A patient searching for a dental implant provider in their area might previously have scrolled through several listings and visited a few websites before making a decision. Now, they may receive a generated response that names two or three practices and answers their core questions in a few sentences. If your practice is not among those surfaced, the patient may never reach you at all.

This is closely related to the zero-click problem that has been building for some time. Zero-click search and what it means for dental enquiries explains how patients increasingly get answers without clicking through, and why that creates a specific challenge for practices relying on organic traffic alone.

How AI systems find and evaluate local dental information

It is worth being honest about what we do and do not know here. Google has not published a precise list of signals that determine which practices appear in AI-generated local results. What we can say, based on how large language models and retrieval systems generally work, is that they draw on structured, authoritative, consistent information to construct their responses.

In practical terms, this means that practices with well-maintained Google Business Profiles, consistent NAP data (name, address, phone number) across directories, strong and recent review profiles, and clearly structured website content are better placed to have their information used accurately in AI responses. It is not a guarantee, and no honest marketer will tell you otherwise. But it does mean there are concrete steps worth taking.

AI assistants including Google Gemini, ChatGPT, Microsoft Copilot and Perplexity all work in broadly similar ways when handling local queries. They look for entities they can verify, information that appears consistently across multiple credible sources, and content that directly addresses the questions people are asking. For dental practices, this translates into a set of practical priorities.

What practice owners can do now

Treat your Google Business Profile as a core asset

Your Google Business Profile is arguably the single most important local signal in an AI-driven search environment. Ensure it is fully completed, with accurate opening hours, a correct address, an up-to-date phone number, your full list of services, and high-quality photos. Add posts regularly. Respond to every review, positive or negative. The completeness and activity level of your profile influences how confidently AI systems can characterise your practice.

Build consistent citations across relevant directories

AI models cross-reference information. If your practice name, address and phone number appear differently across NHS Choices, Yell, Dental Phobia, and other directories, that inconsistency creates uncertainty in automated systems. Audit your citations and correct any discrepancies. This is not glamorous work, but it matters more now than it did two years ago.

Create content that answers specific local questions

Generic "about us" pages and vague service descriptions are becoming less useful in an AI search environment. Practices that perform well tend to have content that directly answers the questions patients are actually asking. What does a dental implant consultation involve? How long does Invisalign take for mild crowding? Are you currently accepting NHS patients in Rotherham? These are the kinds of queries AI systems are being asked, and practices with clear, accurate answers on their websites are better positioned to have that content drawn upon.

Structuring this content with clear headings, concise paragraphs and specific local references can help AI systems identify it as relevant to location-based queries. This sits at the heart of what good generative engine optimisation looks like in practice.

Focus on genuine review volume and recency

Reviews remain one of the clearest trust signals available to both human patients and AI systems. A practice with a steady stream of detailed, recent reviews signals to Google and other AI tools that it is active, trusted and worth recommending. Encourage satisfied patients to leave reviews on Google, and make it easy for them to do so. Avoid anything that could be construed as incentivising or manipulating reviews, which would breach GDC guidance and ASA rules.

Do not abandon paid search

As AI Mode reshapes organic visibility, paid search becomes a more reliable way to guarantee your practice appears for high-intent local queries. AI-generated responses do not replace paid ads, which continue to appear prominently for commercial searches. If you have been running Google Ads for your practice or are considering it, this is worth factoring into your planning. For a worked example of how local PPC fits alongside organic and AI strategies, Dental Google Ads in Huddersfield: a local PPC strategy is a useful reference point.

The broader context: AI Overviews and what came before

AI Mode builds on the foundation that AI Overviews established. If you have not yet looked at how AI Overviews were already affecting your visibility before AI Mode arrived, it is worth doing so. AI Overviews explained: what they mean for dental practices covers the earlier iteration of this shift and the principles it introduced, many of which carry directly into understanding AI Mode.

The important pattern across all of these changes is consistent: Google is moving from a system that lists sources to a system that synthesises them. For practices, this means the question is no longer simply "how do I rank on page one?" It is "how do I become the kind of authoritative, consistent, well-evidenced source that AI systems trust and reference?"
