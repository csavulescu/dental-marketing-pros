A 30-day social media content calendar for dental practices

Most dental practices know they should be posting on social media, but without a clear plan, content either dries up or becomes repetitive. This article gives you a practical 30-day dental social media content calendar and explains how to make it work for the long term.

Most dental practices have a social media account. Many of them have not posted in three weeks. It is one of the most common patterns we see when speaking to practice owners across South Yorkshire and North Derbyshire: there is a genuine intention to stay active online, but without a structured plan, social media becomes the task that gets pushed to the bottom of the pile every single day. The result is an inconsistent presence that neither builds trust with prospective patients nor supports the rest of your marketing. A proper dental social media content calendar fixes this at the root, rather than patching the symptom with the occasional rushed post.

Why dental practices struggle to post consistently

It is worth being honest about why the problem exists, because the solution only works if it addresses the real cause. For most practices, inconsistent posting comes down to three things.

  • No plan in place. When there is nothing decided in advance, every post requires a fresh creative decision. That is genuinely exhausting on top of a clinical day, and the path of least resistance is to skip it.
  • Uncertainty about what is appropriate. The GDC and ASA place clear obligations on dental advertising. Many practice owners are understandably cautious about what they can and cannot say, so they say nothing rather than risk getting it wrong.
  • Content that runs out quickly. Practices often start well with a burst of before-and-after photos or team introductions, then run dry because they have not mapped out a varied content mix across a longer timeframe.

A structured calendar solves all three. It removes the daily decision-making, gives you a framework within which compliance is easier to maintain, and ensures variety is baked in from the start.

What a 30-day dental social media content calendar looks like in practice

The calendar below is designed for practices posting five days a week across platforms such as Facebook and Instagram. You do not need to be on every platform at once. Consistency on two platforms beats sporadic activity across five. Adapt the schedule to fit your team's capacity, but try not to drop below three posts per week or you lose the compounding benefit of regular visibility.

Week one: Build familiarity and trust

The opening week should focus on who you are, not what you sell. Prospective patients decide whether to trust a practice long before they book. Give them reasons to feel comfortable.

  1. Day 1: Team introduction post. A warm, professional photo of your front-of-house team with a short caption about the welcome patients can expect. Keep it genuine and avoid hyperbole.
  2. Day 2: Practice tour or short video. A thirty-second walkthrough of your waiting room and surgery. Familiarity reduces new patient anxiety and increases conversion from enquiry to appointment.
  3. Day 3: Meet the dentist. A short profile of the principal dentist or a lead clinician, including their area of interest and what they enjoy about patient care. No invented credentials; stick to facts.
  4. Day 4: Patient education post. A plain-English explanation of a common question, such as how often patients should have a check-up, or why gum health matters. No clinical claims; frame it as general information.
  5. Day 5: Behind the scenes. A photo of your sterilisation room, your appointment book, or a clinical team member preparing for a procedure. This builds confidence in your standards without any promotional language.

Week two: Showcase your services

By week two, your audience has a sense of who you are. Now you can talk about what you offer, but do so in a way that is helpful rather than promotional. Focus on what a treatment does for a patient, not just that you provide it.

  1. Day 6: Teeth whitening explainer. What the process involves, how long it takes, and who it tends to suit. No before-and-after images unless they comply fully with ASA guidance on misleading comparisons.
  2. Day 7: Invisalign or clear aligner awareness post. A general overview of how aligner treatment works, not a price or outcome promise.
  3. Day 8: Children's dentistry. Tips for parents on preparing young children for their first visit. Useful, shareable, and positions you as a family-friendly practice.
  4. Day 9: NHS versus private dental care. A factual, balanced post explaining the difference for patients who may be confused. This is genuinely useful content that generates engagement.
  5. Day 10: Dental implant awareness. An educational post about implants as a tooth replacement option, framed as information rather than a sales pitch.

Week three: Community and credibility

Social media works best when it connects your practice to the local community. Week three shifts the focus outward.

  1. Day 11: Local landmark or event post. A photo connected to something happening in your area, with a brief, friendly caption. This signals that you are part of the community, not just a service provider.
  2. Day 12: Oral health myth-busting. Pick a common misconception, such as the idea that whitening damages enamel, and address it factually. Keep the tone informative, not condescending.
  3. Day 13: CPD or training update. If a team member has recently completed a course or gained additional training, share it. This demonstrates ongoing professional development without making unsubstantiated claims.
  4. Day 14: National awareness day content. Check whether any relevant health awareness days fall in your posting month. National Smile Month, for example, provides ready-made content hooks. Always credit the source organisation.
  5. Day 15: Patient FAQ. Answer a question your reception team hears regularly. This is practical, demonstrates responsiveness, and often performs well in reach because it addresses real concerns.

Week four: Conversion and re-engagement

By week four, some of your audience will have been following you for three weeks. They are warmer. This is the right time to include gentle calls to action and content that nudges people towards booking.

  1. Day 16: New patient information post. What happens at a first appointment, what to bring, and what to expect. Removes friction from the booking decision.
  2. Day 17: Finance options post. If your practice offers a dental plan or payment options, explain them factually. Avoid any language that could be interpreted as a financial promotion under FCA guidelines.
  3. Day 18: Emergency dental care. What to do if a patient has a dental emergency. Helpful, shareable, and positions your practice as a reliable resource.
  4. Day 19: Seasonal oral health tip. Tie your content to the time of year. In summer, this might be advice about sports mouthguards. In December, it could be the effects of sugar-heavy festive foods on enamel.
  5. Day 20: Soft call to action. Invite followers to book a check-up or get in touch with any questions. Keep the language relaxed and helpful, not pushy.

Days 21 to 30: Repeat the cycle with fresh angles

You do not need thirty completely different ideas. The second half of the month is an opportunity to revisit the same content pillars with fresh examples, different team members, or updated seasonal hooks. A team introduction in week one can become a team milestone celebration in week four. An education post about gum disease in week one can become a myth-busting post about bleeding gums in week three. Variety comes from angles, not just topics.

Compliance points every dental practice must keep in mind

The GDC's guidance on advertising and the ASA's CAP Code both apply to social media content. This is not an area where it is safe to wing it. A few firm rules to build into your calendar:

  • Do not use testimonials that refer to clinical outcomes. Patient testimonials about service or experience may be permitted in certain contexts, but any that reference treatment results must be handled very carefully and are often best avoided entirely.
  • Before-and-after images carry significant compliance risk. If you use them, ensure they are an accurate, fair representation of a typical result and that the accompanying copy makes no misleading claim.
  • Do not imply that a treatment is suitable for everyone, or that results are guaranteed. Use qualifying language consistently.
  • Promotional content on social media counts as advertising. The same rules that apply to your website apply to your posts.

Why a specialist agency produces better results than a generic social media manager

There is a growing market of general social media freelancers and agencies who will happily manage your dental practice accounts. The difficulty is that dental marketing sits at the intersection of healthcare regulation, local SEO, and patient psychology. A generalist can handle the scheduling. They are far less likely to understand why a post about implant results might need to be reworded, or how to write patient education content that supports your wider search visibility without tipping into keyword stuffing.

A specialist agency brings dental-specific content frameworks, an understanding of the regulatory environment, and experience of what actually generates enquiries for practices in your region, not just engagement metrics that look good in a monthly report. There is a meaningful difference between a calendar that keeps your accounts active and one that actively supports patient acquisition.

The 30-day framework above will get you started and is far better than no plan at all.
