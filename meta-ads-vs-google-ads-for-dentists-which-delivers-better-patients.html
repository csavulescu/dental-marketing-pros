Meta ads vs Google Ads for dentists: which delivers better patients?

Choosing between Meta ads and Google Ads is one of the most common dilemmas for dental practice owners investing in paid marketing. This article breaks down how each platform works, where each one falls short, and how to build a strategy that brings in the right patients.

Ask ten dental practice owners how they spend their paid advertising budget and you will likely get ten different answers. Some swear by Google Ads. Others have shifted everything to Meta. Many are running both, unsure whether either is truly working. The question of meta ads vs Google Ads for dentists is not merely academic: the platform you prioritise shapes which patients find you, how much you pay to acquire them, and whether your diary fills with the treatments you actually want to deliver. This article sets out a clear, honest comparison so you can make a better-informed decision for your practice.

The Core Difference: Intent vs Discovery

Before comparing costs or click-through rates, it helps to understand what fundamentally separates the two platforms.

Google Ads captures demand that already exists. When someone types "emergency dentist Sheffield" or "Invisalign cost Rotherham" into Google, they are already in the market. They have a problem or a desire and they are actively looking for a solution. Your ad appears at the moment of highest intent. That is enormously valuable.

Meta ads, which includes Facebook and Instagram, work on an entirely different principle. Your potential patient is not searching for anything. They are scrolling through photographs of their nephew's birthday party or watching a reel about interior design. Your ad interrupts that experience. Done well, it plants a seed. Done poorly, it is ignored completely. Meta is a demand generation platform, not a demand capture one.

Neither approach is superior in isolation. They solve different problems, and the right answer for your practice almost always involves both, deployed strategically rather than in competition with one another.

Where Google Ads Excels for Dental Practices

High-intent searches convert quickly

Patients searching for urgent or specific dental treatments are often ready to book within hours of performing a search. Emergency appointments, tooth extractions, and even same-day consultations are well-suited to Google search campaigns. Because the user has declared their intent through a search query, the path from ad to enquiry is short.

Treatment-specific campaigns work well

If you want to fill your implant list or attract more patients for composite bonding, a well-structured Google Ads campaign lets you bid on keywords that match exactly those treatments. You can control which searches trigger your ads, set bids by location, and limit spend to the hours your phone lines are staffed, all of which improves efficiency.

The challenges you need to understand

Google Ads in the dental sector can be expensive, particularly in competitive urban areas. Bidding against large corporate practices and NHS providers on popular keywords can push cost-per-click to a level that makes the economics challenging unless your average treatment value is high enough to justify it. Poorly managed campaigns, with broad match keywords, weak ad copy, or no negative keyword lists, can burn through budget very quickly with little to show for it.

Landing page quality matters enormously too. Google rewards relevance. If your ad promises Invisalign consultations but sends traffic to your homepage, your Quality Score suffers, your cost-per-click rises, and your conversion rate drops. A specialist dental PPC service will ensure your campaigns are built around tightly themed ad groups, strong landing pages, and ongoing optimisation rather than a set-and-forget approach.

Where Meta Ads Excels for Dental Practices

Reaching patients who do not know they want you yet

The majority of people in your catchment area who would benefit from your services are not, at this moment, searching for a dentist. They are not in market yet. Meta lets you reach them anyway, putting your practice in front of a carefully defined audience based on location, age, interests, and behaviour. You are building awareness and desire before the search even happens.

Visual treatments are made for social

Cosmetic and aesthetic treatments, such as teeth whitening, veneers, smile makeovers, and clear aligners, perform particularly well on Meta because they lend themselves to visual storytelling. A well-produced before-and-after image (presented in line with GDC and ASA guidance on patient consent and responsible promotion) can stop a scroll and prompt genuine interest from someone who had not previously considered treatment.

Retargeting is one of Meta's strongest tools

If someone visits your website, watches a video, or engages with your Facebook page, Meta allows you to serve them a specific follow-up ad. This retargeting capability means you can nudge warm prospects who have already shown interest, which typically converts at a much higher rate than cold audiences and makes your overall ad spend more efficient.

The challenges you need to understand

Meta's targeting has changed significantly over recent years, with reduced granularity following privacy updates. Audience sizes and the data available to the platform are no longer what they were, which means campaign structure and creative quality matter more than ever. Generic stock-photo ads with vague copy no longer perform. Campaigns that speak directly to a specific concern, in a specific community, with imagery that feels authentic to your practice, consistently outperform bland promotional content.

Attribution is also trickier on Meta. A patient who sees your Instagram ad on Tuesday, Googles your practice on Thursday, and books on Friday may be recorded as a direct or organic conversion in your analytics. This can make Meta's contribution look smaller than it actually is, leading some practice owners to undervalue it prematurely.

The Budget Question: Where Should Your Money Go First?

There is no single correct answer, but there are sensible starting points based on what most practices need.

If your practice has empty appointment slots and you need bookings quickly, Google Ads is usually the faster route to results. People searching for a dentist in your area can become patients within days.

If you are building a cosmetic or private patient base for the longer term, or if you want to establish your practice's reputation in a specific community before they are even in market, Meta is a powerful complement to your Google activity.

If budget is genuinely limited, start with Google for conversion and add Meta retargeting once you have meaningful website traffic to work with. Retargeting a small, warm audience is far more cost-effective than running broad cold campaigns on Meta with a modest budget.

Why Running Both Without a Strategy Wastes Money

Many practices run Google and Meta campaigns simultaneously but treat them as entirely separate activities managed by different people or handled in-house without clear objectives. The result is usually fragmented messaging, duplicated effort, and no coherent picture of which activity is driving which outcomes.

A joined-up approach treats the two platforms as parts of the same patient acquisition system. Google captures the patient who is already searching. Meta builds awareness and retargets warm prospects. Your website and booking process converts both. Your tracking setup attributes enquiries correctly. Each element reinforces the others rather than competing for credit.

This kind of integration does not happen by accident. It requires a clear strategy, consistent messaging across platforms, properly configured conversion tracking, and someone with the time and expertise to review performance and adjust regularly. Most practice owners are too occupied with clinical work and running their teams to manage this effectively on their own, which is why specialist support makes a measurable difference. How a Specialist Agency Approaches This Differently

A dental marketing agency that understands both platforms, and understands the specific regulatory environment in which you operate, approaches paid advertising differently from a general digital agency.

  • Keyword and audience research specific to dentistry: Understanding which search terms signal genuine patient intent, and which attract tyre-kickers, takes experience in the sector.
  • Ad creative that complies with GDC and ASA guidelines: Claims about results, testimonials, and before-and-after imagery all carry specific obligations in dental advertising. Getting this wrong can result in complaints or regulatory action.
  • Landing pages built around conversion: Driving traffic to a poorly designed landing page is expensive and ineffective. Specialist agencies ensure the page that receives your paid traffic is designed to convert visitors into enquiries.
  • Proper conversion tracking: Without accurate tracking, you are guessing. Phone call tracking, form submission recording, and multi-touch attribution give you a real picture of what is working.
  • Ongoing optimisation rather than set-and-forget: Paid campaigns require regular attention. Bid adjustments, negative keyword additions, audience exclusions, ad copy testing, and budget reallocation between campaigns all contribute to improving performance over time.

Making the Right Choice for Your Practice

The meta ads vs Google Ads debate for dentists is ultimately a false choice. The most effective practices use both, but they use them purposefully, with clear objectives, aligned messaging, and proper measurement in place.

If you are currently running either platform without a clear sense of what each campaign is costing you per new patient, or without confidence that your budget is being spent efficiently, it is worth reviewing your approach before committing more spend. Small changes to campaign structure, landing page design, or audience targeting can have a significant impact on results without necessarily requiring a larger budget.
