The GDC Standards for the Dental Team and your marketing

By Cristian Savulescu, Founder · Published 19 October 2026

The GDC Standards for the Dental Team apply to your marketing just as much as your clinical work. This guide explains the rules practice owners most commonly miss and how to market your practice compliantly and effectively.

Most practice owners understand that the GDC Standards for the Dental Team govern clinical care, patient communication, and professional conduct. Fewer realise those same standards reach directly into their marketing. Your website, your social media posts, your Google ads, and the way your team talks about treatments online are all subject to GDC scrutiny. Get it wrong, and you are not just risking a complaint — you are risking your registration. The good news is that compliant marketing is not weak marketing. Done properly, it builds the kind of trust that converts browsers into booked patients, and it does so without cutting corners that could cost you later.

Why GDC Standards and Marketing Are Inseparable

Standard 4 of the GDC Standards for the Dental Team is the one most directly relevant to marketing. It requires dental professionals to maintain and protect patients' information, but the broader principle running through all nine standards is that your conduct must put patients' interests first and that you must be honest and trustworthy in everything you do. The GDC makes clear that "everything you do" includes how you present your practice to the public.

This means your marketing must not mislead. It must not exaggerate outcomes. It must not exploit patient anxieties. And it must not make claims about your services that you cannot substantiate. The Advertising Standards Authority (ASA) enforces similar rules under the CAP Code, and the GDC will take a dim view of any registrant whose advertising attracts upheld ASA complaints.

For a fuller breakdown of what you can and cannot say in your ads, the article on GDC and ASA compliant dental advertising: what you can and can't say covers the specific language rules in depth. It is worth reading alongside this one.

The Most Common Compliance Failures in Dental Marketing

Across practices in South Yorkshire and North Derbyshire, we see the same problems recurring. None of them happen because practice owners are careless. They happen because most marketing agencies do not understand dental regulation, and most dentists do not have the time to audit every piece of content their agency produces.

Outcome guarantees and superlative claims

Language such as "guaranteed results," "the best dental practice in Sheffield," or "the most advanced implant centre in South Yorkshire" falls foul of both the GDC standards and the ASA CAP Code. Superlatives require substantiation. Outcome guarantees mislead patients about the nature of clinical treatments, which are inherently variable. Even softer language — "you will leave with a perfect smile" — can be problematic if it implies a certainty that clinical reality cannot always deliver.

Before-and-after imagery used incorrectly

Before-and-after photographs are one of the most persuasive tools in cosmetic dental marketing, and one of the most heavily regulated. The GDC expects that any such images are representative, that patient consent has been properly obtained and documented, and that the images are not used in a way that creates unrealistic expectations. The detailed rules are covered in the article on patient testimonials and before/after photos: the GDC rules explained, which is essential reading if you are using visual evidence in your marketing.

Unverified testimonials and review management

Patient testimonials must reflect genuine, unprompted opinion. You cannot incentivise positive reviews, cherry-pick only five-star feedback for your website, or fabricate testimonials. If you are actively managing your Google Business Profile or featuring patient quotes on your site, you need a clear, documented process that keeps this activity within the rules.

Team members marketing on your behalf without guidance

Your reception staff sharing a practice post on Instagram, your dental nurse filming a "day in the life" video, or your associate answering treatment questions in a Facebook comment — these are all forms of marketing that carry the same compliance obligations as a paid advertisement. Without proper guidance, your team can inadvertently publish something misleading, make clinical claims without the appropriate qualifications, or share patient information. The article on employee advocacy: turning your dental team into marketers explains how to harness your team's reach safely and effectively.

Why This Keeps Happening

The root cause is almost always a gap between who creates the marketing and who understands the regulation. A general digital marketing agency may be skilled at copywriting and paid ads, but they are unlikely to know the difference between what the GDC permits and what it prohibits. They write what converts. They do not write with Standards 4, 6, and 9 in mind.

At the same time, practice owners are busy running clinical services, managing teams, and handling CQC obligations. Reviewing every blog post, every ad, and every social caption for GDC compliance is not realistic on top of everything else. So compliance checks either do not happen, or they happen inconsistently.

The result is marketing that ranges from slightly risky to genuinely problematic, published and indexed on Google, sometimes for years before anyone notices.

The Fix: Building Compliance Into Your Marketing Process

Compliance should not be a retrospective check. It should be built into every stage of how your marketing is created, reviewed, and published. Here is what that looks like in practice.

Create a written marketing policy

Document what your practice will and will not say in its marketing. Include approved language for key treatments, a clear process for using patient images and testimonials, and guidance for team members who post on the practice's behalf or their own channels. This does not need to be a lengthy document, but it does need to exist and be followed consistently.

Brief your marketing agency properly

If you work with an external agency, they need to understand GDC Standards for the dental team, not just your target keywords and monthly budget. Ask them directly: do they have experience working with regulated healthcare businesses? Can they demonstrate knowledge of CAP Code rules for dental advertising? Do they know what claims require substantiation under ASA guidance? If the answers are vague, that is a risk you are carrying.

Audit your existing content

Pull up your website, your Google Business Profile, your social media pages, and any paid ads currently running. Look specifically for outcome guarantees, unsupported superlatives, before-and-after images without proper consent documentation, and testimonials that may have been incentivised. If your dental SEO content includes blog posts making clinical claims without appropriate caveats, those need reviewing too. Search engines index everything, and a GDC investigation will not discriminate between an old blog post and a live ad.

Establish a review workflow

Every piece of content going out under your practice name should pass through a review step that includes a compliance check. This can be the practice manager, the principal dentist, or your marketing agency if they are genuinely qualified to do it. The key is that it happens before publication, not after a complaint has been raised.

How a Specialist Dental Marketing Agency Does This Better

A general agency will typically optimise for clicks, conversions, and cost per lead. Those things matter, but they are not the whole picture for a regulated professional business. A dental-specialist agency builds compliance into the brief itself.

That means treatment page copy is written with GDC language guidance in mind from the first draft. It means paid ads are structured to attract patients with realistic expectations rather than ones whose first question after treatment is "but you said it would definitely work." It means your social media strategy includes clear guidance for your team so that employee-generated content does not create liability. And it means your SEO strategy does not rely on exaggerated claims to rank, because rankings built on misleading content are fragile in any case.

It also means someone is watching for changes. The GDC updates its guidance. The ASA publishes new rulings. What was acceptable language two years ago may not be acceptable today. A team that works exclusively with dental practices will notice those changes and update your content accordingly, rather than leaving outdated copy indexed on your site.

GDC Standards Marketing Is Not a Constraint — It Is a Differentiator

There is a temptation to see compliance as a limitation on what you can say. In practice, it is the opposite. The dental practices that market within GDC standards tend to produce clearer, more honest, more patient-focused content. That content builds genuine trust. It attracts patients who are well-informed, appropriately motivated, and less likely to complain. It also attracts search engines, because content that clearly and accurately answers patient questions is exactly what Google's quality guidelines reward.

Compliant marketing is not bland marketing. It is marketing that respects your patients and protects your registration — while still being persuasive, visible, and effective.
