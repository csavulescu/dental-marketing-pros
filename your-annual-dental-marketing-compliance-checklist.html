Your annual dental marketing compliance checklist

By Cristian Savulescu, Founder · Published 28 September 2026

Marketing a dental practice in the UK means navigating GDC standards, ASA advertising rules, and data protection law every single year. This practical compliance checklist helps practice owners spot the gaps before a regulator does.

Running a dental practice in the UK means operating inside one of the most tightly regulated advertising environments in healthcare. The General Dental Council (GDC), the Advertising Standards Authority (ASA), the Committee of Advertising Practice (CAP), and the UK GDPR all have a say in how you promote your services. Most practice owners know this in principle. The problem is that day-to-day marketing decisions — a new Google Ad, a social media post, a freshly redesigned website page — get made quickly, without anyone stopping to check whether they meet the current rules. Compliance gaps accumulate quietly, and they tend to surface at the worst possible moment: a patient complaint, an ASA investigation, or a GDC fitness-to-practise referral. This annual dental marketing compliance checklist is designed to help you find those gaps before anyone else does.

Why compliance problems build up even in well-run practices

The honest answer is that compliance is rarely the bottleneck people plan for. Websites get updated in pieces rather than all at once. A treatment page written three years ago may still be live, carrying claims that no longer reflect your services or that breach current CAP guidance. Social media content is produced under time pressure and often by team members who have not read the GDC's guidance on patient testimonials. Paid advertising campaigns are set up, optimised for conversions, and then left running long after the original copy was reviewed by anyone with compliance in mind.

This is not negligence. It is the natural result of running a busy practice where marketing is one of many priorities. But the regulatory framework does not make allowances for busy schedules, which is why a structured, annual review matters.

The dental marketing compliance checklist: what to review every year

1. Website content and claims

Your website is the most scrutinised piece of your marketing. Start here. Work through every treatment page and ask three questions: Is every claim accurate and substantiated? Does any language imply a guaranteed outcome? Does any copy describe a treatment in a way that could mislead a patient about risk, recovery, or results?

  • Before-and-after images: CAP rules require that before-and-after images must not mislead. Images must be genuine, must not be enhanced, and must not imply results that are not typical. Include a clear indication that results vary.
  • Pricing and special offers: If you advertise a price, it must be the full price available to a typical patient. Hidden fees or asterisked conditions that are not immediately clear can breach ASA rules.
  • Superlatives and comparisons: Phrases such as "the best dental practice in Sheffield" or "the most advanced implant centre in South Yorkshire" require substantiation. If you cannot prove it, remove it.
  • GDC-registerable titles: Only use protected titles for team members who hold the appropriate GDC registration. Referring to a therapist as a "specialist" when they do not hold a specialist listing is a GDC matter, not just an ASA one.

If your website was built by a generic agency without dental sector knowledge, it is worth having the copy reviewed by someone who understands both marketing effectiveness and sector-specific rules. A well-structured, compliant website also performs better in search results. Our dental web design service builds sites with compliance and conversion in mind from the first wireframe.

It is also worth checking your site against accessibility standards at this point. A site that excludes users with disabilities is not only an ethical issue but increasingly a legal one. 2. Patient testimonials and reviews

Patient testimonials are a significant compliance area that catches many practices off guard. The GDC's guidance is clear: you must not use testimonials in a way that exploits patients or that implies results that cannot be guaranteed. The ASA's position is equally firm, requiring that testimonials reflect genuine patient experience and that any exceptional result is not presented as typical.

  • Review all testimonials displayed on your website and remove or update any that describe outcomes in terms of guaranteed results.
  • Do not cherry-pick reviews in a way that creates a misleading overall impression of patient experience.
  • If you embed Google or Trustpilot review widgets, check that the overall picture they present is an accurate reflection of your actual ratings.
  • Never fabricate testimonials or ask team members to post reviews as patients. This is both an ASA breach and a potential GDC fitness matter.

3. Paid advertising (Google Ads and social media ads)

Paid advertising moves fast, and compliance in this channel is often overlooked because the focus is almost entirely on performance metrics. But every advert you run must meet the same CAP and ASA standards as your website, and in some respects the bar is higher because adverts reach people who have not actively sought out your practice.

  • Audit every active ad for claims that cannot be substantiated. "Free consultation" must genuinely be free with no obligation or hidden conditions.
  • Ads promoting tooth whitening must not imply that the treatment will be performed by anyone other than an appropriately qualified clinician.
  • Target audience settings must not be used in a way that could be considered discriminatory or exploitative under relevant legislation.
  • Landing pages linked from ads must be compliant in their own right. An ASA investigation into an ad can extend to the landing page it points to.

Paid dental advertising also has to work commercially, not just compliantly. Our dental PPC service manages both dimensions, building campaigns that generate enquiries without creating regulatory exposure.

4. SEO content and search visibility

Search engine optimisation content, including blog posts, location pages, and treatment guides, is subject to the same rules as any other marketing material. Content written to rank in Google is still advertising if it promotes your practice or its services.

  • Review blog posts for outdated clinical claims. Treatment guidelines change, and a post written two years ago may now contain information that is no longer accurate or that contradicts current NICE or FGDP guidance.
  • Check that any statistics or research findings cited in your content are properly attributed and still current.
  • Remove or update any content that makes outcome promises, even indirect ones such as "most patients are pain-free within a week."

5. Data protection and consent

UK GDPR compliance is not optional, and your marketing activity sits squarely within its scope. This section of the checklist covers the most common gaps.

  • Email marketing: Do you have explicit, documented consent for every patient or contact on your marketing list? Are unsubscribe links working and actioned promptly?
  • Website cookies: Does your cookie consent banner meet current ICO requirements? A banner that loads non-essential cookies before consent is given is a breach, regardless of how the banner is worded.
  • Contact forms: Is your privacy notice linked clearly from every form that collects personal data? Does it accurately describe how you use that data for marketing purposes?
  • Third-party tools: If you use a CRM, a chatbot, or a booking system, check that your data processing agreements with those providers are current and that their privacy standards meet UK GDPR requirements.

6. Social media and organic content

Social media content is advertising when it promotes your practice, regardless of whether you have paid to boost it. Review your recent posts and stories against the same standards you apply to your website.

  • Clinical images, including photographs of teeth, must not show recognisable patients without documented, specific consent for that image to be used in marketing.
  • Posts promoting treatments must not overstate benefits or understate risks.
  • If a team member manages your social channels, make sure they have received guidance on what they can and cannot say. Good intentions do not provide a defence in an ASA investigation.

How a specialist dental marketing agency reduces your compliance risk

The checklist above covers the main areas, but applying it consistently requires time, sector knowledge, and an understanding of how the rules interact with each other. A generic marketing agency may produce effective content without knowing that a particular claim about tooth whitening falls within regulated territory. A non-specialist PPC consultant may write ad copy that converts well but contains a phrase that breaches CAP guidance.

Working with an agency that focuses specifically on dental practices means that compliance is built into the process rather than bolted on as an afterthought. Content is written with GDC guidance and ASA rules in mind from the first draft. Ad copy is reviewed against CAP standards before it goes live. Website pages are structured so that claims are accurate, substantiated, and unlikely to attract a complaint.

This does not mean safe, dull marketing. It means confident, well-evidenced marketing that stands up to scrutiny and builds patient trust over time.

A sensible next step

If you have worked through this checklist and found gaps, or if you are not certain whether your current marketing meets the relevant standards, it is worth having a conversation with someone who understands both the regulatory environment and what effective dental marketing looks like in practice.
