Why your practice isn't showing up in ChatGPT answers

By Cristian Savulescu, Founder · Published 20 October 2026

AI assistants like ChatGPT are increasingly influencing how patients find dental care, yet many practices are invisible to them. This article explains why your dental practice isn't appearing in ChatGPT answers and what you can do about it.

Patients are no longer relying solely on Google to find a dentist. Increasingly, they are typing questions into ChatGPT, Google's AI Overviews, Gemini, Copilot and Perplexity and acting on whatever those tools recommend. If your practice is not among the results those AI assistants surface, you are missing a growing slice of new patient enquiries, and the gap is likely to widen. The problem of a dental practice not in ChatGPT results is becoming one of the more pressing visibility challenges for practice owners in 2024 and beyond, and understanding why it happens is the first step towards doing something about it.

The shift in how patients search for dental care

For years, the dominant question was: "How do I rank on Google?" That question has not gone away, and traditional SEO still matters enormously. But a new question is sitting alongside it: "Why is my practice invisible to AI?"

AI assistants work differently from a standard search engine. Rather than returning a list of links and letting the user decide, they synthesise information from multiple sources and present a confident, conversational answer. When a patient asks "Who is a good NHS dentist near Sheffield?" or "What is the best dental implant practice in Chesterfield?", the AI does not show ten blue links. It names practices, summarises their reputations, and may even explain why it is recommending them.

If your practice is not being named, it is not because AI tools dislike you. It is because they cannot find enough reliable, consistent, authoritative information about you to confidently include you in an answer. Understanding that distinction is important, because it means the fix is achievable.

Why AI assistants overlook certain dental practices

To understand the solution, it helps to understand how these tools decide what to recommend. We have covered this in detail in our article on how ChatGPT decides which dentists to recommend, but the core principle is this: AI assistants are trained on publicly available text and, when generating real-time answers, they draw heavily on content that is well-structured, widely cited, and consistent across the web.

There are several reasons why a specific practice might be absent from AI-generated answers.

1. Thin or poorly structured website content

If your website has a homepage, a brief "Services" page and a contact form, there is very little for an AI to learn from. These tools favour websites that clearly explain what the practice does, where it is located, who the clinical team are, and what conditions or treatments they address. Thin content gives an AI assistant almost nothing to cite with confidence.

2. Inconsistent or missing business information online

AI assistants cross-reference information. Your practice name, address, phone number and website need to be consistent across Google Business Profile, NHS choices listings, dental directories such as the BDA's Find a Dentist tool, and other reputable third-party sources. Where that information is missing, outdated or contradictory, it reduces the AI's confidence in recommending you.

3. No authoritative third-party mentions

When other credible websites mention your practice, AI tools treat this as a signal that you are a legitimate, established business. Local news coverage, links from professional bodies, patient review platforms and healthcare directories all contribute. A practice with almost no third-party web presence is effectively invisible to an AI that is trying to assess credibility.

4. Reviews that are sparse, old or unresponded to

Patient reviews on Google, Trustpilot and similar platforms are publicly available text. AI tools can and do draw on them to understand the character and reputation of a practice. A profile with very few reviews, or reviews that were last posted several years ago, signals stagnation rather than an active, trusted practice.

5. No location-specific content

AI assistants try to match recommendations to the location of the person asking. If your website content rarely or never references the towns, areas or postcodes you serve, it becomes harder for an AI to confidently include you in geographically specific answers. A practice in Rotherham that never mentions Rotherham on its website is fighting with one hand tied behind its back.

The connection to traditional SEO, and why it matters here

It is worth being direct about something: many of the signals that help AI assistants find and trust your practice are the same signals that have always mattered for Google search rankings. If your practice has poor local SEO foundations, you are likely struggling on both fronts simultaneously.

Our article on why your dental practice isn't ranking on Google (and the 6 fixes) covers the foundational issues in detail. Addressing those issues tends to have a positive knock-on effect on AI visibility, because both depend on the same underlying signals: authoritative content, consistent citations, and a credible digital footprint.

That said, AI search is not simply an extension of traditional SEO. There are tactics that are specifically useful for improving your chances of appearing in generative AI answers, and these require deliberate effort.

What you can do to improve your AI search visibility

These tactics cannot guarantee that any AI tool will cite your practice. AI assistants make their own judgements, and nobody outside those organisations has precise knowledge of every ranking factor they use. What the following approaches can do is make your practice a far more credible, findable and citable source of information, which improves your overall chances.

Build genuinely informative content around your treatments and location

Write detailed, clear pages about each treatment you offer. Explain what the treatment involves, who it suits, what patients can expect, and where your practice is based. Reference specific towns, areas and local landmarks where it is natural to do so. This kind of content gives AI tools something concrete to draw on when composing an answer about dental care in your area.

Claim and optimise every relevant directory listing

Go through every major and minor directory where your practice could be listed: Google Business Profile, NHS Find a Dentist, Yelp, Yell, Trustpilot, the BDA's directory, local business directories, and any dental-specific platforms. Make sure the name, address, phone number and website URL are identical across all of them. Even small discrepancies, such as "St" versus "Street" in your address, can reduce citation confidence.

Generate and respond to reviews consistently

Develop a simple process for inviting satisfied patients to leave reviews on Google and other platforms, in a way that complies with GDC guidance on patient testimonials. Respond to every review, positive or otherwise, professionally and promptly. An active, well-reviewed profile signals that a practice is currently operating and trusted by patients.

Earn mentions on third-party websites

Consider whether there are legitimate ways to earn coverage or links from credible external sources. Sponsoring a local event, contributing expert comment to a local news outlet, participating in dental association activities, or being listed on healthcare platforms all help build the kind of external credibility that AI tools use to assess legitimacy.

Structure your website so AI can interpret it easily

Use schema markup to tell AI tools and search engines exactly what your practice is, where it is, what services it offers, and how to contact you. This structured data acts as a clear, machine-readable label on your website content. It is a technical step, but an important one, and it is the kind of thing that a specialist agency can implement properly rather than approximately.

Do not neglect social media as a supporting signal

Your social media presence contributes to the overall picture of your practice online. A consistently maintained, professional social media profile adds another layer of credibility and creates more publicly accessible content about your practice. For practical guidance on what actually matters on social, our article on social media for dentists: what actually drives enquiries (not vanity metrics) is worth reading alongside this one.

Practice owners running busy clinical environments rarely have the time or the specialist knowledge to manage this systematically, and a partial or inconsistent approach often produces partial results.

A practical starting point

If you are concerned that your dental practice is not in ChatGPT answers or other AI search results, the worst response is to wait and hope the situation resolves itself. AI-driven search is growing in influence, and practices that build their digital credibility now are likely to hold a meaningful advantage over those that address it later.

Start by auditing what information about your practice is publicly available online. Is it accurate? Is it consistent? Is it informative enough for an AI to confidently recommend you? If the honest answer to any of those questions is no, then you have a clear place to begin.
