Will AI search replace Google for finding a dentist?

By Cristian Savulescu, Founder · Published 13 October 2026

AI tools like ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews and Perplexity are changing how people search for local services, including dentists. Here is what practice owners need to understand now, and what they can do about it.

A patient sits down at their laptop and types: "Which dental practice near Sheffield is best for nervous patients?" They do not get ten blue links. They get a paragraph, written by an AI, recommending two or three practices by name. Your practice is not one of them. This is not a hypothetical. It is happening right now, and it is quietly reshaping how new patients discover dental care across South Yorkshire and North Derbyshire.

The question is not really whether AI search will replace Google. The more useful question for a practice owner is: what do you need to do so that when an AI assistant answers a dental query in your area, your practice has a genuine chance of being part of that answer?

How AI search tools actually work when someone looks for a dentist

Tools such as ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Gemini, Microsoft Copilot and Perplexity do not crawl the web the way a traditional search engine does at the moment a query is fired. Instead, they draw on a combination of pre-trained knowledge, indexed web content, and in some cases real-time search results to generate a synthesised response. When someone asks for a dentist recommendation, the AI is essentially looking for sources it considers trustworthy, well-structured, and relevant to the location specified.

What does "trustworthy and well-structured" mean in practice? Broadly, it means content that is clear, factually consistent, written for real people, and backed by signals from credible third-party sources. Think directory listings, review platforms, local news mentions, professional body profiles, and a well-maintained website with genuinely useful information. None of that is radically new. What is new is the way these signals are weighted and surfaced.

This discipline has its own name: Generative Engine Optimisation, or GEO. If you want to understand the strategic framework behind it, our GEO service page explains the approach we take for dental practices specifically.

Why many dental practices will be invisible to AI search

Traditional SEO has always rewarded practices that invest in their online presence. AI search raises the stakes because the output is far more compressed. A Google results page might show ten organic listings, a map pack, and several ads. An AI response might mention two practices, or none at all. If your practice is not positioned well, there is no page two to fall back on.

Several common gaps leave dental practices poorly positioned for AI discovery:

  • Thin or generic website content. If your About page says "we offer a wide range of dental treatments in a caring environment," an AI has very little to work with. It cannot determine whether you specialise in nervous patients, Invisalign, or implants. Specificity matters far more now than it used to.
  • Inconsistent NAP data. Name, address, and phone number inconsistencies across your website, Google Business Profile, and directories create uncertainty. AI tools that aggregate information may deprioritise sources that appear contradictory.
  • Weak review signals. Review volume and recency influence how authoritative a practice appears across multiple platforms. A practice with 12 reviews from 2019 is at a structural disadvantage.
  • Poor local authority signals. Links and mentions from local organisations, health bodies, press, and community groups help establish that a practice is genuinely embedded in its area. Without them, an AI has less reason to surface you for a local query.
  • No structured data. Schema markup, particularly LocalBusiness and MedicalOrganization schema, helps machines understand what your practice does, where it is, and who it serves. Many dental websites still lack it entirely.

If you are not sure where your practice stands on these points, a free SEO audit is a sensible starting point before making any decisions about where to invest.

The connection between traditional SEO and AI search

It is worth being direct here: GEO is not a replacement for solid SEO. It is built on top of it. If your website does not rank well organically, and your Google Business Profile is incomplete, and your reviews are sparse, then optimising for AI search in isolation is likely to produce limited results.

AI tools, particularly those with real-time search capability such as Perplexity and the current versions of ChatGPT with browsing enabled, frequently pull from the same sources that traditional SEO targets: well-optimised pages, authoritative directories, and review platforms. The practices that show up in AI responses tend to be the same ones that are doing well in local organic search.

This means the fundamentals still apply. Understanding the terms prospective patients actually use when searching, for instance, is foundational. Similarly, your Google Business Profile remains critically important. Google AI Overviews draw heavily on Google's own ecosystem, and a fully optimised profile gives you the best possible footing within it. For a detailed walkthrough of what that looks like, our guide to Google Business Profile optimisation for dental practices is worth reading alongside this article.

What you can actually do to improve your practice's chances

The following tactics can help improve how AI search tools perceive and represent your practice. They are presented as actions to consider, not as guaranteed outcomes. AI systems do not publish their ranking criteria, and anyone claiming certainty about how to "game" them should be treated with caution.

Write genuinely helpful, specific content

AI tools are built to synthesise useful information. If your website answers real questions that patients have, in plain language, with relevant local context, it is more likely to be drawn on as a source. Think about the questions you are asked every week in practice. Write clear, honest answers to them on your website. A page that explains what to expect during a first consultation, or how dental implant treatment works over multiple appointments, or what options exist for anxious patients, is far more useful to an AI (and to a patient) than a generic treatments list.

Build local authority through third-party mentions

When a local newspaper, a community organisation, or a health body references your practice, it creates a signal that you are a real, established presence in your area. This kind of local authority can be built gradually through community involvement, press releases about genuine news (a new service, a charity initiative, a new practitioner joining), and participation in local professional networks.

Keep your review profile active and genuine

Encourage patients to leave honest reviews on Google, and where relevant, on platforms such as Trustpilot or NHS Choices. Do not incentivise reviews, as this conflicts with GDC guidance on patient testimonials. Simply making it easy for satisfied patients to share their experience, for instance via a follow-up text with a direct review link, can meaningfully improve review volume over time.

Ensure technical foundations are sound

A fast, mobile-friendly website with properly implemented structured data and consistent business information across all directories is the technical baseline. Without it, even excellent content may not be processed and surfaced reliably. If you have not addressed the core reasons a dental website might be underperforming in search, the article on why your dental practice isn't ranking on Google (and the 6 fixes) covers the most common issues in straightforward terms.

Think about how patients phrase requests to AI

Searches made to AI assistants tend to be more conversational than traditional keyword searches. Someone might ask "Which dentists near Rotherham accept nervous patients?" rather than typing "nervous dentist Rotherham." Your content should reflect this. Incorporating natural, conversational language into your website, particularly in FAQ sections or blog content, can help align your practice with the way AI queries are actually phrased.

Should you still invest in Google Ads alongside all of this?

AI search is a long-term shift, not an overnight replacement for paid search. Google Ads continues to deliver highly measurable, immediate visibility for practices that need to generate enquiries now. The two approaches are not mutually exclusive. If you are considering whether paid search makes sense for your practice as part of a broader strategy, our article on whether Google Ads is worth it for dentists sets out a balanced view of where it works well and where it does not.

How a specialist agency approaches GEO for dental practices

Working with a dental marketing agency that understands both the clinical regulatory context and the technical requirements of GEO makes a practical difference. The GDC and ASA place specific restrictions on how dental services can be marketed, including rules around testimonials, before and after imagery, and claims about outcomes. These constraints exist for good reasons, and they shape what can and cannot be done when building a practice's online presence.

A specialist agency will audit your current digital footprint across your website, Google Business Profile, directories, and review platforms. It will identify where the structural gaps are, prioritise the work that is most likely to improve visibility across both traditional and AI-driven search, and produce content that is genuinely useful to patients while remaining compliant with professional guidance. Our GEO service is built around exactly this kind of structured, practice-specific approach.

The shift towards AI search is real and continuing. Practices that build strong, trustworthy, well-structured digital presences now will be better placed as these tools become a more routine part of how patients search for care. Those that wait may find the gap harder to close.
