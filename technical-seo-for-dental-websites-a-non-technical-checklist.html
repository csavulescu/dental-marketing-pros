Technical SEO for dental websites: a non-technical checklist

Technical SEO problems are often invisible to practice owners, yet they quietly suppress your rankings and cost you new patient enquiries. This plain-English checklist helps you understand what to look for and what to do about it.

You have invested time and money in your dental website. The copy is professional, the photography looks great, and your services are clearly listed. Yet you are not appearing on the first page of Google for searches in your area. The most common culprit is not your content. It is the invisible layer underneath it: technical SEO. For most practice owners, this is unfamiliar territory, and understandably so. This checklist explains what technical SEO actually involves, why problems creep in, and what you or your agency should be doing about each one.

What is technical SEO and why does it matter for a dental website?

Technical SEO refers to the behind-the-scenes configuration of your website that allows search engines to find, read, and rank your pages correctly. It is distinct from the words on your pages (on-page SEO) or the links pointing to your site (off-page SEO). Think of it as the foundations of a building. You can decorate the rooms beautifully, but if the foundations are cracked, the whole structure is compromised.

For a dental practice, the stakes are real. If Google cannot crawl your site efficiently, your treatment pages will not rank, regardless of how well they are written. If your site loads slowly on a mobile phone, visitors will leave before they book, and Google will notice that behaviour and rank you lower as a result. A solid dental SEO strategy always begins with getting the technical foundations right.

The checklist: eight technical areas every dental practice owner should know about

1. Mobile usability

The problem: The majority of people searching for a dentist are doing so on a mobile device. If your website is difficult to navigate on a small screen, buttons are too close together, or text requires pinching to read, you will lose potential patients immediately.

Why it happens: Many dental websites were built several years ago on desktop-first templates that were never properly adapted for mobile. Even newer sites can have mobile usability errors introduced during updates.

The fix: Use Google Search Console (it is free) and navigate to the Core Web Vitals report and the Mobile Usability section. Any errors flagged there need correcting, typically by a web developer. If your site needs a more fundamental overhaul, a purpose-built dental website design built responsively from the ground up will resolve most mobile issues at the source.

2. Page speed and Core Web Vitals

The problem: Slow-loading pages frustrate users and are explicitly penalised by Google through its Core Web Vitals scoring system. The three metrics that matter most are Largest Contentful Paint (how quickly the main content appears), Interaction to Next Paint (how quickly the page responds to a click or tap), and Cumulative Layout Shift (whether the page jumps around as it loads).

Why it happens: Oversized images, poorly written code, too many third-party scripts (live chat widgets, booking plugins, analytics tags), and cheap shared hosting are the most common causes.

The fix: Run your site through Google PageSpeed Insights, which gives you a score out of 100 and a prioritised list of issues. Quick wins include compressing images before uploading, enabling browser caching, and switching to faster hosting. More complex fixes, such as eliminating render-blocking resources, require developer involvement.

3. HTTPS and site security

The problem: Any dental website that still loads over HTTP rather than HTTPS is displaying a "Not Secure" warning in most browsers. This erodes patient trust immediately and is also a confirmed negative ranking signal for Google.

Why it happens: SSL certificates (the technology behind HTTPS) expire annually. Some web hosts renew them automatically; others do not. It is also common for practices to have HTTPS set up on the homepage but not consistently across all pages.

The fix: Check your site by looking at the address bar. If you see a padlock, you are using HTTPS correctly. If you see a warning, contact your web host immediately to install or renew your SSL certificate. Ensure all HTTP URLs redirect automatically to their HTTPS equivalents.

4. Crawlability and indexation

The problem: Google discovers your pages by sending automated bots to crawl your site. If those bots are accidentally blocked, or if important pages are marked with a "noindex" tag, your pages will not appear in search results at all.

Why it happens: Developers sometimes add noindex tags during a website build to prevent half-finished pages from appearing in Google, then forget to remove them at launch. Incorrect robots.txt files can also block crawlers from entire sections of your site.

The fix: In Google Search Console, check the Coverage report. Pages marked as "Excluded" should be reviewed carefully. Any treatment pages or location pages that are excluded unintentionally need to be corrected. Your robots.txt file (found at yourdomain.co.uk/robots.txt) should not block any folders containing live patient-facing content.

5. XML sitemap

The problem: Without a clear XML sitemap submitted to Google, search engines must discover your pages by following links alone. This makes it easier for important pages to be missed or crawled less frequently.

Why it happens: Sitemaps are often not generated or submitted during a basic website setup, particularly if the site was built by a general web developer rather than an SEO-aware one.

The fix: Most modern content management systems can generate a sitemap automatically, often via a free plugin. Once generated, submit it through Google Search Console under the Sitemaps section. Your sitemap should include all live treatment pages, your about page, contact page, and any blog content you publish.

6. Duplicate content and canonical tags

The problem: If Google finds the same, or very similar, content on multiple URLs within your site, it will not know which version to rank. This dilutes the authority of your pages and can cause ranking fluctuations.

Why it happens: This is particularly common on dental websites that list very similar treatment variants (for example, composite bonding for upper teeth and composite bonding for lower teeth as separate pages with nearly identical text). It also arises from URL parameters, session IDs, or content accidentally published in multiple categories.

The fix: Where near-duplicate pages exist, consolidate them into a single comprehensive page wherever possible. Where separate URLs must exist, use a canonical tag to signal to Google which version is the authoritative one. This is a line of code in the page header that a developer can add.

7. Structured data markup

The problem: Google can display enhanced search results, including star ratings, opening hours, and FAQ dropdowns, directly on the results page. Without structured data markup, you are missing the opportunity to make your listing more prominent and informative than a competitor's plain blue link.

Why it happens: Structured data (also called schema markup) is an additional layer of code that most standard website builds do not include by default. It requires deliberate implementation.

The fix: For a dental practice, the most valuable schema types are LocalBusiness (or more specifically, Dentist schema), FAQPage, and BreadcrumbList. These can be added directly to your page code or via a plugin, depending on your CMS. After adding schema, use Google's Rich Results Test tool to check that it is implemented correctly.

8. Internal linking structure

The problem: Poor internal linking means that the authority accumulated by your well-performing pages is not being shared across your site. Important pages, such as implants or Invisalign, may be receiving little internal link equity and therefore ranking lower than they could.

Why it happens: Most dental websites are built with navigation menus in mind but without a deliberate strategy for linking between pages within the body content. New blog posts and service pages are added over time without connecting them to each other.

The fix: Audit your most important treatment pages and ensure they are linked to from other relevant pages on your site. Your homepage should link clearly to your core service pages. Blog articles should link to relevant treatment pages where there is a natural connection. A simple spreadsheet mapping which pages link to which is enough to start.

How a specialist dental marketing agency approaches this differently

Working through this checklist manually is entirely possible, and doing so will improve your site's performance. However, there are two areas where specialist support makes a material difference.

First, prioritisation. Technical audits typically surface a long list of issues, and not all of them carry equal weight. An agency experienced specifically in dental SEO understands which issues are causing the most damage to your rankings and will address those first, rather than working through a generic checklist in sequence.

Second, integration with wider strategy. Technical fixes on their own are necessary but not sufficient. They create the conditions for your content and local SEO efforts to succeed. An agency that handles technical work as part of a broader dental SEO service ensures that each element reinforces the others, rather than being treated in isolation.

It is also worth noting that technical issues can be reintroduced whenever your site is updated. A website built with SEO requirements in mind from the start, rather than retrofitted afterwards, reduces ongoing maintenance significantly. That is one of the reasons that dental website design and SEO are most effective when planned together.
