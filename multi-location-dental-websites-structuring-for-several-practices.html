Multi-location dental websites: structuring for several practices

By Cristian Savulescu, Founder · Published 25 September 2026

Managing more than one practice means your website needs to work harder, not just look bigger. This guide explains how to structure a multi-location dental website so every branch ranks locally, attracts the right patients, and reflects your brand consistently.

Growing from one dental practice to two, three, or more is a significant achievement. But many practice owners reach that point and then bolt a second location onto an existing website as an afterthought: a brief "Our Locations" page, a duplicated list of services, and a Google Maps embed. The result is a website that fails every branch equally. Patients searching locally cannot find the right practice, search engines struggle to distinguish one location from another, and the brand feels inconsistent. If you are managing or planning a multi-location dental group, getting the website structure right from the outset is one of the highest-return investments you can make.

Why Multi-Location Dental Websites So Often Go Wrong

The problem usually starts with good intentions. A practice owner builds a solid website for their first site, then adds pages for the second and third locations without reconsidering the underlying architecture. This creates several compounding issues.

Thin, duplicated content across locations

The most common mistake is copying service pages from one location to another with only the town name changed. Search engines are sophisticated enough to recognise near-duplicate content, and they will typically choose to rank only one version, or none. If your Sheffield and Doncaster pages both say "we offer teeth whitening in [location]" with identical surrounding text, neither page is doing its job.

No clear local signals

Google ranks dental practices locally based on relevance, proximity, and authority. A practice website that lumps all locations together under a single address or a generic "contact us" page sends weak geographic signals. Patients searching for a dentist in Barnsley or Chesterfield will be shown competitors whose websites clearly correspond to those areas.

Poor internal linking and confusing navigation

When a patient lands on your homepage, they need to reach their nearest branch quickly. If the navigation buries locations two or three clicks deep, or if there is no clear pathway from a service page to the relevant branch, patients leave. High exit rates on key pages tell search engines that your site is not satisfying user intent, which further undermines your rankings.

Inconsistent branding and information

Opening hours, team photographs, pricing ranges, and even the practice name can appear differently across pages when there is no structured template in place. For a regulated profession like dentistry, inconsistency erodes trust before a patient has even picked up the phone.

The Fix: A Location-First Website Architecture

The solution is to treat each practice location as a distinct entity within a coherent group structure. This is not the same as building separate websites for each branch, which fragments your domain authority and multiplies your maintenance burden. Instead, it means creating a deliberate hierarchy that gives each location its own strong presence while keeping the whole group under one roof.

Dedicated location landing pages

Every practice location should have its own page with a unique URL, typically structured as yourwebsite.co.uk/sheffield/ or yourwebsite.co.uk/doncaster/. That page should include the practice's full address, local phone number, opening hours, and a short description of the team and the area it serves. This is the page you will link to from your Google Business Profile for that branch, and it is the page that needs to rank for local searches.

Crucially, the copy on each location page must be genuinely distinct. Describe what makes that particular practice different: its team, its history in the community, any specialist equipment on site, or local landmarks that help patients navigate there. Generic filler copy will not rank and will not convert.

Location-specific service pages

For larger groups, it is worth creating service pages at the location level, particularly for high-value treatments such as dental implants, Invisalign, or same-day emergency appointments. A page structured as yourwebsite.co.uk/sheffield/dental-implants/ can rank for "dental implants Sheffield" independently, giving that branch a competitive advantage it simply cannot achieve from a generic group-level services page.

This does require more content to be written and maintained, which is where many practice owners underestimate the ongoing commitment. The reward, however, is compounding: each well-structured location page builds its own authority over time.

A clear group homepage that directs traffic intelligently

Your main homepage should communicate the group brand clearly, then immediately help visitors self-select their nearest branch. A simple interactive map, a prominent "Find your nearest practice" section, or a well-labelled navigation menu all serve this purpose. The homepage is not the place to bury contact details or list every service offered by every branch. Its job is to orient the visitor and route them efficiently.

Consistent schema markup across all locations

Schema markup, specifically LocalBusiness or Dentist schema, tells search engines exactly who you are, where you are, and what you offer. Each location page should have its own schema block with the correct name, address, phone number, and opening hours for that branch. This is one of the most technically impactful things you can do for local search visibility, and it is frequently overlooked in DIY or template-built websites.

Structured internal linking between locations and services

Every location page should link to the relevant service pages for that branch, and every group-level service page should reference or link to the location pages where that service is available. This creates a web of internal links that helps search engines understand the relationship between your pages and helps patients find what they need without dead ends.

Technical Considerations That Cannot Be Ignored

Beyond content structure, a multi-location dental website has specific technical requirements that a generic website builder or a non-specialist agency is unlikely to address properly.

Canonicalisation and duplicate content prevention

If you have similar content appearing at multiple URLs, you need canonical tags to tell search engines which version to treat as the primary one. Without this, you risk splitting your ranking potential across competing pages. This requires someone who understands technical SEO, not just page design.

Google Business Profile alignment

Each practice location should have its own verified Google Business Profile, and the name, address, and phone number on that profile must exactly match what appears on the corresponding location page of your website. Any discrepancy, even a minor formatting difference, can reduce the local ranking signals for that branch. If you are expanding across South Yorkshire or North Derbyshire, keeping these aligned as you open new sites is an ongoing task, not a one-time setup.

Page speed and mobile performance at scale

More pages mean more opportunities for performance issues to creep in. Image-heavy location pages with embedded maps can become slow on mobile, which matters both for user experience and for search rankings. A well-built multi-location site needs a performance-focused technical foundation, not just a theme applied at scale.

Where to Start If You Are Planning a Multi-Location Website

If you are about to open a second or third practice, or if you already have multiple locations but your website is not structured to support them, the right time to address this is before the problem compounds further. Every month that passes with a poorly structured site is a month your competitors are capturing local search traffic that should be coming to you.

Start by auditing what you have. Check whether each location has its own dedicated page with unique content, verify that your Google Business Profiles match your website exactly, and review your internal linking to confirm patients can navigate between locations and services without confusion. If any of these are missing, you have a clear priority list.
