Writing compliant Google Ads copy for dentists

By Cristian Savulescu, Founder · Published 18 November 2026

Writing Google Ads copy for a dental practice means navigating GDC guidelines, ASA rules and Google's own policies at the same time. This article shows UK practice owners exactly where ad copy goes wrong and how to fix it.

Google Ads can be a genuinely effective way to bring new patients through the door. But writing the ad copy itself is where many dental practices run into trouble. The challenge is not simply finding the right words. It is writing copy that complies with General Dental Council (GDC) guidance, satisfies Advertising Standards Authority (ASA) rules, passes Google's own healthcare advertising policies, and still persuades a real person to click. Get any one of those wrong and you are either wasting budget or risking a formal complaint. This article walks you through where compliant dental ad copy tends to fail, why the mistakes happen and what good practice actually looks like.

Why dental Google Ads copy is harder to write than most

Most industries can make bold, comparative claims in their advertising without much regulatory risk. Dentistry cannot. The GDC's standards for the dental team, combined with ASA guidance on healthcare advertising, mean that common marketing phrases used freely in other sectors become genuinely problematic in a dental context.

The traps are easy to fall into. A practice might write "Get your best smile ever" thinking it sounds aspirational. But "best" is a superlative claim that the ASA would expect you to substantiate. "Pain-free treatment guaranteed" promises an outcome that simply cannot be guaranteed for every patient. "The UK's leading dental implant provider" needs hard evidence behind it. Without that evidence, any of these could lead to a complaint being upheld.

At the same time, Google's own advertising policies add another layer. Healthcare and medicines is a sensitive category under Google Ads. Practices must be verified, certain claims trigger disapprovals, and landing page content is scrutinised as well as the ad itself. If your landing page contradicts your ad or makes unsupported claims, the ad can be disapproved even if the headline itself is fine.

The most common compliance failures in dental ad copy

Superlative and comparative claims

Phrases like "the best," "number one," "leading," "top-rated" and "most advanced" are all comparative or superlative claims. Under ASA rules, these must be backed by robust, up-to-date evidence. For most practices, that evidence simply does not exist in a form that would satisfy a formal challenge. The safer approach is to describe what you genuinely offer without comparing yourself to unnamed competitors you have not actually measured yourself against.

Outcome guarantees

Claiming that treatment will be "pain-free," that results are "guaranteed" or that a patient "will love" their new smile all constitute outcome guarantees. Because clinical outcomes vary between patients, these claims are misleading by definition. The GDC expects advertising to reflect the reality that treatment outcomes depend on individual circumstances. Replace outcome guarantees with honest descriptions of the treatment experience or the range of results a patient might reasonably expect.

Before-and-after imagery and implied promises

While this is more relevant to display advertising than search text ads, it is worth noting that before-and-after images used to support your ads or on linked landing pages are subject to strict rules. The ASA has taken action against dental advertisers for before-and-after imagery that presents an unrealistic or misleading impression of likely outcomes. If your landing page features such imagery, it forms part of the overall ad claim.

Price claims that mislead

Advertising a headline price like "dental implants from £X" is not prohibited, but it must accurately reflect a price that is genuinely available, include any mandatory additional fees and not create a misleading impression about the total likely cost. Practices often advertise a component price (the implant fixture alone, for instance) without making clear that the abutment and crown are additional. This is a straightforward ASA compliance failure that also damages patient trust when they arrive for a consultation.

What compliant dental ad copy actually looks like

Compliance does not mean bland. The goal is copy that is honest, specific and genuinely useful to someone searching for the treatment you offer. Here is how that works in practice.

Lead with the treatment, not a vague promise

Rather than "Transform your smile today," write "Dental implants in Sheffield. Same-day consultations available." The second version tells the searcher exactly what you offer, where you offer it and something genuinely useful about your service. It is also far easier to defend under ASA rules because it makes no claim that requires substantiation.

Use genuine differentiators, not invented ones

If your practice has specific accreditations, if your implant dentist has a postgraduate qualification, if you offer extended evening appointments or if you have a Care Quality Commission (CQC) rating that sets you apart, these are legitimate differentiators. Use them. "BDA Good Practice member. Evening appointments available. CQC-registered" is more credible and more compliant than any superlative claim you might otherwise reach for.

Write for the specific search intent

Someone searching "emergency dentist near me" has a very different intent to someone searching "Invisalign cost Yorkshire." Your ad copy should reflect that difference. For the emergency search, reassurance and speed matter: "Emergency dental appointments. Same-day slots. Call now." For the Invisalign search, information and credibility matter more: "Invisalign-certified practice. Free consultation. Transparent pricing." Matching copy to intent also improves Quality Score, which affects your cost per click. Keep the landing page consistent with the ad

This point is underestimated. If your ad says "Free consultation," your landing page must make that offer clearly and explain any conditions attached. If your ad headline mentions a specific location, that location should appear prominently on the landing page. Inconsistency between ad and page is both a Google policy concern and an ASA concern, because the overall impression created by the two together is what the ASA evaluates, not the ad in isolation.

Building a compliance check into your workflow

Writing compliant copy once is straightforward enough. The harder task is maintaining compliance as your campaigns evolve. Ad variations are tested, promotions are added, seasonal copy is introduced. Each change is a new opportunity to introduce a problematic claim.

A simple internal checklist helps. Before any new ad or ad variation goes live, ask:

  • Does this copy make any superlative or comparative claim? If so, what is the evidence?
  • Does any part of the copy imply a guaranteed outcome?
  • Are all price claims accurate, complete and clearly qualified?
  • Is the linked landing page consistent with every claim made in the ad?
  • Would a reasonable patient be misled by any part of this ad, even unintentionally?

This is not bureaucracy for its own sake. It reflects the standard the ASA and GDC apply, and building it into your process means you catch problems before they become complaints.

The bottom line on compliant dental ad copy

Writing Google Ads copy for a dental practice is a craft that sits at the intersection of marketing, regulation and clinical credibility. The practices that do it well are not the ones with the boldest headlines. They are the ones whose copy is specific, honest, consistent with their landing pages and grounded in what they can genuinely substantiate. That approach also tends to perform better commercially, because it builds the kind of trust that converts a searcher into a patient rather than simply attracting a click.
