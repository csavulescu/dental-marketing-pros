Advertising finance & payment plans for dental treatment

Promoting payment plans and dental finance can significantly increase treatment uptake, but the rules around advertising dental finance plans are strict and easy to get wrong. This guide explains exactly what UK practice owners need to know before they publish a single word.

Payment plans and patient finance options can be transformative for a dental practice. When patients can spread the cost of implants, orthodontics, or a full smile makeover over twelve or twenty-four months, the conversation about treatment acceptance changes completely. The problem is that advertising those options is a compliance minefield. Practices across South Yorkshire and North Derbyshire are running Google ads, posting on social media, and updating their websites with finance messaging that, in many cases, breaches Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) rules, Advertising Standards Authority (ASA) codes, or General Dental Council (GDC) guidance, sometimes all three at once. This article explains why that happens, what the rules actually require, and how to promote your finance options confidently and correctly.

Why Dental Finance Advertising Is Regulated So Tightly

Dental treatment finance is, in legal terms, a consumer credit product. The moment you advertise that patients can spread payments, pay monthly, or access interest-free credit, you are making a financial promotion. That triggers a separate body of law from the one governing clinical advertising, specifically the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 and the Consumer Credit Act 1974, enforced day-to-day by the FCA.

Practices do not usually hold their own FCA authorisation. Most work under the authorisation of a third-party lender, such as Chrysalis Finance, Medenta, or similar providers, under what is known as an Appointed Representative (AR) arrangement. That arrangement gives you permission to introduce patients to credit, but it does not give you a free pass to say whatever you like in your marketing. The AR relationship comes with strict conditions about how, and how accurately, you describe the product.

On top of FCA requirements, the ASA applies the UK Code of Non-broadcast Advertising (CAP Code) and the Broadcast Code (BCAP) to all dental ads, including those describing finance. The GDC's Standards for the Dental Team and its guidance on ethical advertising add a further layer, particularly around claims that could mislead patients about clinical outcomes or costs.

The Most Common Mistakes Practices Make

Headline rates that omit mandatory information

The most frequent error is leading with a monthly payment figure or an interest rate without including the other information the law requires alongside it. Under the Consumer Credit Act, a financial promotion that includes a "trigger" element, such as a repayment amount, an interest rate, or the phrase "interest-free," must also include the representative APR, the total amount payable, and the duration of the agreement. Omitting any of these turns your ad into an unlawful financial promotion, regardless of how accurate the headline figure itself is.

This catches a lot of well-intentioned practices out. A Facebook post saying "Dental implants from £89 per month" looks harmless. In FCA terms, it is not. The monthly figure is a trigger, and the missing required information means the post is non-compliant.

Using the phrase "interest-free" without qualification

Offering 0% finance is a genuinely powerful patient incentive, but "interest-free" is itself a trigger term under the Consumer Credit Act. It must be accompanied by the representative APR and the full credit details, even if that APR is 0%. Saying "interest-free finance available" with no further detail is not enough.

Implying guaranteed approval

Phrases such as "everyone accepted" or "no credit checks" are likely to breach both FCA rules and the ASA CAP Code. Credit decisions are made by the lender, not the practice, and claiming or implying that approval is automatic is misleading. Even "subject to status" must be written prominently rather than buried in microscopic footer text.

Testimonials that reference finance terms

Patient testimonials are already a sensitive area under GDC guidance. If a testimonial references the finance arrangement, for example a patient saying they could not believe how affordable the monthly payments were, that introduces an additional layer of FCA risk. The testimonial becomes part of a financial promotion and must meet the same disclosure requirements as any other ad.

What Compliant Dental Finance Advertising Actually Looks Like

Compliance does not mean your marketing has to be dull or legalistic. It means being precise. Here is what a properly structured finance promotion includes.

  • Representative example: When any trigger element appears, a representative example must be shown clearly and prominently. This typically includes the purchase price, deposit (if any), loan amount, duration, monthly repayment, representative APR, and total amount payable.
  • "Representative" APR wording: Unless you are advertising a specific, personalised offer, you must use the word "representative" before the APR, because individual patient rates may vary based on credit assessment.
  • Prominence: Required information cannot be hidden. Small print at the bottom of a landing page, or in a colour that blends with the background, is not acceptable. The ASA has upheld complaints on exactly this basis.
  • "Subject to status" and lender name: These must appear in every financial promotion and be legible at the size used.
  • Honest eligibility language: Phrases like "finance may be available, subject to credit assessment" are more accurate than "everyone qualifies."

Your lender or AR provider will usually supply a compliance checklist or sign-off process for marketing materials. If yours does not, that is itself a risk signal. Use it. Do not publish finance-related content without it being reviewed against your credit agreement and FCA obligations.

Platform-Specific Risks You May Not Have Considered

Google Ads

Google applies its own financial services advertising policy on top of legal requirements. In the UK, ads promoting credit products may require pre-approval from Google and must link to a landing page that also meets their requirements. A dental PPC campaign promoting finance needs to be built with both Google's policies and FCA rules in mind from the very first keyword, not retrofitted after the account goes live. A single non-compliant ad group can cause the entire account to be suspended.

Social media

Meta (Facebook and Instagram), TikTok, and LinkedIn all have financial advertising policies. Meta, in particular, restricts certain credit-related claims and may disapprove ads that do not meet its definitions of accurate financial promotion. Boosted posts are treated as paid advertising and are subject to the same rules as a formally created campaign.

Your own website

Many practices focus on their paid advertising and forget that their website is also an advertising medium regulated by the ASA. A page titled "Dental Finance Options" that includes monthly payment figures without a representative example is just as non-compliant as a Google ad with the same omission. The ASA receives complaints about website content and can require removal or amendment.

How a Specialist Agency Handles This Differently

A general marketing agency may produce attractive-looking ads and well-written landing pages, but unless they understand the specific intersection of FCA financial promotion rules, ASA advertising codes, and GDC dental advertising guidance, they are likely to create materials that expose your practice to regulatory risk. The consequences of getting this wrong range from having campaigns taken down at a critical moment to formal ASA rulings, which are published publicly and can damage practice reputation.

Working with an agency that specialises in dental marketing, and that understands the compliance framework within which UK practices operate, means a different process from the start. Campaign briefs include a compliance review. Ad copy is written with representative examples built in, not added as an afterthought. Landing pages are structured so required information is prominent and legible. Lender approval processes are factored into campaign timelines so nothing goes live without the necessary sign-off.

For paid search campaigns specifically, this matters enormously. Finance-related keywords are among the most commercially valuable in dental advertising. Getting them wrong is expensive in more than one sense: wasted ad spend on non-compliant campaigns, or accounts suspended just as a seasonal promotion was due to launch.

A Practical Checklist Before You Publish

Before any finance-related content goes live on any channel, run through the following.

  1. Does the content include a trigger element (monthly payment, APR, interest rate, or the phrase "interest-free")? If yes, a representative example is required.
  2. Is the representative APR calculation based on your lender's actual figures, reviewed and approved by them?
  3. Is all required information displayed prominently, in a legible size, and with sufficient contrast?
  4. Does the content include "subject to status" and the name of the credit provider?
  5. Has the content been reviewed by your AR lender or their compliance team?
  6. Does the ad platform require pre-approval for this type of financial promotion?
  7. Are any testimonials or patient quotes free from specific references to finance terms or repayment amounts?

If you cannot answer yes to all of these, the content is not ready to publish.

Getting This Right Is Worth the Effort

Finance and payment plans, when advertised correctly, are one of the most effective tools a practice has for increasing treatment acceptance on higher-value procedures. Patients who are aware of accessible payment options are more likely to proceed with treatment they have been deferring. The compliance framework around advertising dental finance plans rules exists to protect those patients, and navigating it carefully protects your practice too.
