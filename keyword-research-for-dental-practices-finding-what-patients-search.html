Keyword research for dental practices: finding what patients search

Most dental practice websites miss out on new patients not because they lack content, but because they are targeting the wrong search terms entirely. This guide walks practice owners through the fundamentals of dental keyword research and explains how to build a strategy that connects your services to local demand.

Many dental practice websites look perfectly professional. They have a services page, an about section, some patient photos, and a contact form. Yet month after month, the phone stays quiet and the enquiry inbox stays empty. In most cases, the problem is not design or even content quality. It is that the site has been built around the words the practice thinks patients use, rather than the words patients actually type into Google. Getting this distinction right is the foundation of any effective dental marketing strategy, and it starts with proper dental keyword research.

Why most practice websites target the wrong terms

Dentists are trained in clinical language. That is entirely appropriate in a clinical setting, but it creates a predictable problem online. A practice might optimise its website for "composite restorations" or "dental implant prosthetics," while local patients are searching for "tooth-coloured fillings Sheffield" or "missing tooth replacement Rotherham." Both sides are describing the same treatments, but they are using entirely different vocabulary.

This mismatch is extremely common, and it is not a reflection of poor effort. It happens because practice owners and their teams are simply too close to their own services to see them from a patient's perspective. Add to this the fact that many website designers and general digital agencies use generic keyword lists that are not tailored to dentistry, local geography, or the specific mix of NHS and private services a practice offers, and the problem compounds quickly.

There is also a tendency to target broad, highly competitive terms like "dentist" or "dental practice" without considering that these terms are dominated nationally by large directories and aggregator sites. A single-location practice in Doncaster or Chesterfield is unlikely to outrank a well-funded comparison platform for a one-word search. The opportunity lies in being specific.

Understanding the types of dental search queries

Before running any keyword research tool, it helps to understand the three broad categories of search queries your potential patients are using.

Navigational queries

These are searches from people who already know your practice name. They are typing it in to find your phone number or opening hours. You should rank for these automatically, but they will not bring you new patients.

Informational queries

These are from people at the early stage of their decision. They are searching for things like "how much does Invisalign cost in the UK" or "is tooth whitening safe." They are researching, not yet booking. This content can build trust and bring visitors to your site, but converting them takes more effort.

Transactional queries

These are from people who are ready to act. They have already decided they need a dentist or a specific treatment and they are searching for somewhere to go. "Emergency dentist Sheffield," "Invisalign provider Barnsley," or "dental implants near me" are transactional. These are the searches that produce bookings, and they should be the priority for most practice websites.

A well-structured keyword strategy addresses all three, but the weighting and where you invest your effort should reflect your business goals. If you are trying to fill appointment slots quickly, transactional and local keywords come first.

How to conduct dental keyword research yourself

You do not need to spend money on enterprise tools to get started. Several free and low-cost resources can help you build a solid initial keyword list.

Start with Google's own suggestions

Type a treatment name into Google and pay close attention to the autocomplete suggestions that appear. These are drawn from real search behaviour in your region. The "People also ask" boxes and the related searches at the bottom of the results page are equally useful. Spend twenty minutes working through your core services this way and you will already have a more patient-focused vocabulary than most practice websites use.

Use Google Search Console

If your website is already live, Google Search Console is one of the most valuable free tools available to you. Under the Performance section, you can see the exact queries that have brought visitors to your site, along with how often your site appears in results and how many people click through. Sorting this list by impressions will often reveal searches you are almost ranking for, where a modest amount of targeted content could make a meaningful difference.

Use a keyword research tool

Tools such as Google Keyword Planner (free with a Google Ads account), Ubersuggest, or the free tier of Semrush allow you to enter a seed keyword and see related terms, estimated search volumes, and an indication of how competitive each term is. For a practice in South Yorkshire or North Derbyshire, filter results to the UK and pay particular attention to terms that combine a treatment with a location. These localised combinations tend to have lower competition and much higher conversion intent.

Group your keywords by topic and intent

Once you have a list, organise it. Group terms by treatment category, then by intent. This helps you plan which pages need to be created or improved and prevents the common mistake of trying to target too many different terms on a single page. Each core service should have its own dedicated page, optimised around the most relevant local keyword for that treatment.

Local keyword research: the factor most practices overlook

For a dental practice, geography is everything. You are not competing nationally. You are competing within a defined catchment area, and your keyword strategy should reflect the towns, suburbs, and neighbourhoods you realistically serve.

This means going beyond "dentist Sheffield" and thinking about the surrounding areas. A practice based in Rotherham might attract patients from Maltby, Wickersley, Mexborough, and Rawmarsh. A practice in Chesterfield might serve patients from Staveley, Hasland, or Clay Cross. Creating content that references these areas, and using keyword combinations that reflect them, significantly improves your visibility to people who are genuinely likely to become patients.

It is also worth researching how local patients phrase things. Someone in Doncaster might search differently from someone in Sheffield, even for the same treatment. Local knowledge, combined with data from the tools above, allows you to build a keyword map that reflects how your specific community actually searches.

Your dental SEO strategy should treat local keyword targeting not as an afterthought but as a core pillar from the outset.

Common keyword research mistakes to avoid

  • Targeting volume over relevance. A term with high search volume is only valuable if the people searching it are likely to become your patients. A thousand searches a month for a broad term you cannot realistically rank for is worth less than fifty searches a month for a specific local term where you can achieve visibility.
  • Ignoring negative intent. Some searches around dental topics are informational to the point of being almost never transactional. Targeting these heavily can drive traffic that never converts.
  • Keyword cannibalism. This occurs when multiple pages on your site target the same term, causing them to compete with each other in search results. Each page should have a clear primary keyword focus.
  • Treating keyword research as a one-time task. Search behaviour evolves. New treatments become popular. Local competitors enter the market. Keyword research should be reviewed at least every six months.
  • Forgetting about NHS versus private intent. Patients searching for NHS dental care often use very different language from those seeking private treatment. If your practice offers both, your keyword strategy should reflect this distinction clearly.

How a specialist agency approaches dental keyword research differently

General digital marketing agencies can run keyword research tools, but they often lack the context to interpret the results correctly for a dental practice. They may not understand the regulatory constraints around how dental treatments are described online, the nuances of NHS and private positioning, or the specific competitive landscape in a particular South Yorkshire or North Derbyshire postcode.

A specialist dental marketing agency brings several things to the table that generic providers cannot easily replicate.

First, accumulated data from working specifically within dentistry means faster, more accurate identification of which keyword categories perform well for different practice types. A practice focused on cosmetic dentistry has a very different keyword profile from one built around family NHS care or specialist implant provision.

Second, local market knowledge matters. Understanding which towns around Sheffield or Doncaster have underserved demand, which competitors are ranking and how robust their positions are, and where the genuine opportunities lie requires sustained attention to a specific geographic area. This is not something a generalist agency with clients across multiple sectors can easily maintain.

Third, keyword research done well does not sit in isolation. It informs your page structure, your content plan, your Google Business Profile optimisation, and your local link-building priorities. A specialist agency builds a coherent strategy where each element supports the others, rather than producing a keyword report that then sits unused in a folder.

Properly conducted dental SEO begins with understanding exactly what your prospective patients are searching for, then building your online presence around those terms with precision and consistency.

The practical starting point is straightforward: spend time with Google's own suggestions and Search Console data, build an initial keyword list organised by treatment and intent, and audit your existing pages to see how well they reflect what local patients are actually searching for.
