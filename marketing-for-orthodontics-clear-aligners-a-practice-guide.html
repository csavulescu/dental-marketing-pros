Marketing for orthodontics & clear aligners: a practice guide

Clear aligner treatments are among the highest-value services a dental practice can offer, yet many practices struggle to fill their books consistently. This guide explains why orthodontics aligner marketing so often underperforms and what to do about it.

Clear aligners and orthodontic treatments sit at the top end of the treatment fee scale for most general and mixed dental practices. They are also among the most actively searched-for treatments by adult patients who are already motivated, already researching, and often already willing to spend. Yet a large number of practices in South Yorkshire and North Derbyshire are not converting that demand into booked consultations at anything close to the rate they should be. This article explains the specific reasons orthodontics aligner marketing so frequently underperforms, and sets out a practical framework for improving results without wasting your marketing budget.

Why Orthodontic and Aligner Marketing Is Different

Orthodontic treatments, and clear aligners in particular, present a different marketing challenge to routine dental services. A patient searching for an emergency dentist needs to act today. A patient considering clear aligners is usually at the start of a longer decision-making journey. They are comparing providers, reading reviews, watching before-and-after content, and worrying about price. The average gap between a patient first researching aligners and actually booking a consultation can stretch across several weeks or even months.

This matters because most practices treat aligner marketing the same way they treat general dentistry marketing: a Google Business Profile, perhaps a page on their website, and the hope that interested patients will simply ring up. That approach can generate some enquiries, but it leaves a great deal of value on the table. To compete effectively, you need a strategy that is built around the patient decision journey, not just around visibility.

The Core Problems Practices Run Into

Weak or generic website content

The majority of dental practice websites have a single page for clear aligners or orthodontics that reads like a product brochure: a brief description of the treatment, a price range, and a contact form. This content does almost nothing to address the concerns that are actually delaying a patient's decision. Common concerns include whether the treatment will be suitable for their specific case, how discreet the aligners really are in daily life, what happens if something goes wrong mid-treatment, and how the cost compares to what they have seen advertised elsewhere.

A page that does not answer those questions will not rank well in organic search, and it will not convert the visitors it does receive. Both problems reinforce each other: poor content means lower rankings, lower rankings mean less traffic, and the few visitors who do arrive leave without enquiring.

Targeting too broadly with paid advertising

Paid search and social advertising can generate aligner enquiries quickly, but they are also the easiest way to burn through a budget without return. The two most common mistakes are targeting keywords that attract patients outside a realistic travel distance, and sending paid traffic to a generic homepage rather than a dedicated landing page built around the specific offer. A patient clicking on an aligner ad wants to land on a page that immediately speaks to aligners, gives them a clear next step, and makes it easy to request a consultation. If they land on your homepage and have to work to find that information, most of them will leave.

No structured follow-up for enquiries

Aligner enquiries are rarely ready to convert on first contact. Many practices follow up once by telephone, fail to reach the patient, and then move on. This is a significant source of lost revenue. Patients who enquire but do not immediately book a consultation are not necessarily uninterested. They are often still in the comparison phase. A practice that follows up consistently, through a mix of phone, email, and where appropriate SMS, will convert a meaningfully higher proportion of those initial enquiries into consultations.

The Fix: A Joined-Up Approach to Aligner Marketing

Build content that matches the patient journey

Rather than a single aligner page, think in terms of content that addresses patients at different stages of their decision. A patient in early research mode needs reassurance and education: how do clear aligners work, are they right for mild, moderate or more complex cases, what is the difference between branded systems. A patient who is almost ready to book needs social proof, clear pricing information, and a low-friction way to take the next step. Creating content across these stages not only improves your organic rankings for orthodontics aligner marketing related terms, it also gives you material to use in retargeting campaigns and email sequences.

It is worth investing in genuinely specific content rather than filling pages with vague claims. If your practice uses a particular aligner system, explain why you chose it and what the clinical benefits are for your patient base. If your lead orthodontist has specific experience or postgraduate training, that is worth stating clearly. Specificity builds trust, and trust is what converts a researcher into a patient.

Use paid search with properly structured campaigns

Done well, pay-per-click advertising for dental practices is one of the most effective ways to generate aligner consultations in a defined local area. The key is precision: tightly defined geographic targeting, negative keywords to filter out irrelevant searches such as NHS orthodontics or children's braces where these are outside your service offer, and dedicated landing pages for each campaign rather than directing traffic to your main website.

Your landing page should do several things clearly: confirm the location and the treatment, address the most common objections (usually cost and suitability), include a straightforward consultation booking mechanism, and offer something of practical value to a patient who is not yet ready to book, such as a downloadable guide or a brief phone assessment. This last point is important. Not every click will convert on the day, but capturing contact details from warm prospects allows you to continue the conversation over the following weeks.

Set up a structured follow-up process

A follow-up sequence for aligner enquiries does not need to be complicated, but it does need to be consistent. At a minimum, this should include an immediate acknowledgement of the enquiry, a follow-up call within one working day, and at least two further touchpoints over the following fortnight if the patient has not yet booked. Email is well-suited to this because it allows you to share useful content, such as answers to frequently asked questions or information about financing options, without feeling intrusive. The goal is to remain present and helpful while the patient completes their decision-making process.

Practical Priorities for Practice Owners

If you are reviewing your approach to aligner marketing, the following are the areas most likely to make a measurable difference in the short to medium term:

  • Audit your existing aligner content. Does it address the questions patients are actually asking? Does it reflect your specific team, systems, and clinical approach?
  • Check where your paid traffic lands. If aligner ad clicks are going to your homepage, that is a straightforward fix with an immediate impact on conversion rates.
  • Review your enquiry follow-up process. How many touchpoints does your team make before marking an enquiry as lost? Is there a documented process, or does it depend on individual initiative?
  • Look at your local search visibility. Search for the aligner and orthodontic terms your ideal patients would use, in the areas you serve. Where does your practice appear, and how does your listing compare to competitors?
  • Consider the patient journey end to end. From the first search to the booked consultation, how smooth and reassuring is the experience? Where are the points at which patients are most likely to drop out?

None of these require a complete overhaul of your marketing. In most cases, targeted improvements to existing activity will produce results more quickly than starting from scratch.

A Note on Compliance

Advertising orthodontic and aligner treatments in the UK is subject to GDC standards and ASA guidelines. This includes restrictions on the use of before-and-after images in certain contexts, the need to avoid misleading claims about treatment outcomes, and requirements around how pricing and finance options are presented. Any agency or individual managing your marketing should have a clear working knowledge of these requirements. Getting this wrong is not just a regulatory risk: it can damage patient trust and the reputation of your practice.

Next Steps

If your practice offers clear aligners or orthodontic treatment and you are not consistently filling your consultation diary, the most useful thing you can do is to have an honest conversation about where in the patient journey you are losing people. In many cases, the problem is identifiable and fixable without significant additional spend: it is a matter of applying the right approach to the right part of the process.
