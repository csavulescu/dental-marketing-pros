Is it too early for dental practices to invest in GEO?

By Cristian Savulescu, Founder · Published 10 November 2026

AI-powered search is changing how patients find dental practices, but many owners are unsure whether now is the right time to act. This article gives you a clear, honest answer.

A question we hear from practice owners with increasing regularity goes something like this: "I keep hearing about AI search and GEO, but is it actually worth doing anything about right now, or should I wait until it becomes more established?" It is a fair question. Marketing budgets are not unlimited, and committing resources to something that feels uncertain is uncomfortable. But the framing of that question contains a hidden assumption worth examining: that "waiting to see" is the neutral, risk-free option. In reality, the decision about whether to invest in GEO for your dental practice now or later carries consequences in both directions, and understanding those consequences clearly is the only way to make a genuinely informed choice.

What GEO actually is, and why it matters for dental practices

GEO stands for Generative Engine Optimisation. It refers to the process of making your practice more visible, more credible, and more useful to AI-powered tools such as ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Gemini, Microsoft Copilot, and Perplexity. These tools are increasingly being used by people who want quick, synthesised answers to questions like "where's a good NHS dentist near me?" or "which dental practices in Sheffield offer Invisalign?"

Unlike traditional search engines, which return a list of links and let the user decide, AI assistants often produce a single, structured answer. They draw on a range of signals, including your website content, third-party mentions, structured data, reviews, and broader online presence, to construct that answer. If your practice is well-represented in those signals, you have a reasonable chance of being included. If you are not, you are simply absent from the response entirely.

If you are starting from scratch on this topic, What is GEO? A plain-English guide for dental practice owners gives a solid grounding before you go further.

The timing problem: why "wait and see" is not neutral

The dental marketing landscape has been through this pattern before. When Google Maps became a significant source of new patient enquiries, practices that built their local presence early gathered reviews, citations, and visibility before the space became crowded. Those that waited found themselves starting from behind, competing against established profiles with hundreds of reviews and years of consistent signals.

GEO is following a recognisably similar path. AI-powered features in search are not a future possibility. They are already live. Google AI Overviews appear in results for many health and location-based queries today. ChatGPT's browsing capabilities and Perplexity's real-time indexing mean that AI tools are actively reading and referencing web content right now, not at some future point when the technology "matures."

The practices building GEO-ready content, earning authoritative mentions, and structuring their online presence appropriately are doing so in a space that is not yet saturated. That window does not stay open indefinitely.

This does not mean every practice should immediately overhaul everything. It means that understanding what GEO requires, and beginning to implement it systematically, is a reasonable and timely thing to do now. The cost of doing nothing is not zero. It is the opportunity cost of a head start that closes over time.

What GEO actually requires from a dental practice

One concern practice owners raise when they first look at GEO is that it sounds technically complex or expensive. Some of it does require specialist knowledge. But a significant proportion of what makes a dental practice GEO-ready aligns closely with things that constitute good marketing practice anyway.

Clear, structured, authoritative content

AI tools use content to understand what your practice offers, who it serves, and how credible it is. Pages that answer specific questions clearly, written in plain language, covering your treatments, your team's qualifications, your location, and your approach, are far more useful to an AI assistant than vague or promotional copy. If your website currently says things like "we offer a wide range of dental treatments in a friendly environment," that tells an AI model almost nothing concrete.

Content that specifies which treatments you offer, what they involve, who they suit, and where you are based gives the model something to work with. This is not about keyword stuffing or gaming an algorithm. It is about being genuinely informative.

A trustworthy, consistent online footprint

AI tools draw on signals beyond your own website. Reviews on Google, mentions in local publications, listings in credible directories, and consistent name, address, and phone number (NAP) data all contribute to how a model assesses your practice's credibility and relevance. Practices with inconsistent or sparse external presence are harder for AI tools to confidently reference.

Technical structure that AI can read

Structured data markup, clean page architecture, fast load times, and mobile-friendly design all affect how easily AI crawlers can process your site. Many practices have websites that are visually acceptable but technically poorly structured underneath. That matters more as AI-powered tools become a larger share of how people discover health services.

It is also worth understanding how GEO sits alongside your existing SEO strategy. These are related but distinct disciplines, and the article GEO vs SEO for dentists: what's actually different explains the practical differences clearly.

What GEO does not promise, and why that matters

It is worth being direct about something. No reputable specialist can tell you that optimising for GEO will guarantee your practice appears in a specific AI-generated response. The models used by ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, and others are not transparent systems with published ranking criteria in the way that, say, Google's structured data guidelines are. What we know, with reasonable confidence, is that certain signals can help AI tools find, understand, and reference your practice. What we cannot know with certainty is exactly how those signals are weighted in any given model, or on any given day.

What we can say is that practices with thin, poorly structured content, inconsistent citations, and weak review profiles are systematically less likely to appear than practices that have invested in building a credible, well-structured online presence. That is not a guarantee of inclusion. It is a reduction of the factors that lead to exclusion.

If you work with an agency that is offering guaranteed rankings or citations in AI search results, treat that claim with scepticism. The honest position is that GEO is about improving your probability of visibility, not guaranteeing it.

How a specialist agency approaches GEO differently

A generalist web agency may update your website copy or run a blog. A dental marketing specialist approaches GEO with a different level of depth and domain knowledge.

At Dental Marketing Pros, our GEO service for dental practices is built around the specific signals and content structures that are most relevant to AI-powered health and location queries. That means auditing your current online presence across all the signals AI tools draw on, not just your website. It means creating content that reflects how patients actually ask questions about dental care, and structuring it so AI tools can process it reliably. It means ensuring your practice's information is consistent and credible across the broader web, not just on your own domain.

It also means integrating GEO activity with your existing SEO, paid search, and local visibility work, so that investment in one area supports the others. These disciplines are not competing. Done well, they reinforce each other.

So, is it too early?

The honest answer is no. The practices that will look back and wish they had acted earlier are, in many cases, the ones reading articles like this right now and deciding to revisit it in six months. AI-powered search is not arriving. It is here. The question is whether your practice is positioned to be found within it.

That said, investing in GEO does not mean doing everything at once or committing to a large spend immediately. It means starting with an honest assessment of where your online presence currently stands, identifying the gaps that most affect your visibility to AI tools, and working through them in a structured, prioritised way.
