What is GEO? A plain-English guide for dental practice owners

By Cristian Savulescu, Founder · Published 29 September 2026

AI-powered search tools are changing how patients find dental practices online. This plain-English guide explains what Generative Engine Optimisation means for practice owners and what you can do about it.

If you have noticed that Google looks different lately, you are not imagining it. Search results now often open with a block of text generated by artificial intelligence, summarising an answer before a patient ever sees a list of websites. Tools such as ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Gemini, Microsoft Copilot and Perplexity are fielding millions of questions every day, including questions like "which dentist in Sheffield accepts new NHS patients?" or "best dental implant clinic near Rotherham". The question for every practice owner in South Yorkshire and North Derbyshire is simple: when a patient asks one of these tools for a recommendation, does your practice have any chance of being mentioned? That is exactly what Generative Engine Optimisation (GEO) is designed to address.

What GEO actually means

GEO stands for Generative Engine Optimisation. Where traditional SEO focuses on ranking in the blue-link results of a search engine, GEO focuses on helping AI-driven tools understand, trust and potentially reference your practice when they generate answers for users.

These AI tools do not work like a search engine index. They pull information from a wide range of sources, including your website, online directories, review platforms, local news, professional bodies and structured data. They then synthesise that information into a generated response. Because they are trained on what is already publicly available about you, the quality, consistency and authority of your online presence matters a great deal.

It is important to be clear about something: no agency, including ours, can guarantee that an AI tool will cite or recommend your practice. The algorithms behind these systems are not fully disclosed by the companies that build them, and the outputs vary depending on the user's question, location and device. What we can say, with confidence, is that certain practices are better positioned than others to appear in AI-generated responses, and there are well-established steps that can help move a practice in the right direction. You can read a fuller overview of our approach on our GEO services page.

Why this matters for dental practice owners right now

The behaviour of patients searching for dental care is shifting. A growing proportion of people are turning to AI assistants as a first stop when they have a question, rather than scrolling through pages of search results. This is particularly true for higher-consideration decisions, such as finding a new dentist, understanding treatment costs, or comparing options for cosmetic or restorative work.

This shift does not mean traditional SEO is dead. Far from it. Google's standard search results still drive the majority of dental enquiries, and local SEO remains essential. But ignoring AI-generated search entirely is increasingly a gap in your practice's digital strategy. The practices that begin thinking about GEO now will be better placed as these tools continue to mature and attract wider usage.

How AI tools decide what to include in their answers

This is where it is important to be honest about the limits of what anyone knows. The large language models and AI search tools that generate responses do not publish a precise formula for what makes a source trustworthy or citation-worthy. What is well understood, however, is that they tend to favour information that is:

  • Consistent across multiple sources. If your practice name, address and phone number appear differently across your website, Google Business Profile, NHS finder and directory listings, AI tools are less likely to treat your practice as a reliable source of information.
  • Specific and well-structured. Vague content does not help AI tools understand what you offer, where you are, or who you serve. Clear, well-organised pages that answer real patient questions perform better.
  • Authoritative and credible. Mentions of your practice on reputable third-party sites, positive reviews on platforms such as Google and Trustpilot, and links from relevant local or professional sources all contribute to your perceived authority.
  • Technically sound. Structured data markup (schema), fast loading times and a mobile-friendly site help both traditional search engines and AI tools parse your content accurately.

These are not new ideas in isolation. What GEO does is apply them with the specific goal of being intelligible and trustworthy to AI-generated responses, not just to a search index.

What GEO looks like in practice for a dental clinic

Structured, question-led content

AI tools are built to answer questions. If your website content is written in the same format as a brochure from 2012, it is less likely to be useful to these systems. A GEO-informed content approach means writing pages and articles that directly address the questions your patients actually ask. "How much do dental implants cost in Sheffield?" or "What is involved in Invisalign treatment?" are the kinds of questions your site should be answering clearly, factually and in plain language.

This is good for patients regardless of AI. But it is especially relevant now because AI tools often extract passages directly from websites when constructing their answers. If your page has a clear, accurate, well-worded answer to a common question, it is in a better position to be drawn upon than a page that is vague or overly promotional.

Consistent, complete local information

AI tools cross-reference information about local businesses from multiple sources. Your Google Business Profile, NHS choices listing, Dentist.co.uk profile, Yell and any other directories you appear in should all carry identical, up-to-date information. This consistency signals reliability. It also matters for the kinds of location-based queries that AI tools handle, such as finding a dentist within a specific town or postcode area.

If you run paid advertising alongside your organic strategy, the same principle applies to how your targeting is structured. Our article on geo-targeting dental ads to reach patients in your catchment covers how location-based targeting can complement your broader digital presence.

Reviews and reputation signals

Reviews are increasingly influential in how AI tools assess a business. A practice with a strong, recent and genuine review profile across Google and other platforms is more likely to be regarded as credible. Encouraging satisfied patients to leave reviews is not a new idea, but in the context of GEO it takes on additional significance beyond what it does for your star rating.

Schema markup and technical foundations

Schema markup is code added to your website that helps machines understand what your business does, where it is located, what it specialises in and how to contact you. For dental practices, relevant schema types include local business markup, medical or dental clinic schema, FAQ schema and review schema. This is technical work that most practice owners will not want to handle themselves, but it is an important part of making your site legible to AI systems.

E-E-A-T: Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness and Trustworthiness

Google introduced E-E-A-T as a framework for evaluating content quality, and it has become highly relevant to GEO as well. For a dental practice, this means demonstrating that your content is written by or reflects the knowledge of qualified dental professionals, that your GDC registration and qualifications are visible, and that your site is transparently honest about what you offer. Avoid vague or exaggerated claims. AI tools, like human readers, are better served by content that is precise, measured and clinically responsible.

How a specialist dental marketing agency approaches GEO differently

GEO is not something that can be addressed with a single piece of content or a one-off technical fix. It requires an audit of your existing online presence, a clear content strategy, consistent attention to your local data across every directory and platform, and ongoing technical maintenance as AI tools themselves continue to evolve.

A generalist marketing agency may have a working knowledge of SEO but lack the specific understanding of how the dental sector is regulated by the GDC and overseen by the ASA, and what that means for the kind of content a practice can and cannot publish. Getting this wrong is not just a marketing problem. It is a compliance risk.

A measured view on where GEO is heading

It would be wrong to suggest that every patient in Doncaster or Chesterfield is now finding their dentist via ChatGPT. Traditional search, word of mouth and NHS referral pathways remain the dominant routes. But the direction of travel is clear. AI-generated responses are becoming a more prominent part of how people gather information, and the practices that take this seriously now will be better positioned than those that wait until the shift is impossible to ignore.

GEO is not a silver bullet and it is not a replacement for a well-run website, a strong Google Business Profile or a sensible paid advertising strategy. It is an additional layer of thinking that recognises the search landscape is changing and prepares your practice accordingly.
