How patients use AI assistants to choose a dentist

By Cristian Savulescu, Founder · Published 16 October 2026

AI assistants like ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Overviews are changing how patients find dental practices. Here is what practice owners need to understand and act on now.

A growing number of people are no longer typing "dentist near me" into Google and scrolling through results. Instead, they are asking an AI assistant. They type something like "find me a good NHS dentist in Sheffield" or "which dentist in Chesterfield is best for nervous patients" into ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Gemini, Copilot or Perplexity, and they expect a direct, conversational answer. If your practice is not part of that answer, you are losing enquiries to competitors who are, often without realising it is happening. Understanding how patients use AI to choose a dentist is no longer a future consideration for practice owners; it is a present one.

What AI-assisted dental searches actually look like

AI assistants do not behave like traditional search engines. Rather than presenting a list of ten blue links for the user to evaluate, they synthesise information from multiple sources and offer a summarised recommendation. The patient receives something that feels like advice from a knowledgeable friend rather than a directory listing.

The queries patients use with AI tools tend to be more conversational and specific than traditional search terms. Examples include:

  • "Which dentist in Rotherham accepts nervous adult patients?"
  • "Is there a family dentist near Dronfield with good reviews?"
  • "Can you recommend a dental practice in Sheffield that offers Invisalign?"
  • "What is the difference between an NHS and private dentist in Barnsley?"

These are not keyword searches. They are questions that carry intent, context and nuance. The AI interprets them and draws on whatever trustworthy, structured information it can find about local practices. If that information does not exist, is inconsistent or is buried in an outdated website, the AI either omits your practice entirely or, worse, presents inaccurate details.

Where AI assistants find information about your practice

This is the critical part for practice owners to understand, because it directly informs what you can actually do about it. AI models do not have access to a live, magic database of dental practices. They surface information that has been indexed, published and cited across a range of credible sources. These typically include:

Your Google Business Profile

Google AI Overviews in particular pulls heavily from Google Business Profiles when answering local queries. A complete, regularly updated profile with accurate opening hours, services listed, location details and genuine patient reviews gives the AI structured data it can confidently draw upon. An incomplete or inconsistent profile means the AI may skip your practice in favour of one with cleaner data.

Your practice website

AI tools crawl and analyse website content. Practices whose websites clearly describe what they do, who they serve and where they are located, using natural language that mirrors how patients actually speak, are far better positioned to be referenced. A website that buries service information in vague marketing copy, or that loads slowly on mobile, is harder for AI systems to extract useful information from. If you have not had your site reviewed recently, a free SEO audit is a practical starting point for identifying gaps.

Third-party directories and citations

Consistent mentions of your practice name, address and phone number across directories such as NHS.uk, Dentist Finder, Yell and local council websites help AI systems build confidence that your practice exists, is legitimate and serves the area you claim. Inconsistencies, a different postcode here, a slightly different trading name there, introduce doubt that AI tools resolve by prioritising more consistent competitors.

Patient reviews

Qualitative review content matters here in a way it did not with older SEO. When a patient asks an AI which dentist is best for anxious adults, the AI may draw on the language used in your reviews. If multiple reviewers have written about how calm and reassuring your team made them feel, that content can inform an AI's recommendation for that specific query. Reviews are not just social proof for humans; they are data for machines.

Why most dental practices are poorly positioned for AI search

The majority of dental practice websites in South Yorkshire and North Derbyshire were built with traditional SEO or simply brand presence in mind. That means they tend to have thin location content, generic service descriptions and little structured data to help machines understand what the practice actually offers and to whom. This was not a problem when patients searched for "dentist Sheffield" and clicked the third result. It is a significant problem when an AI is asked to recommend a dentist and has to decide which practices it can confidently describe.

There is also the issue of authority. AI assistants, particularly in a healthcare context, are cautious about recommending sources they cannot verify as credible. A practice with no external mentions, no reviews, no consistent directory presence and a website that has not been updated in three years presents a weak signal. The AI does not know enough about you to include you, so it defaults to practices it does.

This connects directly to broader patient acquisition challenges. If you are already working on how to get more patients for your dental practice, AI visibility should now be part of that conversation.

What you can do to improve your practice's AI visibility

It is important to be straightforward here: no agency, tool or tactic can guarantee that your practice will be cited or recommended by any AI assistant. The models that power these systems are updated regularly, their ranking logic is not publicly disclosed in full, and results vary by query, user location and platform. What responsible optimisation does is improve the quality and consistency of the signals those systems draw upon, which can help your practice appear more frequently in relevant AI-generated responses.

With that in mind, the following steps are genuinely worth prioritising:

Write your website content the way patients speak

AI tools are trained on natural language. Content that answers real patient questions in plain English, rather than keyword-stuffed paragraphs, tends to align better with how AI interprets search intent. For each core service, consider what a patient would actually ask, then answer that question clearly on the relevant page.

Create location-specific pages that go beyond the postcode

A single "Contact Us" page with your address is not enough. Dedicated pages for each location you serve, describing the area, the community you work within and the specific services available there, give AI systems richer, more useful data to draw from. This is the foundation of effective generative engine optimisation for dental practices.

Build and maintain your Google Business Profile properly

Fill out every section. Add photos regularly. Respond to every review, positive and negative, professionally. Post updates when you add new services or change opening hours. The more complete and active your profile is, the stronger a signal it sends to AI systems that rely on Google's own data.

Earn consistent, high-quality patient reviews

Encourage satisfied patients to leave detailed reviews, not just star ratings. The more descriptive the language in reviews, the more useful that content is as a signal for conversational AI queries. Ensure your team has a simple, comfortable process for requesting reviews after positive appointments.

Audit your directory listings for consistency

Check that your practice name, address and phone number are identical across every directory where you appear. Any discrepancy weakens your citation signals. This is often a surprisingly quick win once someone systematically works through it.

Consider how patients with specific needs search

AI queries often include patient-specific context. "Dentist for nervous patients," "wheelchair accessible dental practice" and "dentist that sees children" are the kinds of phrases an AI will try to match against what it knows about local practices. If your website clearly describes your approach to these patient groups, you are better positioned for those queries. This overlaps with broader accessibility thinking explored in accessibility on dental websites: compliance and more patients, and with the work involved in designing a dental website for nervous patients.

How a specialist dental marketing agency approaches this differently

General digital marketing agencies can set up a Google Business Profile and write website copy. What they typically cannot do is apply the specific knowledge required to position a dental practice correctly within a regulated healthcare context, across the nuanced geography of areas like Sheffield, Rotherham, Barnsley, Chesterfield and the surrounding communities.

A specialist approach involves auditing your current AI and search visibility, identifying where competitors are outperforming you in AI-generated responses, restructuring your content around the actual questions patients in your area are asking, and building the citation and review infrastructure that AI systems find credible. It also means staying current as the platforms themselves evolve, because what works in AI search today may need refinement as models update.

The practices that will benefit most from AI-driven patient acquisition over the next few years are those that invest in this infrastructure now, before their local competitors do. It is also worth noting that strong AI visibility supports long-term patient relationships, not just first contact. Patients who feel confident in a practice from their very first AI-assisted search are more likely to stay. That is a principle explored further in patient retention marketing: keeping the patients you win.
