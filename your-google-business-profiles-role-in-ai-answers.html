Your Google Business Profile's role in AI answers

By Cristian Savulescu, Founder · Published 24 November 2026

AI assistants are increasingly drawing on structured local data, including your Google Business Profile, when generating answers for people searching for a dentist. Here is what dental practice owners need to understand, and act on, before their competitors do.

Search behaviour is shifting faster than most practice owners realise. Patients who once typed "dentist near me" into Google and scrolled through a list of results are increasingly getting answers from AI tools such as Google AI Overviews, Gemini, ChatGPT, Copilot and Perplexity. These tools do not simply list links. They synthesise information and present a direct answer, often before the user ever clicks on a website. The uncomfortable truth for many practices is that their Google Business Profile, the asset sitting at the heart of local visibility, is not in a good enough shape to feature positively in those AI-generated answers. This article explains why that happens, what it means in practical terms, and what you can do about it.

Why AI tools pay attention to your Google Business Profile

To understand the problem, it helps to understand how AI search tools gather local information. Tools like Google's AI Overviews and Gemini have deep access to Google's own data ecosystem. Your Google Business Profile is a structured, verified data source, which is precisely the kind of signal that AI systems can read and process reliably. When someone asks an AI assistant for a dentist in a specific town, the system needs to pull structured, trustworthy information from somewhere. A well-maintained profile gives it exactly that.

Other AI tools, including ChatGPT and Perplexity, draw on a wider range of publicly available sources. They crawl websites, aggregate review platforms, local directories and structured data embedded in web pages. Your Google Business Profile feeds into the broader ecosystem of local information that these tools can access and cross-reference. If your profile is incomplete, inconsistent or outdated, there is a real risk that AI tools either ignore your practice entirely or present inaccurate information about it.

The common profile problems holding practices back

Most practices have a Google Business Profile. Far fewer have one that is genuinely optimised for the way search, including AI-assisted search, works today. The problems tend to fall into a few predictable categories.

Incomplete or inconsistent information

AI systems are drawn to clear, consistent, verifiable data. If your practice name, address or phone number differs between your Google Business Profile, your website, your NHS profile and local directories, that inconsistency creates noise. Rather than confidently presenting your practice as the answer to a query, an AI tool may downweight or skip your listing in favour of one that presents cleaner data. This is sometimes called a NAP inconsistency (Name, Address, Phone), and it is one of the most common and most fixable problems we encounter.

Missing services and categories

Google allows you to list the specific services you offer within your profile. Many practices leave these fields either blank or populated with only the most generic options. If a patient, or an AI tool fielding a question on their behalf, is looking for a practice offering Invisalign, dental implants or nervous patient care in a specific area, a profile that does not mention these services is effectively invisible for those queries. AI tools trying to match a detailed query to a local provider need that detail to work with.

Sparse or unresponded reviews

Reviews are not just a trust signal for human readers. They are a rich source of natural language that describes your practice, your team, your location and your services. AI tools can and do process review content when building a picture of a local business. A profile with very few reviews, or one where the owner never responds, presents a thinner, less authoritative picture. Responding to reviews also demonstrates engagement and legitimacy, two qualities that reflect well in any AI-assisted evaluation of your practice.

Outdated or missing photos and posts

Google Business Profile allows you to publish posts, upload photos and keep your profile visually current. These elements contribute to what Google understands about your practice, and they indicate an active, legitimate business. A profile that has not been updated in months or years signals stagnation, which is not the impression you want to give either human visitors or AI systems pulling together a local recommendation.

What good Google Business Profile optimisation actually looks like

Getting this right is not complicated, but it does require attention to detail and consistency over time. Here is what a properly maintained profile looks like in practice.

Complete every available field

That means your primary and secondary categories, your full service list, your opening hours (including special hours for bank holidays), your website URL, your appointment link and your practice description. The description should be written clearly, include the services and areas you serve, and read naturally rather than being stuffed with keywords. Think of it as a brief but accurate summary of your practice, written for both people and machines.

Build consistent citations across the web

Your Google Business Profile does not exist in isolation. AI tools, particularly those that do not have direct access to Google's data, build their understanding of local businesses by cross-referencing multiple sources. That means your practice details need to be consistent across NHS profiles, Yell, Bing Places, Apple Maps, dental directories and your own website. Inconsistencies across these sources can undermine the credibility of your profile data.

Actively generate and respond to reviews

You cannot pay for reviews or incentivise patients to leave them, as this would breach GDC and ASA guidelines. What you can do is make it straightforward for satisfied patients to leave feedback by sharing your profile link at the end of an appointment. Responding to every review, positive or critical, shows that yours is an active and professionally run practice. This is valuable both for patient trust and for the richness of data your profile presents to AI tools.

Post regularly and keep information current

Use Google Posts to share relevant updates: new services, team changes, seasonal information or anything that keeps your profile active. Update your hours promptly when they change. Add new photos of your practice, your team and your treatment areas on a regular basis. These are small tasks individually, but collectively they build a profile that consistently signals an active, credible practice.

How this connects to the broader shift in AI search

It is worth stepping back for a moment to place all of this in context. AI search is not a distant future concern. Google AI Overviews are already appearing for a wide range of queries, and tools like Perplexity and ChatGPT are being used by patients right now to find and evaluate local services. The strategies that helped practices rank in traditional local search, strong profiles, consistent citations, genuine reviews, clear website content, remain relevant. But the way those signals are interpreted and used is evolving.

If you are unfamiliar with the terminology being used in discussions of AI search, An AI search glossary for dental practice owners offers a plain-English introduction to the key concepts. And if you are wondering about the longer-term question of whether AI tools will eventually displace Google for local search altogether, Will AI search replace Google for finding a dentist? addresses that question directly.

The key point is this: your Google Business Profile is one of the most accessible, high-impact levers you have for influencing how AI tools present your practice. It will not guarantee that any particular AI tool cites or recommends you, nothing can do that honestly, but a well-optimised profile gives you a considerably better chance of being part of the conversation than a neglected one does.
