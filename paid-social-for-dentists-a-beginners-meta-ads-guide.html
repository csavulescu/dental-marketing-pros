Paid social for dentists: a beginner's Meta ads guide

By Cristian Savulescu, Founder · Published 15 September 2026

Running Facebook or Instagram ads without a clear strategy is one of the most common ways dental practices burn through budget with little to show for it. This guide walks you through the fundamentals of Meta advertising, written specifically for UK dental practice owners who want to grow their patient list without wasting money.

Many dental practice owners try Meta ads once, spend a few hundred pounds, and conclude that social advertising does not work for dentistry. The real issue is rarely the platform. It is almost always the setup. Facebook and Instagram have become genuinely powerful channels for attracting private patients, but the gap between a campaign that breaks even and one that consistently fills your diary is wider than most people expect. This guide covers the fundamentals in plain language, so you can go in with your eyes open, whether you decide to manage ads in-house or work with a specialist.

Why Meta Ads Are Worth Taking Seriously

Google Ads capture demand that already exists. Someone types "Invisalign Sheffield" because they have already decided they want clear aligners and they are ready to enquire. Meta ads work differently. They create demand by placing your message in front of people who match the profile of your ideal patient, before they have thought to search.

This distinction matters because dentistry has a large pool of potential patients who would say yes to a treatment if it were presented to them at the right moment and in the right way. Composite bonding, teeth whitening, dental implants and orthodontics all fall into this category. People are not urgently searching for these treatments every day, but they are interested if the offer is relevant and the practice looks trustworthy.

Meta's advertising platform, which covers both Facebook and Instagram, gives you tools to reach that audience by location, age, interests and behaviours. For a practice in South Yorkshire or North Derbyshire, that means you can serve ads specifically to adults within a realistic travel radius of your surgery, which is something that broadcast advertising has never been able to do cost-effectively.

The Most Common Mistakes Before You Even Launch

Sending traffic to the wrong page

A significant number of practices run Meta ads that link directly to their homepage. The homepage is designed for visitors who already know who you are. Someone who clicks a Invisalign ad expects to land on a page about Invisalign, with a clear explanation of the treatment, a price indication or a specific offer, and a single obvious next step. Sending them to a general homepage forces them to do extra work, and most of them will not bother. Build or use a dedicated landing page for each campaign.

Using the wrong campaign objective

Meta's Ads Manager asks you to choose a campaign objective before you do anything else. The options include Awareness, Traffic, Engagement, Leads and Conversions. Many first-time advertisers choose Traffic because they want more people visiting their site. In most cases, Leads or Conversions is the correct choice for a dental practice. Choosing Traffic tells Meta's algorithm to find people who click links. Choosing Leads tells it to find people who are likely to submit their details. These are not the same person, and the difference in campaign quality is considerable.

Audiences that are too broad or too narrow

A common beginner error is either targeting the entire country or, equally unhelpfully, creating an audience so tightly defined that Meta cannot spend your budget effectively. For most practices, a radius of 10 to 15 miles around your surgery, filtered by relevant age ranges for the treatment you are promoting, is a sensible starting point. If you are promoting dental implants, targeting adults aged 45 and above makes more sense than including 18-year-olds.

Building a Campaign That Actually Works

Choose one treatment and one audience per campaign

The temptation is to run a single ad promoting everything your practice offers. Resist it. A campaign focused on a single treatment, aimed at a single audience segment, will almost always outperform a general practice awareness ad. It allows you to write specific, relevant copy, choose images that match the treatment, and send people to a landing page that speaks directly to their concern.

Creative that does not look like an ad

On Meta, people are scrolling through content from friends, family and pages they follow. Your ad competes with all of that. The creative, meaning the image or video and the accompanying text, needs to stop the scroll without looking out of place. Overly polished, stock-photo imagery tends to perform poorly. Images of your actual surgery, your team or authentic before-and-after photography (used in full compliance with GDC guidance and ASA rules) perform better because they feel real.

Video performs particularly well on Instagram. A short, direct-to-camera video from the treating dentist explaining who the treatment is for and what to expect can build a level of trust that a static image simply cannot match.

Writing compliant ad copy

This point deserves particular attention for UK dental practices. The GDC and the ASA set clear boundaries on how dental treatments can be advertised. You must not make misleading claims, imply guaranteed outcomes, use before-and-after images in a way that could mislead, or use testimonials that cannot be verified. Before any ad goes live, check your copy against the CAP Code and GDC guidance. If you are uncertain, seek advice. Getting this wrong carries real reputational and regulatory risk.

Effective compliant copy is still possible. Focus on who the treatment is for, the experience patients can expect, and the process of getting started. You do not need to overpromise to write persuasive, interesting ad copy.

Using Meta's Lead Generation forms

Meta's native lead forms allow prospective patients to submit their name, phone number and email address without ever leaving the platform. For mobile users in particular, this reduces friction considerably. The downside is that the leads can be lower intent than someone who has visited your website, read your treatment page and chosen to fill in a contact form. For practices just starting out, native lead forms are a useful way to generate initial volume and test your messaging before investing in landing page development.

Budgeting and Expectations

There is no universal correct budget for Meta ads, but it is worth being realistic about what a small spend can achieve. Meta's algorithm needs data to optimise. A campaign that spends five pounds per day will accumulate that data slowly, which means it takes longer to exit the learning phase and start performing predictably. Most specialists recommend a minimum daily budget that allows for a meaningful number of impressions and clicks each week.

Cost per lead varies by treatment, location and how well your campaign is set up. Higher-value treatments such as implants or full-arch restorations can justify a higher cost per lead because the lifetime value of that patient is significant. Lower-value treatments require tighter cost management. Track your cost per booked appointment, not just your cost per lead, because leads that never convert to appointments have no value to your practice.

Tracking Results Properly

Without the Meta Pixel (now part of the broader Meta Business Suite tracking setup), you cannot measure conversions accurately, and you cannot retarget website visitors. Installing the Pixel correctly on your website is a foundational step that should happen before you launch any campaign. Once it is in place, you can track which ads are generating enquiry form submissions, phone call clicks and appointment requests.

Set up your events carefully. A completed contact form submission is a conversion. A page view is not. Many practices measure the wrong things and draw incorrect conclusions about their campaigns as a result.

Why Working With a Specialist Makes a Practical Difference

Managing Meta ads competently takes time that most practice owners simply do not have. Beyond the time commitment, the platform changes regularly. Targeting options shift, creative best practices evolve, and the algorithm rewards those who test and adapt consistently rather than those who set up a campaign and leave it running.

A specialist agency that understands the dental sector brings several practical advantages. They understand GDC and ASA compliance requirements, so your ads do not inadvertently breach guidelines. They have existing data from running campaigns for similar practices, which shortens the testing phase. They manage the technical setup correctly from the start, including Pixel installation, conversion tracking and audience structuring. And they report on the metrics that actually matter to a practice owner, such as cost per booked appointment and return on ad spend, rather than vanity metrics such as impressions and reach.

A Final Word Before You Start

Meta ads are not a magic solution, and any agency or individual that tells you otherwise is not being straight with you. They are a channel that, when managed correctly, can consistently bring new private patients through your door at a predictable cost. When managed poorly, they drain budget and leave practice owners with the mistaken impression that social advertising does not work for dentistry.

The fundamentals covered in this guide, choosing the right objective, targeting the right audience, using compliant creative, sending traffic to the right page and tracking the right outcomes, are the difference between the two results. Getting those fundamentals right takes experience and ongoing attention.
