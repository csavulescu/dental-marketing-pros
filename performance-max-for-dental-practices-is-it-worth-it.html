Performance Max for dental practices: is it worth it?

By Cristian Savulescu, Founder · Published 7 October 2026

Performance Max campaigns promise to do more with less, but for dental practice owners, the reality is more complicated. This article breaks down exactly when PMax helps, when it hurts, and what you should do instead.

Google's Performance Max campaigns have been marketed as the future of paid search: one campaign type, all placements, automated bidding, minimal management overhead. For time-pressed dental practice owners, that pitch is understandably attractive. Hand it to Google, let the algorithm do the work, and watch the bookings roll in. If only it were that straightforward. In practice, many dental practices that have adopted PMax without proper configuration have found their budgets spread thinly across placements that produce no appointments at all. This article explains what Performance Max actually does, where it works against dentists, and how to use it intelligently, or decide it is not the right tool for your practice.

What Performance Max actually is

Performance Max, often shortened to PMax, is a Google Ads campaign type that runs across every Google channel simultaneously: Search, Display, YouTube, Gmail, Maps, and Discover. Rather than you choosing keywords and placements, Google's machine learning decides where and when to show your ads based on the conversion signals you feed it.

In theory, this gives smaller advertisers access to the full breadth of Google's inventory with one campaign. In practice, it shifts a significant amount of control away from the advertiser and towards Google's automated systems. For industries where buying intent is highly specific and the cost of an irrelevant click is high, that loss of control matters enormously.

Dental advertising sits firmly in that category. A patient searching for "emergency dentist Sheffield" has immediate, high-value intent. A patient who sees a banner ad for your practice while watching a YouTube video has none. Performance Max, by default, is perfectly happy to spend your budget on the second scenario.

The core problem: dental intent is very specific

Most of the value in dental paid advertising comes from capturing patients at the exact moment they are looking for a specific treatment or facing a dental problem. That precision is the reason Google Search campaigns, when set up correctly, can deliver a strong return for dental practices. You are matching your message to a declared need.

Performance Max dilutes that precision. Because the campaign runs across all channels, Google will often favour Display and YouTube placements early in a campaign's life because they are cheaper per impression and the algorithm mistakes impression volume for good performance data. The result is that a meaningful portion of your budget can be absorbed by placements where you are interrupting someone who was not looking for a dentist at all.

For a deeper look at which search queries actually deliver value in dental advertising, the article on Dental Google Ads keywords: which searches are worth bidding on covers the intent landscape in detail. The principles there become even more relevant when you are deciding how much trust to place in an algorithm that cannot replicate that level of human keyword judgement.

Why Google pushes Performance Max so hard

It is worth being honest about this. Google has a commercial incentive to encourage adoption of Performance Max. It increases inventory utilisation across channels that are harder to sell individually, and it simplifies campaign management in ways that tend to increase total spend. Google's automated bidding systems also require high conversion volumes to optimise properly, which means they work better for high-volume advertisers than for a dental practice running a modest monthly budget.

None of this makes PMax a bad product in every context. But it does mean that Google's recommendation to switch to PMax is not a neutral piece of advice. A dental practice with a limited budget and a specific geographic catchment area is not the ideal use case the product was designed around.

When Performance Max can work for dental practices

There are situations where PMax genuinely earns its place in a dental advertising account. Understanding them helps you make a grounded decision rather than adopting or rejecting the campaign type wholesale.

High-volume conversion data

Google's automation needs data to learn. If your account is generating a substantial number of conversions each month, PMax's machine learning has enough signal to optimise sensibly. Practices with well-established Google Ads accounts, strong conversion tracking, and consistent appointment volumes are better placed to benefit than those just starting out.

Brand awareness for new or expanding practices

If you are opening a new site or launching a new service, PMax's multi-channel reach can build local awareness faster than Search alone. The key is treating it as a brand-building exercise with a separate budget, not as a direct response campaign where you are measuring cost per booked appointment.

Remarketing at scale

Performance Max uses your audience signals to target people who have previously visited your website or interacted with your Google Business Profile. If you have meaningful website traffic, PMax can keep your practice visible to warm prospects across multiple touchpoints. This is one of its more defensible use cases in a dental context.

Where Performance Max consistently falls short for dentists

  • Limited keyword-level control: You cannot specify exactly which search terms trigger your ads. Google's system infers intent, but it makes mistakes, particularly with broad dental terms that attract non-patient traffic.
  • Poor transparency: PMax provides less granular reporting than standard Search campaigns. It is harder to identify which placements, audiences, or creative assets are driving results and which are wasting budget.
  • Negative keyword limitations: Adding negative keywords to PMax campaigns is more restricted than in Search campaigns. This makes it harder to filter out searches that will never convert for a dental practice.
  • Geographic imprecision: For practices serving a specific town or postcode area, PMax's lookalike audience modelling can serve ads outside your catchment. Every click from a patient who cannot realistically attend your practice is wasted spend.
  • Budget cannibalisation: When PMax and Search campaigns run simultaneously in the same account, PMax tends to absorb budget from high-intent Search clicks. Google's own documentation acknowledges that PMax takes priority in the auction in most circumstances.

The fix: a structured approach to PMax in a dental account

If you want to test Performance Max without exposing your entire budget to its weaknesses, the following approach reduces risk significantly.

1. Protect your best Search campaigns first

Before introducing PMax, ensure your exact and phrase match Search campaigns for high-intent terms are solid and well-funded. These campaigns should be your primary patient acquisition engine. If you are unsure whether your current Search campaigns are working hard enough, the article Is Google Ads Worth It for Dentists? covers the broader case for paid search and the benchmarks to expect.

2. Use asset groups strategically

PMax relies on asset groups: combinations of headlines, descriptions, images, and videos that Google assembles into ads. Build separate asset groups for each service or audience type rather than using one generic group. A set of assets for Invisalign, another for dental implants, and another for general NHS availability gives the algorithm more relevant material to work with and makes performance data more interpretable.

3. Load strong audience signals

Provide PMax with your best audience data: website visitors, patient email lists (uploaded compliantly), and custom intent audiences built around relevant search terms. The algorithm performs better when it has clear signals about who your ideal patient is rather than starting from scratch.

4. Add negative keywords at the account level

Because PMax offers limited in-campaign negative keyword controls, work with Google support or your agency to add a negative keyword list at the account level. At minimum, exclude generic terms that attract non-patient traffic, competitor brand names you do not want to appear against, and terms irrelevant to your service mix.

5. Run PMax with a separate, capped budget

Treat PMax as a supplementary campaign rather than your main investment. Allocate a fixed budget you are comfortable treating partly as a learning exercise, and monitor placement and search term reports weekly during the first two to three months.

How a specialist dental PPC agency approaches this differently

The honest answer is that most practice owners do not have time to manage the technical detail that good PMax configuration requires. Audience signal uploads, asset group architecture, account-level negative keyword management, and the weekly analysis of placement data all take time and expertise that sits outside running a clinical practice.

A specialist agency working in dental PPC will also bring something Google's automation cannot replicate: an understanding of what patient journeys look like in South Yorkshire and North Derbyshire specifically, which services are price-sensitive and which are driven by urgency, and how seasonality affects search behaviour for different treatments. That local and sector knowledge shapes campaign structure in ways that generic automation cannot.

Our dental PPC service is built around that kind of structured, transparent management. We do not simply hand your budget to an algorithm and wait. We use PMax where it is genuinely appropriate, protect high-intent Search campaigns where they perform better, and report clearly on what your spend is producing.

So, is Performance Max worth it for your dental practice?

The answer depends on where your practice is in its advertising journey. If you are new to Google Ads, starting with a well-structured Search campaign targeting high-intent local terms will almost always outperform a PMax campaign running on limited data. If you have an established account with strong conversion tracking and want to extend your reach, a carefully configured PMax campaign running alongside your Search activity can add value, particularly for remarketing and brand awareness.

What PMax is not is a shortcut. It is not a replacement for thoughtful keyword strategy, and it will not automatically produce the right patients at the right cost without proper setup and ongoing management. The practices that get the most from it are those that go in with clear objectives, clean conversion data, and the discipline to monitor results closely.
