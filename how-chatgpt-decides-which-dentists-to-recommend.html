How ChatGPT decides which dentists to recommend

By Cristian Savulescu, Founder · Published 6 October 2026

AI assistants like ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Overviews are increasingly answering "find a dentist near me" queries directly, without sending patients to search results. This article explains how those recommendations form and what dental practices in South Yorkshire and North Derbyshire can do to improve their chances of being included.

A patient opens ChatGPT, types "recommend a dentist in Sheffield" and receives a confident, neatly formatted answer. Your practice is not in it. A competitor two streets away is. This is no longer a hypothetical scenario. AI assistants are actively answering local service queries, and the practices that appear in those answers tend to share certain characteristics. Understanding what those characteristics are, and how to develop them deliberately, is becoming an important part of dental marketing strategy.

This article explains how tools like ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Gemini, Microsoft Copilot and Perplexity form their recommendations, why most dental practices are currently invisible to them, and what steps can genuinely help change that picture.

Why AI assistants are now part of the patient journey

For years, appearing on the first page of Google was the primary objective of local dental marketing. That remains important, but the landscape has shifted. A growing number of people now use AI chat tools as their first port of call when they have a question, including questions like "where can I find a good NHS dentist in Rotherham" or "which private dentists near me offer Invisalign."

When someone asks an AI assistant for a local recommendation, the system does not simply run a Google search and paste the top result. It draws on a wide range of sources it has already processed: websites, directory listings, review platforms, local business data, news articles, professional association pages and more. It then constructs an answer it judges to be accurate and useful based on what it has found across all of those sources.

The practical implication is straightforward. If your practice has a thin online presence, inconsistent information across directories, very few reviews or a website that communicates little substantive detail, there may simply not be enough reliable data about you for an AI to confidently include you in a recommendation. It is not that the AI dislikes your practice. It is that your practice does not have enough visible, coherent, credible information for the system to work with.

How AI assistants actually form local recommendations

It is worth being honest about the limits of what any marketer can claim here. The exact weighting and ranking logic inside tools like ChatGPT or Google AI Overviews is not publicly documented in detail, and anyone who tells you otherwise is speculating. What we can say with reasonable confidence, based on how these systems are designed and what their developers have described in general terms, is that they tend to favour sources with the following qualities.

Consistent and accurate structured data

AI systems are much better at processing information that is presented consistently and clearly. If your Google Business Profile, NHS choices listing, Denplan directory entry and your own website all agree on your practice name, address, phone number, opening hours and the services you offer, that consistency acts as a signal of reliability. Contradictions, for example a different phone number on Yell than on your website, create ambiguity that can reduce confidence in your data.

Depth and relevance of content on your website

A website that devotes a single paragraph to each treatment is less useful to an AI trying to understand what you offer than one that explains each service in meaningful detail. Content that answers genuine patient questions, describes your approach, lists the locations you serve and explains what makes your practice a reasonable choice gives AI systems more material to work with when forming a recommendation.

It is also worth noting that mobile-first dental websites: why 70% of your traffic decides on a phone are also better indexed by Google, which feeds into how AI Overviews and other tools evaluate your credibility. A slow, poorly structured website is a problem for both traditional SEO and for AI visibility.

Review volume and sentiment

Reviews are among the most human-readable signals available to an AI assistant trying to judge quality. Practices with a meaningful number of recent, specific, positive reviews on Google, Trustpilot and similar platforms give AI tools strong evidence that real patients have had good experiences. The key word is specific: reviews that mention particular treatments, staff members or outcomes are generally more informative than generic five-star ratings with no text.

Third-party mentions and citations

Being mentioned on credible third-party websites, whether that is a local news outlet, a professional association directory, a dental care comparison site or a community organisation, adds to the body of evidence that your practice exists and is worth recommending. These citations do not need to include a link to be useful, though links certainly help from a traditional SEO perspective as well.

Geographic relevance signals

When a patient asks for a dentist "near me" or names a specific town or postcode, AI assistants look for geographic signals that connect your practice to that location. This means your website should mention the specific towns, districts and postcodes you serve, not just your main address. A practice in Barnsley that serves patients from Wakefield, Cudworth and Royston should say so explicitly, in natural, readable content rather than as a keyword-stuffed list.

Why most dental practices are currently underrepresented in AI results

The honest answer is that most dental practices have not yet thought about this at all. The shift towards AI-assisted search is recent enough that many practice owners are still focused entirely on traditional Google rankings, pay-per-click advertising or social media. Those channels still matter. But the practices that begin building AI-readable authority now will be better placed as patient behaviour continues to shift.

Common gaps that make practices harder for AI to recommend include: incomplete or inconsistent Google Business Profiles, websites with minimal written content, no systematic approach to collecting patient reviews, and an absence of any meaningful third-party presence beyond a basic NHS listing. These are all fixable problems, but they require deliberate effort rather than passive hope.

What you can do to improve your practice's AI visibility

The following steps can help improve how AI assistants understand and represent your practice. None of them guarantee a specific outcome, and results will vary depending on your location, competition and starting point. What they do is build a stronger, more coherent online presence that serves you across multiple channels, AI search included.

  • Audit your directory listings. Check every major directory where your practice appears and ensure the information is identical and current. Google Business Profile, NHS Find a Dentist, Bing Places, Yell, Healthgrades and any dental-specific directories are all worth reviewing.
  • Invest in substantive website content. Each core service should have its own page with enough detail to genuinely answer patient questions. Describe the treatment, who it suits, what the process involves and what patients can expect. Mention the specific towns and areas you serve.
  • Build a review acquisition process. Ask patients to leave a review at the right moment, whether that is via a follow-up text, a card at reception or a prompt in your post-appointment communication. Make it easy by providing a direct link. Aim for regular, recent reviews rather than a one-off burst.
  • Seek out legitimate third-party mentions. Sponsor a local event, contribute to a community initiative, join relevant professional directories or reach out to local health journalists. Genuine mentions on credible local websites help build the kind of external evidence that AI systems use to validate a practice's reputation.
  • Use structured data markup on your website. Schema markup for local businesses and healthcare providers helps search engines and AI systems parse your information accurately. This is a technical task best handled by a developer or specialist agency, but it can make a meaningful difference to how your data is read.
  • Keep your Google Business Profile active. Post updates regularly, respond to reviews promptly and professionally, upload current photographs and ensure your services section is fully completed. An active, well-maintained profile is a much richer data source than a neglected one.

The bottom line

AI assistants are not replacing Google. They are adding a new layer to how patients find healthcare providers, and that layer is growing. The practices that will fare best in this environment are not necessarily the ones with the biggest marketing budgets. They are the ones with the most coherent, consistent and credible online presence, practices that have invested in good content, accurate data, genuine reviews and a clear geographic identity.

None of that happens overnight, but every month you delay is a month your competitors have more time to establish themselves in these new channels.
