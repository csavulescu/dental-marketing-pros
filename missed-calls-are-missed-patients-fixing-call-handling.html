Missed calls are missed patients: fixing call handling

By Cristian Savulescu, Founder · Published 23 November 2026

Every unanswered call to your dental practice is a potential patient who will simply ring the next practice on their list. This article shows you exactly where call handling breaks down and what to do about it.

Your marketing is working. Someone searched for a dentist, found your practice, picked up the phone and called. And then nothing happened. The phone rang out, went to a generic voicemail, or was answered by someone too busy to handle it properly. That patient is gone. They did not leave a message. They rang the next practice on the list. This is the reality of dental missed calls for a significant proportion of UK dental practices, and it is quietly undermining every pound you spend on attracting new patients.

This article is about fixing that. Not with gimmicks, but with practical, specific changes you can make to your call handling process to ensure that the patients you work hard to attract actually get through, feel welcomed, and book an appointment.

Why missed calls matter more than most practice owners realise

It is tempting to frame a missed call as a minor inconvenience. In reality, it represents the full cost of acquiring that enquiry: your website spend, your SEO investment, your Google profile optimisation, your paid ads, all wasted on a patient who never got to speak to anyone. Multiply that across a week, and the financial impact becomes significant.

The problem is compounded by patient behaviour. People searching for a dental practice, particularly those who are anxious or in pain, will not persist. They want reassurance quickly. If your phone rings out, they assume you are too busy, too disorganised, or simply not interested. Those impressions are hard to reverse. This is especially relevant if you are trying to attract nervous patients, where the first point of contact sets the tone for the entire relationship. For more on how to build trust with that group from the outset, see our article on designing a dental website for nervous patients.

Where call handling typically breaks down

Before you can fix the problem, you need to understand where it is happening. In most practices, dental missed calls cluster around a handful of predictable failure points.

Peak hours with insufficient cover

The majority of inbound calls to dental practices arrive in a narrow window: Monday to Friday, 8:30am to 10:30am and again at lunchtime. If your reception team is handling check-ins, preparing treatment rooms, or already on another call during these periods, calls go unanswered. Most practices have never actually mapped their call volume against their staff availability. That is usually where the analysis starts.

Inadequate out-of-hours handling

A patient searching online at 7pm on a Tuesday is a real patient with a real need. If they call and reach a voicemail message that says "our opening hours are 8:30 to 5:30, please call back during office hours," many will not call back. They will book somewhere else, or book online with a practice that makes it easy to do so. Your out-of-hours response is not a secondary concern; it is a direct conversion point.

Poor voicemail practice

When patients do leave a voicemail, is it actually listened to, logged, and followed up the same day? In busy practices, voicemails accumulate and get returned late, or not at all. There is also the question of what your voicemail message says. A vague or abrupt recorded message does not reassure an anxious caller or give them confidence that someone will get back to them promptly.

Reception staff without a clear call handling process

Even when calls are answered, they are not always handled effectively. A receptionist who is simultaneously managing a patient at the desk, answering the phone, and trying to access the diary is not in a position to give the caller proper attention. Without a structured call script or framework, calls that could convert to bookings are lost because the patient felt rushed, did not get their question answered, or was not offered the right appointment type.

Auditing your current situation

The first practical step is to get an honest picture of what is happening. Many practice management systems allow you to review call logs if you use a compatible phone system. If yours does not, a simple tally sheet kept at reception for two weeks will reveal a great deal. Record the time of every inbound call, whether it was answered, whether it led to a booking, and if not, why not.

You should also listen to your own voicemail message. Call your practice yourself during and outside of opening hours. Note how long the phone rings before it diverts. Consider what a first-time patient in pain or anxiety would feel when they hear your recorded message. That experience, start to finish, is what you are asking people to trust.

If you use a third-party call tracking number, as many practices working with a marketing agency will, your agency should be able to provide you with call data including missed call rates and peak call times. If they cannot, that is worth raising.

Practical fixes that make a real difference

Cover your peak call windows deliberately

Once you have mapped when most calls arrive, you can roster your team accordingly. This might mean staggering lunch breaks, designating one member of staff as the sole call handler during your busiest hour, or simply ensuring that the person at the front desk is not also the one managing the waiting room at the same time.

Implement an online booking option

Not every patient wants to call. For out-of-hours enquiries and patients who prefer a digital interaction, a bookable appointment widget on your website removes the dependency on the phone entirely. It also captures patients who would otherwise be lost between 5pm and 9am. If your website does not currently offer this, it is a relatively straightforward addition and one that can meaningfully reduce your reliance on call handling alone.

Rewrite your voicemail message

Your voicemail message should do three things: confirm the patient has reached the right practice, tell them specifically when someone will call them back (not just "as soon as possible"), and if relevant, direct emergency cases to an appropriate number or service. A message that says "Thank you for calling Riverside Dental. We are sorry we missed your call. Please leave your name and number and we will call you back before 12pm today" is more reassuring than a generic prompt, and it sets a commitment you then need to deliver on.

Create a call handling framework for your team

Reception staff benefit from a simple, consistent framework for handling new patient enquiries by phone. This does not mean a rigid script that sounds robotic. It means a clear sequence: warmly greet the caller by practice name, ask how you can help, establish whether they are new or existing, identify what they are looking for, and move towards booking. The goal is to make the caller feel heard and to give them a reason to trust your practice before they have met anyone in person.

Follow up missed calls the same day

If a call is missed and no message is left, you still have an opportunity. Call tracking numbers will often show you the caller's number. A brief, non-intrusive return call during the same working day ("Hi, I see we missed your call earlier, is there anything I can help you with?") can recover bookings that would otherwise be lost. Set this as a standard end-of-morning and end-of-afternoon task at reception.

The compliance dimension

When handling call data and patient contact information, UK GDPR obligations apply. Any process that involves logging caller details, storing numbers from missed calls, or following up by phone needs to sit within your practice's data handling policies. For a thorough overview of how to manage patient enquiries compliantly, our article on data, GDPR and patient enquiries: handling leads compliantly covers this in detail.

How call handling fits into your wider patient acquisition strategy

Fixing dental missed calls is not a standalone project. It sits at the end of a chain that includes your visibility online, the quality of your website, your reputation, and how well your marketing attracts the right patients in the first place. A practice that answers every call promptly but has very few calls coming in has a different problem to solve. Equally, a practice generating strong enquiry volume but losing bookings at the call stage is wasting its entire marketing investment.

If you are actively trying to grow your patient numbers, it is worth reading our broader guide on how to get more patients for your dental practice, which addresses the full picture from visibility through to conversion.

It is also worth noting that the way patients find and choose a dental practice is evolving. AI assistants and voice search tools are increasingly part of the journey, and understanding that shift helps you think about where your enquiries are coming from and what patients expect when they make contact. Where a specialist agency adds genuine value

A dental marketing agency that takes call handling seriously will not simply hand you a website and leave you to manage the rest. The best agencies integrate call tracking into your marketing from the outset, so you have real data on how many calls your campaigns generate, how many are answered, and what happens to those that are not. They will flag patterns in your call data, help you identify the right tools for online booking, and ensure that the experience a patient has when they contact your practice matches the impression your marketing has created.
