Pricing pages for dental treatments: should you show prices?

Showing prices on your dental website is one of the most debated decisions a practice owner faces. This article breaks down the real arguments on both sides and gives you a practical framework for getting it right.

At some point, almost every dental practice owner asks the same question: should we publish our prices online? Some practices list every fee in detail. Others hide everything behind a "call us for a quote" message. Most land somewhere in the middle without any clear strategy behind the decision. The result is a dental treatment pricing page, or the absence of one, that either puts patients off before they enquire or attracts the wrong type of enquiry altogether. Neither outcome is good for your practice. This article sets out the honest arguments on both sides, explains why the default approach tends to underperform, and gives you a clear framework for making an informed decision.

Why this question matters more than most practice owners realise

Your website is usually the first place a prospective patient forms a serious impression of your practice. They arrive with a specific problem in mind, whether that is a missing tooth, discoloured enamel, or a child who needs an orthodontic assessment. Before they book, the majority of people want to understand whether your practice is even within reach financially. If your site does not answer that question, many will simply move on to a competitor that does.

This is not a minor UX detail. Pricing transparency, or the lack of it, directly affects the quality and volume of your enquiries. Practices that get this wrong in either direction tend to notice it: either the phone rings with people who argue about fees the moment they arrive, or it does not ring at all.

There is also a compliance dimension. The Advertising Standards Authority and the General Dental Council both expect dental marketing to be honest and not misleading. Vague phrases such as "affordable treatment" or "competitive prices" without any supporting information can create consumer expectations you cannot meet. Getting your pricing communication right is therefore not just a commercial decision, it is part of your regulatory responsibility.

The case for showing prices

Transparency builds trust, and trust converts visitors into patients. When a prospective patient can see that a composite veneer starts from a clearly stated figure, they feel they are dealing with a practice that has nothing to hide. That feeling of confidence tends to carry through to the initial consultation and beyond.

Showing prices also pre-qualifies your enquiries. If someone contacts you having already seen your fee structure and still wants to book, there is far less chance of a wasted consultation where the patient balks at the cost. Your reception team spends less time managing expectation gaps, and your dentists see patients who are genuinely ready to proceed.

From a search engine visibility standpoint, a well-structured dental treatment pricing page can also help your website rank for searches that include cost-related terms. People searching "how much does Invisalign cost in Sheffield" or "dental implant price Chesterfield" are often closer to a decision than people searching more generic terms. Capturing that intent matters.

The case against showing prices (and whether it holds up)

The most common objection from practice owners is that every case is different. A full-mouth implant restoration cannot be accurately priced without clinical assessment. That is entirely true, and no one is suggesting you publish a fixed fee for something that genuinely cannot be fixed. The nuance here is important.

A second concern is that competitors will see your prices and undercut you. This is a real risk in some markets, but it tends to be overstated. Patients do not choose a dental practice on price alone. Proximity, reputation, the feel of the website, reviews, and clinical expertise all play a role. Being cheaper than you is not straightforwardly a disadvantage for your practice if your other signals are strong.

A third concern is that listed prices can feel like a commitment. If your lab costs change or you upgrade equipment, updating a published price list takes time. This is a valid operational point, but it is a process problem, not a reason to avoid transparency altogether.

The fix: a tiered approach to pricing transparency

The most effective dental treatment pricing pages do not attempt to answer every possible question with a single number. Instead, they use a structured, tiered approach that gives visitors enough information to self-qualify, while setting honest expectations about how final fees are determined.

Tier one: straightforward treatments with predictable fees

For treatments with a consistently predictable cost, such as a standard examination, hygiene appointment, a single tooth extraction, or a simple composite filling, publish a clear price. Use language such as "from" only when there is a genuine range; do not use it simply to hedge or avoid commitment where none is warranted. Patients appreciate directness.

Tier two: treatments with a genuine range

For treatments such as tooth whitening, composite bonding, or clear aligner orthodontics, explain what determines the final cost. For example, you might note that whitening fees depend on whether the patient chooses home, in-surgery, or combined treatment, and give a starting price for each option. This approach is informative without being misleading.

Tier three: complex or bespoke treatments

For full-arch implants, full smile makeovers, or complex restorative cases, it is entirely appropriate to state that a personalised treatment plan and fee estimate is provided following a consultation. What matters here is that you explain why this is the case. Telling a patient that "every case is assessed individually to ensure you receive the right treatment for your circumstances" is more reassuring than a bare "call us for a quote."

What every pricing page should also include

  • Finance options. If you offer payment plans, say so on the pricing page itself. A patient who sees a fee they cannot afford upfront will leave without enquiring unless they know there is an alternative.
  • What the fee includes. Patients often do not know whether a quoted price covers follow-up appointments, X-rays, or aftercare. Spelling this out reduces post-treatment disputes and builds confidence before the patient even arrives.
  • A clear next step. Every pricing page should make it easy to book a consultation or ask a question. A well-placed link or button removes the final barrier.

Common mistakes to avoid

Even practices that have decided to publish prices often make avoidable errors that undermine the effort.

Burying prices in a PDF. A downloadable fee guide has its uses, but it should never replace visible pricing on your web page. PDFs are not easily indexed by search engines, and many users will not bother downloading them.

Using jargon without explanation. Writing "composite resin direct restoration from £X" is not helpful to a patient who simply knows their tooth has a chip. Use plain language alongside clinical terminology where needed.

Setting prices and forgetting them. A pricing page that has not been reviewed in two or three years creates a compliance risk and damages trust the moment a patient is quoted a different figure at reception. Build a review process into your practice calendar.

Ignoring mobile presentation. A large percentage of patients browsing for dental treatment are doing so on a smartphone. A pricing page that presents clearly on desktop but becomes a wall of text or a broken table on mobile is losing you enquiries. This is closely tied to how your dental website is designed and maintained as a whole.

How a specialist dental marketing agency approaches this differently

Most generalist web designers and marketing agencies treat a pricing page as a simple content task. They add a table, populate it with whatever the client sends over, and move on. A specialist dental marketing agency approaches it as part of a broader conversion strategy.

That means thinking carefully about which search terms potential patients in your area are using, structuring the page so that it captures that intent, and ensuring that the way pricing is presented aligns with how your reception team handles initial enquiries. It also means building the page within a well-structured dental website that supports the patient journey from first click to booked appointment, rather than treating each page as a standalone item.

Beyond the page itself, a specialist agency will consider how your pricing content interacts with your broader online presence: your Google Business Profile, your paid search campaigns if you run them, and the way your reviews and reputation reinforce the value behind your fees. Pricing transparency works best when it sits within a coherent, well-managed digital presence.

Making the decision for your practice

There is no single right answer to whether you should show prices, but there is a right process for deciding. Start by asking what your current pricing page, or absence of one, is actually communicating to patients who arrive on your site. Then ask whether the enquiries you receive reflect the patients you are trying to attract. If there is a mismatch, your pricing communication is likely part of the problem.

For most practices in competitive markets across South Yorkshire and North Derbyshire, some level of pricing transparency will improve the quality of enquiries and reduce friction in the patient journey. The goal is not to publish a complete fee menu regardless of how your treatments are priced, but to give prospective patients enough information to feel confident taking the next step.
