The best social media platforms for UK dental practices ranked

By Cristian Savulescu, Founder · Published 8 September 2026

Not every social media platform deserves your time as a dental practice owner. This guide ranks the best social media platforms for dentists so you can focus on the ones that actually bring new patients through the door.

Most dental practices in the UK are active on social media. Fewer are getting anything meaningful from it. If you are posting regularly but seeing little in the way of new enquiries, you are not alone, and the problem is rarely effort. It is almost always platform choice and execution. This guide ranks the best social media platforms for dentists operating in the UK, explains why certain platforms consistently outperform others for dental practices, and shows you where a specialist agency can make a genuine difference to your return on time and budget.

Why platform choice matters more than posting frequency

There is a widespread assumption in small business marketing that more presence equals more results. Post on every platform, stay consistent, and the leads will come. In practice, spreading your content across five or six channels with no clear strategy produces thin, low-quality output on all of them and meaningful results on none.

Dental marketing sits in a specific niche. You are targeting adults, typically within a defined geographic radius, who are either actively looking for a new practice or who need nudging towards a treatment they have been putting off. The platform you choose needs to match that intent. Social media platforms differ enormously in their audience demographics, ad targeting capabilities, content formats, and how users behave when they are on them. Choosing the right ones for your practice is a strategic decision, not a creative one.

The ranked list: best social media platforms for dentists in the UK

1. Facebook: still the strongest all-round platform for most practices

Facebook remains the most effective paid social channel for the overwhelming majority of UK dental practices, particularly those targeting patients aged 30 and above. Its advertising infrastructure is sophisticated: you can target by postcode radius, age, household income indicators, and interest signals. For practices promoting Invisalign, dental implants, or composite bonding, this level of targeting makes it possible to put your message in front of the right local audience without significant waste.

Organic reach on Facebook has declined over the years, which is worth acknowledging honestly. A Facebook page alone, without a paid strategy behind it, will reach a very small proportion of your followers. But as a paid platform, it remains outstanding value for dental practices when campaigns are structured correctly, with the right creative, the right audience parameters, and proper conversion tracking in place.

Facebook is also the home of local community groups, which offer a genuine organic opportunity. Practices that participate helpfully in local groups, rather than simply advertising in them, build brand familiarity that pays off over time.

2. Instagram: essential for cosmetic and aesthetic treatments

Instagram is visually led, and dentistry is a visually demonstrable service. Before-and-after photography for cosmetic treatments, short video walkthroughs of your practice, and short-form Reels showing treatment processes all perform well here. The platform skews younger than Facebook, with strong engagement from users in their mid-twenties to early forties, which makes it particularly relevant for practices offering teeth whitening, veneers, clear aligners, and composite work.

A critical note on compliance: the GDC and ASA have clear expectations around before-and-after imagery in dental advertising. Any images used must be genuine, unmanipulated representations of actual patient outcomes, and must not mislead prospective patients about likely results. This is not an area where artistic licence applies. Before using before-and-after content on Instagram or anywhere else, ensure it meets both your professional obligations and the CAP Code standards for non-broadcast advertising.

Instagram works best in tandem with Facebook, since both are managed through the same Meta Ads platform. Running coordinated campaigns across both channels with appropriate creative for each format is generally more efficient than managing them separately.

3. Google Business Profile: not strictly social media, but too important to omit

Strictly speaking, Google Business Profile is not a social media platform. But in any honest ranking of where dental practices should focus their digital presence, it belongs near the top. When someone searches for a dentist in your area, your Google Business Profile is often the first thing they see. The posts feature, Q and A section, and review management capabilities give it genuine social characteristics, and the intent of the user finding you through Google is far higher than the intent of someone scrolling past a Facebook ad.

Keeping your profile fully populated, responding to reviews professionally, and posting updates regularly all contribute to local search visibility. This connects directly to your broader SEO strategy, which is something our full range of dental marketing services covers in detail.

4. TikTok: high potential, high resource demand, proceed with caution

TikTok has a large and growing UK user base, and some dental practices have achieved impressive organic reach through short-form educational video content. Explainer videos about how Invisalign works, what a hygiene appointment involves, or what to do in a dental emergency can genuinely perform well if executed consistently and with good production quality.

The caution is this: TikTok rewards volume and consistency. Practices that try it sporadically rarely see results. The platform also skews towards a younger demographic, which may or may not align with your patient acquisition goals. If you have capacity to produce regular short video content and your target patient profile includes people under 35, TikTok is worth exploring. If resources are limited, it should not take priority over Facebook and Instagram.

5. LinkedIn: useful, but limited for most practices

LinkedIn has a role for dental practices in certain specific contexts. If you are looking to recruit associates or dental hygienists, LinkedIn is a sensible channel. If your practice offers corporate dental plans and you want to reach HR managers and business owners, it has some targeting utility. For general patient acquisition, however, it is a poor fit. The platform is professional in context and high in cost-per-click on the paid side. For most independent practices, it belongs low on the priority list.

6. X (formerly Twitter): largely not worth the time for most dental practices

X has undergone significant changes since its rebrand and has seen shifts in its user base and advertiser confidence. For dental practices targeting local patients, the targeting options are limited and the organic reach for local service businesses is poor. There may be a marginal reputational benefit to having a maintained presence, but active investment of time or budget here is difficult to justify for most practices.

Why many practices struggle to get results from social media

Even when practices choose the right platforms, several recurring issues hold them back. Content is produced inconsistently when time allows, rather than as part of a planned schedule. Paid campaigns are set up without proper conversion tracking, so it is impossible to know which ads are generating enquiries. Creative does not speak to specific patient concerns, it speaks to the practice. Targeting is too broad, reaching people 30 miles away who will never travel to your surgery.

These are not failings of effort. They are the natural result of trying to run a clinical business and a marketing operation simultaneously. Social media for a dental practice, done properly, requires strategic input, regular creative production, campaign management, compliance awareness, and performance analysis. That is a significant workload alongside the day-to-day demands of running a practice.

The practical starting point: where to focus first

If you are a practice owner looking to make better use of social media without spreading yourself thin, here is the straightforward answer. Start with Facebook and Instagram, managed together through Meta. Ensure your Google Business Profile is complete and actively maintained. Produce content that addresses real patient questions and concerns, rather than content that simply promotes your practice. If you run paid campaigns, track them properly from click to booked appointment.

If you are not sure where your current social media efforts stand or whether your budget is being used effectively, a conversation with a specialist is a sensible next step. There is no obligation involved, just a clearer picture of what is working and what is not.
