How to check what AI says about your dental practice

By Cristian Savulescu, Founder · Published 3 November 2026

AI assistants are now answering patients' questions about local dental practices, often without those practices knowing what is being said. This guide explains how to check what AI tools say about your practice and how to improve your chances of being represented accurately.

Patients are increasingly turning to AI assistants to answer questions before they ever visit a search results page. They ask ChatGPT which dentists near them accept NHS patients. They ask Google's AI Overview whether a particular practice offers Invisalign. They ask Perplexity to compare private dental practices in their town. And the answers those tools give can influence decisions in ways that a simple directory listing never could. The problem is that most practice owners have no idea what these tools are saying about them, and have never thought to check. This article explains how to check AI mentions of your dental practice systematically, what to look for, why inaccuracies appear, and what you can do to improve how your practice is represented.

Why AI assistants matter to your practice now

It is tempting to treat AI search as a future concern. It is not. Google AI Overviews are already appearing at the top of results pages for a wide range of health and dental queries in the UK. Tools such as ChatGPT, Microsoft Copilot, Gemini and Perplexity are being used daily by people who want a direct, conversational answer rather than a list of blue links to sift through.

These tools do not work like traditional search engines. They synthesise information from multiple sources, including your website, Google Business Profile, review platforms, online directories, local news mentions and third-party dental directories. They then generate a response that may or may not reflect your practice accurately. If the sources those tools draw on contain outdated, inconsistent or simply absent information about your practice, the answer a patient receives could be wrong. It might name a competitor instead of you, list services you no longer offer, or simply leave you out entirely.

This is not a technical curiosity. It is a practical reputation issue that warrants the same attention you would give to a negative Google review.

How to check what AI tools are currently saying about your practice

Start with direct queries across multiple platforms

The simplest starting point costs nothing and takes around thirty minutes. Open each of the following tools and run a series of test queries as if you were a prospective patient in your area.

  • ChatGPT (chat.openai.com): use both the free and, if accessible, the paid version with browsing enabled, as they draw on different data.
  • Google AI Overviews: search directly in Google for queries such as "best dentist in [your town]", "dentist accepting new NHS patients [your area]" and "[your practice name] reviews".
  • Microsoft Copilot (copilot.microsoft.com): particularly useful because it cites its sources, which tells you exactly where the information is coming from.
  • Perplexity (perplexity.ai): also cites sources and is increasingly used for research-style queries.
  • Gemini (gemini.google.com): Google's own assistant, which tends to draw heavily on Google's index and Business Profile data.

For each tool, test at least three types of query. First, search for your practice by name and see whether the description, address, services and contact details are accurate. Second, search for your services in your location without naming your practice ("orthodontist Sheffield" or "dental implants Chesterfield") and note whether you appear in the response at all. Third, ask a broader question a patient might genuinely ask, such as "which dental practices in [your town] offer payment plans for private treatment?"

Record what you find

Keep a simple log. Note the query, the tool, whether your practice was mentioned, what was said, and whether any of the information was inaccurate or outdated. Common errors include old phone numbers, incorrect opening hours, services listed that you no longer provide, and missing services that are now central to your offer. Also note whether competitors are being recommended in contexts where you should reasonably appear.

Check your source materials

When AI tools generate responses about local businesses, they draw on sources that are publicly visible and crawlable. If you find errors, the cause is almost always traceable to one of the following.

  • Your Google Business Profile contains outdated or incomplete information.
  • Your website lacks clear, structured content about the services you offer and the areas you serve.
  • Your practice is listed inconsistently across directories such as Yell, Yelp, NHS.uk, Dentaly or Find-a-Dentist, with variations in your practice name, address or phone number.
  • There is little or no third-party content about your practice, such as reviews, local press mentions or professional profile pages, for AI tools to draw on.

Why inaccuracies appear and why they persist

AI language models are trained on data gathered up to a particular point in time, and even tools with live browsing capabilities do not always revisit every source frequently. If you changed your phone number two years ago but your old number persists on a handful of directory listings, an AI tool summarising information about your practice may still present the old number with confidence.

There is also a compounding effect. If several lower-authority sources repeat the same incorrect information, AI tools may treat that as corroboration rather than error. This is why citation analysis matters. When Copilot or Perplexity shows you which URLs it drew on to generate a response, those are the exact sources you should audit and, where necessary, correct.

It is worth noting that no marketing agency, including us, has insider knowledge of the specific ranking or citation factors used by ChatGPT, Gemini or any other AI platform. These systems are not transparent in the way that traditional search engine guidelines are. What we do know, from practical experience and from what AI developers have made publicly available, is that consistent, accurate, structured and authoritative information across your digital presence can help improve how your practice is represented. It is not a guarantee of citation, but it reduces the likelihood of errors and increases the relevance of what these tools find.

What you can do to improve your AI presence

Audit and update your Google Business Profile

Your Google Business Profile is one of the most heavily referenced sources for local business information across AI tools, particularly Google AI Overviews and Gemini. Ensure your practice name, address, phone number, website URL, opening hours and service categories are all accurate and complete. Add a detailed practice description that uses natural language to describe what you offer and who you serve. Upload recent photographs. Respond to reviews. These are not glamorous tasks, but they are foundational.

Create structured, specific content on your website

AI tools can extract information more reliably from pages that are clearly structured and specific. A single generic "treatments" page is less useful to an AI assistant than individual pages for implants, Invisalign, teeth whitening and so on, each with clear descriptions of the treatment, who it suits and how patients can enquire. Location-specific content also helps, particularly if your practice draws patients from a wider catchment area. Pages or sections that clearly state which towns and postcodes you serve give AI tools more to work with when generating local responses.

This type of structured local content sits at the heart of what is sometimes called Generative Engine Optimisation (GEO). You can read more about how we approach this for dental practices on our GEO and AI search optimisation page.

Standardise your directory listings

Consistency matters. Your practice name, address and phone number should be identical across every directory listing, social media profile and professional listing site. Even minor variations, such as "St." versus "Street" or an old mobile number on one listing, can introduce confusion into the data AI tools use. Work through your key listings methodically and bring them into alignment.

Encourage and respond to reviews

Reviews on Google, NHS.uk and platforms such as Trustpilot are among the third-party signals that AI tools can draw on when forming a picture of your practice. A practice with a strong, recent review profile that reflects the services it offers is more likely to be represented positively than one with sparse or outdated reviews. Encouraging satisfied patients to leave a review is not only good for traditional search visibility but increasingly relevant to AI-generated responses.

A word of caution here: if you are considering how to encourage reviews or how to describe your services in any patient-facing context, it is worth familiarising yourself with GDC and ASA compliant dental advertising guidelines. The rules around what you can and cannot say apply whether the content appears on your website, in a directory listing or in any other publicly visible context.

How a specialist agency approaches this differently

Doing all of this yourself is genuinely possible. The challenge is that it is time-consuming, requires ongoing attention rather than a one-off fix, and the landscape is changing quickly. What AI tools prioritise and how they source information is evolving, and keeping pace with that alongside running a busy practice is a significant ask.

A specialist dental marketing agency brings a structured approach to auditing your current AI presence, identifying the specific sources where errors or gaps exist, and making targeted improvements across your website, directory profile and content. Crucially, this work is integrated rather than siloed. Improvements to your structured content, your local SEO signals and your review profile all reinforce each other, and they support both traditional search rankings and AI-generated responses simultaneously.

If you want a clear picture of where your practice currently stands before committing to any particular course of action, a free SEO and visibility audit can be a useful first step. It gives you an objective baseline rather than a sales pitch.

A practical starting point

If you take one thing from this article, let it be this: open ChatGPT or Perplexity today and search for your practice name. Then search for your main service in your town. Read what comes back. If what you find is accurate, complete and positions your practice well, that is a strong foundation to build on. If it is wrong, incomplete or does not mention you at all, you now know where to start.

Managing how AI tools represent your practice is not about gaming a system. It is about ensuring that the information patients encounter about you is accurate, consistent and reflective of the practice you have worked to build. That is a reasonable expectation, and it is increasingly achievable with the right approach.
