What a £50 a month AI website includes for a dental practice

By Cristian Savulescu, Founder · Published 5 October 2026

A £50 a month AI website sounds like an easy win for a busy dental practice, but the reality is more nuanced. This article breaks down exactly what you get, what you do not, and how to decide whether it is the right fit for your practice.

Every few weeks, another platform promises to build your dental practice a professional website for roughly the price of a takeaway for two. AI website builders have made this feel entirely plausible, and for a practice owner juggling clinical work, staff management and compliance, the appeal is obvious. Before you commit, though, it is worth understanding precisely what that monthly fee covers, where the gaps tend to appear, and at what point a specialist dental marketing agency adds value that a template tool simply cannot replicate.

What "AI website" actually means in this context

The phrase AI website is used loosely across the industry. In most cases it refers to a website builder that uses artificial intelligence to generate initial copy, suggest layouts, and populate pages based on a short questionnaire you complete at sign-up. Platforms such as Wix ADI, Squarespace AI, Jimdo and a growing number of newer entrants all work along these lines.

The AI element handles the first draft quickly. You answer questions about your practice name, location, the treatments you offer, and your preferred visual style. Within minutes you have a working site. At the entry-level price point, typically around £50 per month, that is genuinely what you are paying for: speed and convenience.

What is usually included at the £50 price point

Hosting and a basic domain

Most plans at this level include shared hosting and either a free subdomain (yourpractice.wixsite.com) or a discounted first-year custom domain. Hosting performance is adequate for low-traffic sites, though shared environments can be slower than managed hosting during peak periods.

Template-driven design

You will have access to a library of templates, some of which are positioned as suitable for healthcare or professional services. The AI may rearrange colours and fonts to match your brand, but the underlying structure is still a template that thousands of other businesses are using simultaneously.

AI-generated page copy

This is where practices often feel the most initial excitement, and later the most disappointment. The AI will produce serviceable placeholder copy for a homepage, an about page, and perhaps a handful of treatment pages. The copy is grammatically sound and inoffensive, but it is rarely specific to your practice, your patient demographic, your fee structure, or your clinical ethos. It is, by definition, generic.

Basic contact and booking tools

A contact form, a clickable phone number, and sometimes a basic appointment request widget are typically included. Integration with more sophisticated practice management software usually sits behind a higher-tier plan or requires a paid third-party plugin.

SSL certificate and mobile responsiveness

These are standard across virtually all modern builders regardless of price. An SSL certificate is the minimum required for patient trust and for browsers to display your site without a security warning. Mobile responsiveness means the layout adjusts for phones and tablets, which is essential given that the majority of dental searches now happen on mobile devices.

What is usually not included at the £50 price point

Genuine local SEO

This is the most significant gap. An AI website builder will add your location to the page and perhaps generate a Google Maps embed, but it will not conduct keyword research specific to South Yorkshire or North Derbyshire search behaviour, structure your service pages around the terms your prospective patients actually type, or build the internal linking architecture that helps search engines understand what your practice specialises in. For context on how this structure matters, our guide on dental website navigation: structuring for treatments and locations explains why page hierarchy has a direct impact on how easily new patients find you.

GDC and ASA-compliant copy review

AI-generated copy has no awareness of the Advertising Standards Authority's rules on healthcare claims or the General Dental Council's guidance on patient communications. Phrases that sound natural in a generic context can breach advertising standards in a dental one. A platform charging £50 a month has no mechanism to flag this. Compliance review requires human expertise, and the absence of it is a genuine regulatory risk.

Conversion-focused design decisions

There is an important difference between a website that looks reasonable and a website that turns visitors into booked appointments. Conversion-focused design considers where nervous patients drop off, how trust signals should be ordered, what a first-time visitor needs to see before they pick up the phone, and how the journey differs for a patient searching for Invisalign versus one searching for emergency dental treatment. Our article on designing a dental website for nervous patients covers this in detail, and almost none of it is addressed by a template-driven AI builder.

Accessibility compliance

The Public Sector Bodies Accessibility Regulations and the broader requirements of the Equality Act 2010 mean that accessibility is not optional for dental practices. AI builders vary considerably in how well their output meets WCAG 2.1 standards. Many produce sites that fail on colour contrast, keyboard navigation or screen reader compatibility. This is both a legal risk and a missed commercial opportunity, since accessible websites serve a wider patient base. If this is new territory for you, our piece on accessibility on dental websites: compliance and more patients sets out what good looks like in practical terms.

Ongoing optimisation

A £50 a month plan does not include anyone monitoring your traffic, identifying pages that are underperforming, updating treatment information as your services evolve, or adjusting your site in response to algorithm changes. The site is largely static once published unless you invest time in maintaining it yourself.

Why practices end up with an AI website and then outgrow it

The pattern is a familiar one. A practice uses an AI builder to establish an online presence quickly, often when opening a new site or after a rebrand. In the early months it feels adequate. Over time, as the practice grows and competition in the local area increases, the site's limitations become more apparent. Organic search traffic plateaus. New treatment lines are not properly represented. Patients who were referred by word of mouth find the site fine, but the practice is not winning new patients from search at the rate it needs.

The cost of switching later, including rebuilding content, redirecting URLs correctly, and re-establishing any domain authority the old site had accumulated, is often greater than it would have been to build properly from the outset. This is not an argument against ever using an AI builder. For a practice that genuinely needs a placeholder site while a proper build is in progress, or for a very small single-chair practice with modest growth ambitions, it can be entirely appropriate. The problem arises when the £50 site becomes the permanent solution by default rather than by design.

What a specialist dental marketing agency includes instead

A specialist agency builds a dental website as part of a broader marketing system rather than as a standalone product. That means the design, copy, structure and technical setup all work together toward a specific outcome: more of the right patients booking the treatments that matter most to your practice.

In practical terms, this includes keyword research grounded in how patients in your specific area search; treatment pages written by humans who understand both dental terminology and the concerns of the patient reading the page; an information architecture that makes it easy for search engines and patients alike to navigate; and compliance review of all copy before anything goes live.

It also means the site is built to grow. If you add implants to your treatment list, or open a second location, or want to run a campaign around clear aligners, the site can accommodate that without starting from scratch. Our 12-month dental marketing growth plan outlines how a properly built website fits into a longer-term strategy, and why the decisions made at the build stage affect what is possible in months six through twelve and beyond.

How to decide what is right for your practice

Ask yourself three questions before choosing a path.

  • Is this site your primary route to new patients, or a supporting presence? If the majority of your new patients currently come through referrals and you simply need a credible online address, a well-maintained AI builder may be sufficient for now.
  • Are you trying to grow specific treatment lines? If you want to attract more implant, orthodontic or cosmetic patients, you need content depth and SEO structure that no £50 template can deliver.
  • Do you have time to manage and update the site yourself? If not, you need either a retainer arrangement with an agency or a very clear plan for who keeps the site current.

There is no single right answer, but there is usually a clearer answer once you map your growth ambitions against what a given tool can realistically support.
