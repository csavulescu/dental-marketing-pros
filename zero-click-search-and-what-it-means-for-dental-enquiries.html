Zero-click search and what it means for dental enquiries

By Cristian Savulescu, Founder · Published 30 October 2026

Zero-click search is reshaping how patients discover dental practices online, often without ever visiting your website. This article explains what it means for your enquiry pipeline and what you can do about it.

There is a growing problem that a significant number of dental practice owners have not yet noticed. Their website might be ranking reasonably well on Google. Their SEO reports might look healthy. But their enquiry volumes are quietly falling. One of the most likely culprits is zero-click search, and it is reshaping the way patients find, evaluate and choose a dental practice, often without ever landing on your website at all.

What zero-click search actually means

A zero-click search occurs when someone types a query into a search engine and gets the answer they need directly on the results page, without clicking through to any website. This has been happening in small ways for years, through featured snippets, knowledge panels and map packs. But the rise of AI-generated answers, including Google's AI Overviews, Microsoft Copilot, Perplexity and other generative tools, has accelerated the trend considerably.

For dental practices, this matters in very practical terms. A patient searches for "NHS dentist accepting new patients in Sheffield" or "how much does Invisalign cost in the UK" and receives a synthesised answer at the top of the page. They may read it, feel informed and then either call the first practice mentioned in that answer, use the map pack below it, or simply move on. Your website, even if it ranks on page one, may never receive that visit at all.

If you want to understand how these AI systems work in more depth before reading further, our AI search glossary for dental practice owners covers the key terms clearly and without jargon.

Why zero-click search is increasing for dental queries

Search engines are increasingly designed to answer questions rather than just list sources. Google's stated goal has long been to give users the most helpful experience possible, and for many informational queries, that now means surfacing an AI-generated summary before any organic results appear.

Dental searches are particularly vulnerable to this shift because so many of them are informational in nature. Questions about treatment costs, NHS versus private options, recovery times, what to expect at a first appointment, and which practice offers a particular treatment are all the kind of queries that AI Overviews handle directly. Patients seeking this information may never need to click anywhere.

Even more commercially focused queries, such as "best dental implant practice near me," are increasingly answered through a combination of the local map pack, AI-curated summaries and review aggregates, all visible without a click. The upshot is that organic website traffic for many dental practices is under quiet but real pressure.

For a broader view of where AI search is headed, it is worth reading our article on whether AI search will replace Google for finding a dentist. The short answer is that it probably will not replace it entirely, but it is already changing it significantly.

The specific impact on dental enquiries

The practical consequences for your practice depend on which types of searches are driving your enquiries. If your new patient flow has historically come from informational content, such as blog posts explaining treatments or guides to NHS registration, those pages are at greater risk of being bypassed by AI-generated answers.

If your enquiries come primarily from local, intent-driven searches, such as patients looking for a practice near them right now, the picture is slightly different. Local searches still rely heavily on Google Business Profiles and map results, which remain relatively click-dependent. But even here, AI systems are beginning to surface specific practices by name in conversational responses, and the practices that appear tend to be those with strong, well-structured online presences.

The key point is this: zero-click search does not mean your digital presence matters less. It means it needs to work differently. Instead of just driving clicks to your website, your content and your online profile need to be the source that AI systems draw from when they construct their answers.

What you can do to stay visible in a zero-click environment

Optimise your Google Business Profile properly

Your Google Business Profile remains one of the most influential signals for local dental search. Practices that keep their profile accurate, complete and regularly updated are more likely to appear in map packs and, increasingly, in AI-generated local recommendations. This means verifying all contact details, adding services clearly, responding to reviews consistently and using the posts feature to share relevant updates.

Structure your content so AI systems can use it

AI Overviews and tools like Perplexity and Copilot draw on publicly available web content to construct their answers. They tend to favour content that is well-structured, clearly authoritative and directly addresses specific questions. This means your website content should use clear headings, answer common patient questions in plain language, and demonstrate expertise without being evasive or overly salesy.

Schema markup, which is a form of structured data added to your website's code, can also help search engines understand what your practice offers, where you are located and what patients say about you. This is technical work, but it can make your information more legible to AI systems.

Build authority through consistent, accurate information

AI systems can only use what they can find and verify. If your practice name, address and phone number appear differently across different directories and platforms, that inconsistency creates doubt. Ensuring your details are consistent across your website, Google Business Profile, NHS finder listings, Trustpilot, Yelp and any other directories you appear on is foundational work that pays dividends in how confidently AI tools reference your practice.

Encourage and manage patient reviews

Reviews are increasingly used by AI systems as a proxy for trustworthiness and quality. A practice with a strong volume of recent, detailed and positive reviews is more likely to be surfaced in AI-generated recommendations than one with few or outdated reviews. The GDC's guidance on patient testimonials applies here, so any review process you put in place must be compliant and free of incentivisation, but building a legitimate process for encouraging satisfied patients to leave feedback is a sound investment.

Develop content that earns citations

One of the ways AI systems identify authoritative sources is through citations, that is, other credible websites linking to or referencing your content. If your practice publishes genuinely useful material, whether that is an honest guide to implant costs in the UK, an explanation of what to expect during orthodontic treatment, or a clear overview of your NHS registration process, other sites and directories may reference it. This builds the kind of authority that makes AI tools more likely to draw on your content.

Our AI Overviews explained article goes into more detail on how Google's AI system specifically selects and surfaces content, which is a useful reference as you think through your content strategy.

How a specialist dental marketing agency approaches this differently

Most general SEO agencies have not yet caught up with what zero-click search means for healthcare and dental practices specifically. They may still be focused primarily on keyword rankings and page-one positions, which remain relevant but are no longer the whole picture.

A specialist agency working in dental marketing approaches this from a different angle. It understands that the goal is not just to rank but to be the source that patients and AI systems trust and reference. That means combining technical SEO, structured data, local profile management, content strategy and reputation management into a coherent approach rather than treating them as separate tasks.

It also means understanding the regulatory environment. The GDC and ASA place clear restrictions on how dental services can be described and promoted. A specialist agency works within those rules from the outset, rather than producing content that has to be revised or removed later.

Our generative engine optimisation service for dental practices is built around this approach. It is designed to help practices become the kind of well-structured, authoritative, consistently presented online presence that AI search systems can confidently draw on, without making claims that cannot be substantiated or promising outcomes that cannot be guaranteed.

It gives you a clear picture of where things stand before you decide whether any action is needed.

The bottom line

Zero-click search is not a temporary quirk. It reflects a structural shift in how patients interact with search engines, one that is being accelerated by the mainstream adoption of AI tools. For dental practice owners, the response is not to panic or to abandon existing SEO efforts. It is to ensure that your online presence is built in a way that works in this new environment, not just the one that existed five years ago.

The practices that will remain visible in this landscape are those that are accurately represented, consistently described and genuinely authoritative across the platforms that AI systems use to construct their answers. That is achievable, but it requires a different kind of thinking than simply chasing keyword rankings.
