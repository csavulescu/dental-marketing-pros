Dayparting & scheduling dental ads around your opening hours

By Cristian Savulescu, Founder · Published 17 September 2026

Running Google Ads around the clock sounds thorough, but it often means paying for clicks at times when no one can answer the phone. This guide explains how smart scheduling and dayparting can focus your budget where it actually converts.

If your Google Ads campaign runs twenty-four hours a day, seven days a week, you are almost certainly wasting a meaningful portion of your budget. Every click that arrives at two in the morning, on a bank holiday, or during the hour your phones go to voicemail is a click you have paid for and cannot convert. For dental practices across South Yorkshire and North Derbyshire, where competition for NHS and private patients is real and budgets are finite, that kind of waste is worth taking seriously. This article explains what dayparting is, why most practice-level campaigns ignore it, and how applying proper dental Google Ads scheduling can make your spend work considerably harder.

What is dayparting and why does it matter for dental practices?

Dayparting is the practice of restricting or adjusting how much you bid during specific hours of the day and specific days of the week. In Google Ads, it is managed through the Ad Schedule settings inside a campaign. At its simplest, you switch the campaign off outside your opening hours. At a more sophisticated level, you increase bids during the periods when your highest-value patients are most likely to search, and reduce them during quieter or lower-converting windows.

For most consumer businesses, the logic is straightforward: be visible when your audience is active. For dental practices, there is an additional layer. A prospective patient who clicks your ad and reaches your website at 11pm on a Saturday can browse your services and may even submit an online enquiry form. However, the majority of dental leads are driven by phone calls, and calls convert at a considerably higher rate than web enquiries left overnight. If no one is picking up the phone, you are paying for interest you cannot capture.

This is not a fringe concern. It sits at the heart of why generic, always-on campaigns so often underperform for practices that have not had their PPC set up by someone who understands how dental patient journeys actually work. The common problem: campaigns that run like a shop with the lights always on

When a practice sets up Google Ads in-house, or uses a generic digital marketing agency, the default is almost always to leave the campaign running continuously. This happens for a few understandable reasons.

  • Default settings favour Google's revenue. Google's campaign defaults do not prompt advertisers to restrict their hours. The platform earns more when your ads show more often, so there is no nudge to schedule carefully.
  • Fear of missing enquiries. Practice owners reasonably worry that switching off the ads overnight means missing a patient who searches late. That concern is valid, but it needs to be weighed against the cost of clicks that produce no outcome.
  • Lack of conversion tracking. Without proper call tracking and form-submission tracking tied back to specific hours, it is impossible to know which time slots are actually generating appointments. Many campaigns simply do not have this data set up correctly.

The result is a budget that is spread thinly across 168 hours per week, when in reality the hours that drive booked appointments are a much smaller subset of that window.

When are dental patients actually searching?

Search behaviour for dental services tends to follow a predictable pattern, though it will vary by practice location, patient demographics, and the type of treatment being promoted. Broadly speaking:

  • Weekday mornings, particularly between eight and ten, see a spike in health-related searches as people start their day and remember problems they have been putting off.
  • Lunchtime windows, roughly twelve to two, produce strong search volumes as people have a moment away from work to sort personal admin.
  • Early evening, between five and seven, can generate reasonable volume, though conversion rates often dip if the practice phones have already switched to answerphone.
  • Weekends produce variable results. Saturday mornings can perform well for practices that open on Saturdays. Sunday traffic tends to have weaker intent and lower conversion rates for most dental services.

None of this means you should apply these windows blindly. Your own conversion data, once properly tracked, should drive your scheduling decisions. But it does give you a starting framework if you are beginning from scratch.

How to set up dental Google Ads scheduling properly

Step one: install meaningful conversion tracking before you touch the schedule

Scheduling decisions made without data are little better than guesswork. Before you restrict your hours, make sure you are tracking phone calls (including calls from your Google Ads number extension), form submissions, and, if applicable, online booking completions. Tag these as separate conversion actions in Google Ads so you can see which hours produce which outcomes.

A call tracking solution that passes call timestamps back into Google Ads is particularly useful here. It lets you build a genuine picture of which hour blocks are delivering booked appointments, not just clicks.

Step two: pull a time-of-day report from your existing campaign

Inside Google Ads, navigate to Reports and run a time-of-day and day-of-week breakdown. Look at cost, conversions, and cost-per-conversion across each segment. You will almost certainly find that a portion of your weekly spend is concentrated in hours or days where conversions are minimal or absent. This is your baseline.

Step three: set your core schedule and apply bid adjustments

Rather than simply switching the campaign off and on, use bid adjustments to create a graduated schedule. During your highest-converting hours, set a positive bid adjustment, perhaps plus fifteen to thirty per cent, to make sure you are competitive when it matters most. During lower-performing but still worthwhile hours, run at your base bid. During hours where conversion data shows minimal return, reduce bids significantly or pause entirely.

For most South Yorkshire and North Derbyshire dental practices, a sensible starting point is to remove spend entirely between roughly ten at night and six in the morning, and to review whether weekend afternoon and Sunday budgets are genuinely earning their place.

Step four: account for bank holidays and practice-specific closures

Bank holidays are often overlooked. If your practice is closed on a bank holiday Monday and your campaign is still running at full spend, you are paying for clicks to a phone that will not be answered. Build a recurring review into your calendar to pause or reduce budgets ahead of planned closures. Some practices use automated rules in Google Ads to handle this, though manual oversight is still advisable.

Step five: review and refine monthly

Scheduling is not a set-and-forget task. Patient search behaviour shifts with seasons, with local economic conditions, and with the specific treatments you are promoting. An implant campaign may perform differently across the week compared to an emergency dental campaign. Review your time-of-day data at least monthly and adjust your schedule accordingly.

How a specialist dental PPC agency approaches scheduling differently

A generalist agency managing dozens of industries will apply dayparting as a line-item task. A specialist agency brings a different starting point: an understanding of how dental patient journeys actually unfold, what conversion rates look like across different treatment types, and how local competition in areas like Sheffield, Rotherham, Chesterfield, or Doncaster affects the value of each hour.

Specifically, a well-run dental PPC account managed by a specialist will typically include:

  • Call tracking integrated with the Google Ads account from day one, so scheduling decisions are always data-led rather than intuitive.
  • Separate campaigns for different treatment categories, each with its own schedule tuned to that treatment's search patterns. Emergency dental searches peak at different times than Invisalign enquiries.
  • Regular cross-referencing of the Google Ads schedule against the practice's actual reception data, to catch discrepancies such as a receptionist being off on certain afternoons that the standard opening-hours schedule does not account for.
  • Smart bidding strategies configured with the scheduling data in mind, because automated bidding and manual dayparting can conflict if not managed carefully.

This level of granularity is difficult to achieve without dedicated focus on the dental sector. It is one of the practical reasons why practices that move from generic agencies to specialist dental PPC management tend to see their cost-per-acquisition fall, without any increase in monthly spend. We cover the broader principles behind this on our PPC for dental practices page.

A note on out-of-hours enquiry forms

Some practices reasonably argue that they should stay visible out of hours because they have a strong online enquiry form and patients will submit overnight. This is a fair position, and if your form-to-appointment conversion rate supports it, a reduced-bid overnight presence can be justified. The key word is reduced. Running full bids overnight to capture form submissions that convert at a fraction of the rate of a live phone call is rarely the right balance. A modest overnight bid, just enough to catch high-intent late searches, combined with an automated email response that confirms receipt and sets an expectation of a call the following morning, is a more defensible approach.

Ready to stop paying for clicks that go nowhere?

Dental Google Ads scheduling is one of those optimisations that sounds simple but is routinely done poorly in practice. Getting it right requires proper conversion tracking, a granular understanding of your own patient contact patterns, and ongoing attention to the data. If your current campaign is running around the clock without a clear rationale for every hour it is live, there is almost certainly budget being recovered waiting for you.
