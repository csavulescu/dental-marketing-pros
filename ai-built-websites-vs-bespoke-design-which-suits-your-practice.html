AI-built websites vs bespoke design: which suits your practice?

By Cristian Savulescu, Founder · Published 26 October 2026

Not every dental practice needs the same website, but choosing the wrong build approach can cost you patients and credibility. This article sets out the honest differences between AI-built and bespoke dental websites so you can make the right call.

The number of website options available to dental practice owners has grown considerably in the last few years. At one end of the market, AI-built websites promise a professional-looking online presence for as little as £50 a month and a quick setup. At the other end, bespoke design agencies offer something crafted specifically for your practice, your brand, and your patient base. Both can serve real purposes. The problem is that many practice owners choose based on price or convenience alone, without fully understanding what each option actually delivers, and where each one falls short. This guide is written to help you make a genuinely informed decision.

What do AI-built dental websites actually include?

AI website platforms have improved markedly. Modern tools can generate a structured, mobile-responsive site from a template in a matter of hours, pre-populated with placeholder content and basic SEO settings. For a practice with a tight budget, a new associate just starting out, or a second site for a satellite location, that speed and low monthly cost has genuine appeal.

But it helps to understand precisely what you are paying for. Our detailed breakdown in What a £50 a month AI website includes for a dental practice sets out what is typically covered at the entry-level price point, and, just as importantly, what is not. In brief: you tend to get a hosted template, a handful of customisable pages, a contact form, and some automated on-page SEO. You do not usually get original photography, custom copywriting written for your specific patient demographic, or strategic thinking about conversion.

That distinction matters. A website that looks the part but fails to convert visitors into enquiries is not really doing its job. For more detail on the AI website option specifically, visit our AI websites for dental practices page.

What does bespoke design actually mean in practice?

Bespoke does not simply mean more expensive. It means the website is built around your practice from the ground up: your brand identity, your treatment mix, your target patient profile, the geography you serve, and the clinical tone you want to project. Nothing is carried over from a previous client's site. The sitemap, the navigation structure, the copy, the calls to action, and the visual design are all created specifically for you.

A well-structured bespoke site also takes into account factors that templates rarely address well. Navigation is one of them. If your practice offers implants, Invisalign, nervous patient care, and a children's service, how those are structured and signposted across the site determines whether a visitor finds what they need quickly or leaves. Our article on dental website navigation: structuring for treatments and locations explains why the architecture of a site is often more important than its visual design.

Accessibility is another area where bespoke development typically outperforms templated builds. Many AI platforms generate sites that meet only minimum accessibility standards, which can exclude patients with visual impairments or cognitive differences and potentially expose practices to compliance risk. Our article on accessibility on dental websites: compliance and more patients covers this in more detail. You can also explore the full bespoke service on our dental web design page.

The real comparison: where each option wins and where it does not

Speed and cost

AI-built sites win on both counts in the short term. If you need something live quickly and cannot commit to a significant upfront investment, an AI platform can be a workable starting point. That said, the monthly cost compounds over time. A site at £50 per month costs £600 per year. Over three years, that is £1,800, often without any meaningful ongoing development, content updates, or strategic input included in the fee.

Customisation and brand authority

This is where bespoke design has a clear advantage. Dental patients, particularly those researching higher-value treatments such as implants, orthodontics, or cosmetic work, make judgements about a practice's quality and credibility partly based on its website. A site that looks identical to three other practices in the region, because all three use the same template, does not differentiate you. Bespoke design allows you to build a visual identity that is consistent with your practice environment, your team's personality, and the standard of care you provide.

SEO and discoverability

Both AI and bespoke sites can be optimised for search, but the baseline is different. AI platforms tend to include generic SEO settings that are applied uniformly. Bespoke sites allow for treatment-level and location-level targeting from the outset, which is particularly useful for practices serving multiple towns or offering specialist treatments. Neither approach guarantees specific rankings, but a site built with local SEO in mind from the start is generally better positioned than one where SEO has been retrofitted later.

Conversion and patient experience

This is perhaps the most underappreciated factor. A website's job is not just to be found. It is to reassure a prospective patient, answer their questions, and make it easy for them to book. AI templates are not built around any particular patient psychology. Bespoke design can address specific barriers: for instance, a practice with a strong nervous patient offering can have that message woven throughout the site structure, imagery, and copy, rather than added as a single page afterthought. For context on how design decisions affect nervous patients specifically, our article on designing a dental website for nervous patients is worth reading before you make any decisions about your build approach.

So which option suits your practice?

The honest answer is that it depends on your circumstances. An AI-built site may be the right choice if:

  • You are an associate setting up independently for the first time and need an interim presence while you grow.
  • You have a very constrained budget and need to get something live before you can reinvest.
  • You are opening a satellite location and need a lightweight supporting site rather than a full standalone presence.
  • You already have a strong bespoke main site and want a lower-cost test for a specific campaign or service.

Bespoke design is likely the better investment if:

  • You are a principal or practice owner who relies on new patient enquiries through your website as a meaningful part of your growth strategy.
  • You are competing in a market where several local practices already have well-developed digital presences.
  • You offer higher-value or specialist treatments where the cost of acquiring a single patient justifies a more considered website investment.
  • Your current site is generating traffic but not converting that traffic into bookings.
  • Your brand has matured and your current online presence no longer reflects the quality of care you deliver.

Why working with a specialist dental agency changes the equation

Whether you ultimately choose an AI-assisted build or a fully bespoke site, the expertise behind the work matters more than the technology used to produce it. A generalist web agency building a dental site for the first time will not understand the GDC's guidance on claims and testimonials, the ASA requirements around promotional language, or the specific reasons a patient hesitates before booking an implant consultation. A dental marketing specialist understands these constraints and builds around them from the start.
