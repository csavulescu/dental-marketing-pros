How to recover from a Google algorithm update

By Cristian Savulescu, Founder · Published 11 November 2026

Google algorithm updates can knock a dental practice's website from the first page overnight. This guide explains why it happens and what to do next — step by step.

You log into Google Search Console one morning and notice your dental practice website has dropped out of the top results. Enquiries have slowed. You haven't changed anything on the site. The most likely culprit is a Google algorithm update. It is a situation that affects practices across the country, and while it can feel alarming, it is recoverable. What matters is knowing why it happened and how to respond methodically rather than reactively.

What is a Google algorithm update and why should dentists care?

Google updates the systems it uses to rank websites hundreds of times each year. Most changes are minor and go unnoticed. A handful of times each year, however, Google releases what it calls a "core update" or a targeted update focusing on specific issues such as spam, link quality, or the helpfulness of content. These larger updates can reshuffle results significantly, and healthcare and medical websites, including dental practices, are often in the category Google scrutinises most carefully.

Google refers to this group as YMYL websites, short for "Your Money or Your Life." Because dental advice and treatment decisions directly affect people's health and wellbeing, Google holds dental content to a higher standard. Any weakness in your site's credibility, accuracy, or authority is therefore more likely to be penalised when a dental website algorithm update rolls out.

Why your site may have been affected

Not every drop in traffic is caused by the same underlying problem. Before taking any action, it is worth understanding which of the common causes is most relevant to your site.

Thin or outdated content

Google's helpful content systems, introduced and refined over recent updates, are designed to reward pages that demonstrate genuine expertise and usefulness. If your treatment pages are brief, copied from a supplier's template, or have not been updated in several years, they may now rank lower than competitors whose content is more thorough and clearly written by people with relevant knowledge.

Weak E-E-A-T signals

E-E-A-T stands for Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness. Google's quality raters use these criteria to assess pages. For a dental practice, this means your site should clearly communicate who the clinicians are, what their qualifications and GDC registration numbers are, and why a patient should trust the advice given. If your website lacks bios, credentials, or verifiable contact information, it is more vulnerable to algorithm updates that target thin or untrustworthy content.

Link profile issues

Algorithm updates targeting link spam can remove the ranking benefit of low-quality or irrelevant backlinks your site may have accumulated. If your site was previously benefiting from poor-quality links, an update may have discounted them, causing rankings to fall. Conversely, a site with strong, relevant backlinks from local directories, dental associations, and reputable health organisations tends to be more resilient.

Technical problems that have worsened over time

A slow site, poor mobile experience, or pages that cannot be properly crawled and indexed will hold you back after any update that places greater weight on user experience. These issues rarely cause an instant crash on their own, but they compound the effect of a core update.

How to diagnose the problem accurately

Reacting without diagnosis is the most common mistake practices make. Changing content, removing pages, or disavowing links without evidence can make things worse. Here is a logical sequence to follow.

  1. Confirm the timing. Check when your traffic dropped against Google's official list of confirmed updates. Google announces core updates and other major changes on its Search Central blog. If your drop aligns with a confirmed update date, you have a likely cause.
  2. Identify which pages were affected. Use Google Search Console to filter by page and compare traffic before and after the drop date. If it is concentrated on specific treatment pages, that points to a content issue. If it is sitewide, the causes may be more structural.
  3. Benchmark against competitors. If a competitor who previously ranked below you has now overtaken you, examine their pages. What are they doing differently in terms of content depth, credentials, and site structure? This tells you what Google now appears to prefer in your market.
  4. Run a technical audit. Check for crawl errors, page speed issues, mobile usability problems, and broken links. These issues will not cause a recovery on their own, but they will prevent one if they are left in place.

The recovery process: what to actually do

Once you understand what caused the drop, you can begin addressing it. Recovery from a dental website algorithm update rarely happens overnight. Google typically re-evaluates sites at the next core update, which can be several months away. That is not a reason to delay, however. The improvements you make now will determine how your site performs when that re-evaluation happens.

Improve and expand key treatment pages

Focus on the pages that lost the most visibility. Rewrite them with genuine depth. Address the questions patients actually ask, explain the procedure clearly, describe what to expect, and include clinician credentials where relevant. Avoid copying content from industry templates. Where appropriate, have a qualified clinician review or contribute to the page, and credit them clearly. For advice on how design decisions affect the patient experience on these pages, our article on designing a dental website for nervous patients is worth reading alongside your content review.

Strengthen your E-E-A-T signals

Add or update clinician bios with GDC registration numbers, qualifications, and years of experience. Make your practice address, phone number, and registered business information easy to find. If you have published articles, been featured in dental publications, or hold memberships with professional bodies, these should be referenced on your site. These signals matter more after updates that target credibility.

Build relevant, quality backlinks

Focus on earning links from local business directories, your Local Dental Committee, dental association websites, and any local press or community organisations you work with. These carry far more value than generic directory submissions or paid link placements. If you discover your site has accumulated poor-quality links historically, the Google Search Console disavow tool can be used, but cautiously and only with evidence that specific links are harmful.

Fix technical issues

Address any crawl errors, improve page loading speed, ensure your site is fully functional on mobile devices, and check that your sitemap is submitted and up to date. These are the foundations that allow your content improvements to be properly indexed.

Protecting your site against future updates

Recovery is one goal. Resilience is the longer-term aim. Practices that are least affected by algorithm updates tend to share certain characteristics: their content is thorough and regularly maintained, their clinician credentials are clearly displayed, their technical foundations are sound, and their backlink profile is built on genuine relationships rather than shortcuts.

It is also worth having a realistic view of what your website is trying to do and whether the structure supports that. If your site was built quickly or on a low-cost template without much thought given to dental-specific requirements, it may be worth reviewing whether it is fit for purpose. The right next step

If your practice website has lost ground after a recent algorithm update, the most useful thing you can do right now is get an accurate picture of where the problem lies. Speculation and reactive changes rarely help. A proper audit, benchmarked against your competitors and tailored to the specific demands of dental SEO, will tell you what to prioritise and in what order.
