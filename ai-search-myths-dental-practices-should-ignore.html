AI search myths dental practices should ignore

By Cristian Savulescu, Founder · Published 6 November 2026

AI search is changing how patients find dental practices, but a lot of what's being said about it is either exaggerated or plain wrong. Here's what UK practice owners actually need to know.

Every few months, a new wave of bold claims sweeps through dental marketing circles. First it was "Google is dead." Then it was "social media is all you need." Now the conversation has shifted to AI search, and with it has come a fresh set of myths that are causing real confusion among practice owners. Some are ignoring AI search entirely because they've been told it doesn't affect dentistry yet. Others are chasing tactics that promise to "hack" AI algorithms in ways that simply aren't possible. Neither approach serves your practice well. This article cuts through the noise, addresses the most persistent AI search myths dentists tend to encounter, and gives you a clearer picture of what's actually worth your attention.

Myth 1: AI search is too new to matter for dental practices right now

It's tempting to file AI search under "things to revisit in a few years." The technology does feel new, and the landscape is still evolving. But the truth is that tools like Google AI Overviews, Perplexity, ChatGPT, Gemini, and Microsoft Copilot are already being used by people who are searching for local services, including dental practices, right now. When someone asks an AI assistant "where can I get Invisalign near Sheffield?" or "what's the best private dentist in Doncaster?", something is returned to them. The question is whether your practice has any chance of featuring in that response.

AI assistants draw on a wide range of publicly available information: your website content, your Google Business Profile, online directories, reviews, and third-party mentions across the web. If that information is thin, inconsistent, or outdated, you are already at a disadvantage, even if you haven't fully felt it yet. Waiting until AI search is "mature" before paying attention to it means you'll be playing catch-up while other practices have already built a stronger presence.

Myth 2: You can "optimise" your way into AI recommendations

This is probably the most damaging myth circulating right now, because it has spawned a small industry of people selling "AI ranking" services with very confident promises attached. The reality is more complicated, and far more honest than that.

No one outside of Google, OpenAI, Microsoft, or the other developers behind these tools has precise knowledge of how their systems decide what to surface in a response. The systems are complex, frequently updated, and not fully transparent, even to the people building them. Any agency or consultant telling you they know exactly how to get your practice cited by ChatGPT or featured in a Google AI Overview is overstating what can actually be known.

What we do know is that there are practices and content approaches that can help your practice become more visible across AI-generated responses. These include maintaining accurate and consistent information across your website and directory listings, publishing genuinely helpful and specific content that addresses questions patients actually ask, earning quality mentions and links from relevant sources, and ensuring your Google Business Profile is complete and regularly updated. None of these are "hacks." They are good digital marketing fundamentals that happen to align with what AI systems appear to draw on when constructing responses.

Myth 3: Traditional SEO and AI search are completely separate things

Some practice owners have been told they need to abandon their existing SEO strategy and start again with something called "GEO" or "AIO optimisation" or any number of other newly coined terms. This framing tends to benefit people selling new products more than it benefits your practice.

The honest picture is that strong traditional SEO and a good AI search presence are largely built on the same foundations. A well-structured website with clear, accurate information about your services. Location-specific pages that answer genuine patient questions. A healthy backlink profile from reputable sources. Consistent NAP (name, address, phone number) data across the web. Positive and plentiful reviews. These things have always mattered for search visibility, and they continue to matter as AI systems increasingly rely on the same publicly indexed information that Google has been using for years.

The difference with AI search is in how that information is presented to the user. Rather than a list of links, the user gets a synthesised response. That changes some priorities around content, particularly around being clear, specific, and structured in how you explain what your practice offers. But it doesn't require you to throw away everything you've built.

Myth 4: Reviews don't influence AI search results

This myth tends to arise because reviews feel like a "soft" signal compared to technical SEO factors. But the evidence suggests that reviews are among the more meaningful trust signals that AI systems draw on when evaluating local businesses. Google AI Overviews, for instance, appear to integrate Google Business Profile data, which is heavily influenced by review volume and sentiment. Perplexity and other tools often pull from third-party review platforms when constructing responses about local services.

For dental practices, this means your review strategy isn't just about reassuring prospective patients who land on your profile. It's also about how AI tools characterise your practice when someone asks for a recommendation. A practice with a small number of dated reviews and a middling rating is likely to be represented differently from one with consistent, recent, detailed feedback. The content of reviews matters too. When patients describe specific treatments, mention the name of a clinician, or reference your location, that contextual detail becomes part of the data picture that AI systems can draw on.

Myth 5: AI search will replace Google completely, so Google no longer matters

This is worth addressing directly, because the "Google is finished" narrative has been circulating since ChatGPT launched and it continues to overstate what's actually happening. Google remains by far the most widely used search platform in the UK, and that position hasn't fundamentally changed. What has changed is that Google itself has integrated AI into its own search experience through AI Overviews, meaning the two things are not in opposition: they're converging.

Practices that maintain strong Google visibility, through a well-optimised website, a complete and active Google Business Profile, and a solid local SEO strategy, are also better positioned for the AI-integrated version of Google search. Abandoning Google in favour of chasing visibility on standalone AI platforms would be a significant strategic mistake for most practices.

Myth 6: Content volume is what matters most for AI visibility

The idea that publishing large quantities of content, blog posts, service pages, FAQs, and anything else that can be generated quickly will automatically improve your AI search presence is one of the more persistent misconceptions. AI systems are increasingly capable of identifying thin, repetitive, or low-quality content, and publishing it at volume can do more harm than good to your overall credibility online.

What appears to matter far more is specificity and genuine usefulness. A single, well-written page that clearly explains what your implant process involves, what it costs in approximate terms, what the recovery looks like, and what makes your practice qualified to provide it, is likely to be more valuable than ten pages of generic content padded out to hit a word count. When AI tools are constructing a response about dental implants in your area, they're looking for sources that answer questions thoroughly and authoritatively, not sources that simply mention the topic repeatedly.
