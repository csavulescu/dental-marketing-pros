Perplexity, Gemini and Copilot: how each finds local dentists

By Cristian Savulescu, Founder · Published 27 October 2026

AI assistants are increasingly the first place patients turn when searching for a local dentist, yet most practices have no strategy for appearing in these results. This article explains how Perplexity, Gemini and Copilot each surface dental practices, and what you can do about it.

Patients are no longer relying solely on Google's blue links to find a dentist. A growing number are typing questions directly into AI assistants such as Perplexity, Google Gemini, Microsoft Copilot and ChatGPT, and acting on whatever those tools recommend. If your practice does not appear in those recommendations, you are losing enquiries to competitors who may not even be better practices. They are simply better represented in the sources these tools trust. Understanding how each AI assistant finds and evaluates local dental practices is now a practical concern for every practice owner, not a distant future problem.

Why AI assistants matter for dental practices right now

Search behaviour has shifted considerably over the past two years. AI assistants do not just retrieve a list of links; they synthesise information from multiple sources and present a single, confident-sounding answer. When a patient asks "Which dentist near me offers Invisalign?" or "Where can I find an NHS dentist in Sheffield?", the AI will often name specific practices. Those practices did not pay to be named. They appeared because the AI's underlying data sources could confirm their existence, location, services and credibility.

For context on how this decision-making process looks from the patient's perspective, the article how patients use AI assistants to choose a dentist walks through the journey in detail. The short version: patients trust these answers more than they arguably should, which means being absent from them carries a real cost.

How each AI assistant finds local dental practices

Each major AI assistant has a different architecture, and those differences affect which practices get surfaced. Understanding the broad approach each one takes can help you make better decisions about where to invest your marketing effort.

Google Gemini

Gemini is deeply integrated with Google's own ecosystem. When a user asks Gemini about local dentists, it draws on Google's Knowledge Graph, Google Business Profile data, structured information from indexed websites and, increasingly, AI Overviews within Google Search itself. This means that everything you would do for traditional local SEO, such as maintaining an accurate Google Business Profile, earning genuine reviews, and having well-structured service pages, still matters enormously here. Gemini is, in many respects, the most immediately actionable of the AI assistants for practice owners because the inputs are largely the same ones you should already be managing.

If your Google Business Profile lists outdated opening hours, no photos, or a handful of reviews from several years ago, Gemini is likely to either skip your practice or present incomplete information about it. Accuracy and freshness of your publicly available data are particularly important signals for this assistant.

Microsoft Copilot

Copilot is powered by Bing's index and uses real-time web search to support many of its answers. It cites sources visibly, which means users can and do follow through to check your website. Copilot tends to favour authoritative, well-structured pages that rank in Bing's own results. For dental practices this means two things: first, Bing should not be ignored entirely when thinking about technical SEO; second, having clear, factual, well-written service and location pages gives Copilot the kind of quotable content it needs to reference your practice confidently.

Copilot also pays attention to third-party directories and review platforms. NHS Choices listings, Trustpilot, and reputable dental directories all contribute to the picture it can build of your practice. If those listings are inconsistent or missing, the assistant may simply move on to a competitor with cleaner data.

Perplexity

Perplexity describes itself as an answer engine. It actively crawls the web in near real-time and builds its responses from the most citable, specific and credible sources it can find for a given query. For local dental queries it will draw on your website, relevant directories, review platforms and any local press or editorial coverage your practice has earned. What distinguishes Perplexity from the others is its strong preference for specificity. Vague or generic content is less likely to be pulled; content that clearly states what your practice offers, who it serves and where it is located is far more useful to its retrieval process.

Perplexity also shows its sources transparently, so if a patient asks it which practices in Worksop accept new NHS patients and your practice appears with a proper citation, that is a meaningful moment of visibility. If you operate in areas like those covered in our dental marketing in Worksop: a local growth guide, this matters because smaller local markets are exactly the contexts where Perplexity's real-time crawling can surface individual practices rather than deferring to aggregators.

Why most dental practices are invisible to these tools

The root cause is usually the same across practices of all sizes: the publicly available information about the practice is thin, inconsistent or structured in a way that machines cannot easily parse. Specifically, the most common problems are:

  • Inconsistent NAP data. Name, address and phone number appearing differently across your website, Google Business Profile, NHS directory and other listings confuses AI assistants that are trying to confirm your practice's identity.
  • Generic service pages. A page that says only "we offer dental implants" gives an AI assistant nothing specific to cite. A page that explains the procedure, who it suits, what the process involves at your practice, and where you are located is far more useful.
  • No structured data markup. Schema markup helps machines understand what your pages are about. Without it, even a well-written page may not signal clearly that it belongs to a dental practice at a specific address offering specific services.
  • Weak or sparse reviews. All three AI assistants use review data as a trust signal. A practice with few reviews, or reviews that are many years old, carries less weight in these systems.
  • No credible third-party references. If nothing beyond your own website mentions your practice in a meaningful way, AI assistants have limited evidence to work with when deciding whether to recommend you.

What practice owners can do: practical steps

The discipline that addresses AI assistant visibility is often called Generative Engine Optimisation, or GEO. It overlaps significantly with traditional local SEO but adds a layer of focus on how content is structured for machine comprehension and citation. Our GEO service for dental practices is built specifically around the requirements of AI-driven search, and the following steps reflect the kind of work that can make a meaningful difference.

Audit your existing data footprint

Before adding anything new, check what currently exists. Search for your practice name in each of the major AI assistants and note what comes back. Is the information accurate? Is there any? Are competitors appearing instead of you? A free SEO audit can help identify the specific gaps in your current online presence before you decide where to invest effort.

Standardise your practice information everywhere

Every directory, listing and profile that mentions your practice should show identical name, address and phone number. This includes your Google Business Profile, NHS profiles, Bing Places, Yell, Dental Directory listings and anywhere else you appear. Consistency is not just tidy; it is how AI assistants verify that different mentions across the web refer to the same business.

Build specific, location-anchored content

Write service pages that combine the treatment or service with your location and the patient type you serve. A page about composite bonding at your Leeds practice is more useful to an AI assistant than a generic composite bonding page. Practices in areas with distinct local markets, such as those explored in dental marketing in Leeds: the complete local guide and dental marketing in Wakefield: a local growth guide, benefit particularly from this kind of place-specific content because the AI can match it more precisely to location-based queries.

Add and maintain schema markup

Implement LocalBusiness and Dentist schema markup on your website. This tells search engines and AI assistants in a structured, machine-readable format exactly what your practice is, where it is, what services it offers, and how to contact you. Done correctly, this markup can help AI assistants build a more complete and confident picture of your practice.

Generate a steady stream of genuine reviews

Ask patients to leave reviews on Google and other relevant platforms as part of your normal post-appointment process. AI assistants use review volume and recency as signals. There is no shortcut here; the reviews need to be real, and the process needs to be consistent over time.

Earn mentions beyond your own website

Local press, dental association listings, patient review sites and community directories all contribute to the web of references that AI assistants draw on. Consider whether local editorial coverage or participation in professional bodies could improve your practice's external footprint.
