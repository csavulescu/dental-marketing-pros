Remarketing for dental practices: winning back lost enquiries

Most visitors who land on your dental practice website leave without making an enquiry. Remarketing through Google Ads gives you a structured way to bring them back and convert that lost interest into booked appointments.

If you have ever run a Google Ads campaign and watched the traffic roll in, only to see a small fraction of visitors actually pick up the phone or fill in a contact form, you are not alone. The reality of dental website traffic is that the vast majority of people who visit your site will leave without making any contact at all. They might be genuinely interested in Invisalign, implants, or a hygiene appointment, but something stops them from enquiring on that first visit. Maybe they got distracted. Maybe they wanted to compare a few options. Maybe they simply were not ready yet. Whatever the reason, that person is gone, and without a remarketing strategy in place, you have no way of reaching them again.

This article explains what dental remarketing through Google Ads actually involves, why so many practices are leaving significant opportunity on the table by ignoring it, and what a well-structured remarketing campaign looks like when it is set up properly.

Why visitors leave without enquiring

It is worth understanding the problem before trying to fix it. People searching for dental treatments, particularly higher-value ones like orthodontics, implants, or cosmetic dentistry, rarely make a decision on their first exposure to a practice. This is not a sign that your website is failing or that your ads are not working. It reflects the natural behaviour of a person making a considered purchase.

They will often visit several practice websites, read some reviews, look at before-and-after galleries, check prices where they are listed, and then think about it for a few days. During that period, the practice that stays visible and relevant to them has a clear advantage. Without remarketing, you are invisible during that critical decision-making window. With it, you can maintain a presence and gently reinforce why your practice is worth contacting.

There is also the matter of intent. Someone who has already visited your implants page is far more qualified than someone who has never heard of your practice. They already know you exist. They have shown an interest in the specific treatment you offer. Remarketing lets you target that warm audience rather than starting from scratch every time.

What dental remarketing on Google Ads actually involves

Remarketing, sometimes called retargeting, is a feature within Google Ads that lets you serve ads specifically to people who have previously visited your website. Google places a small piece of code, called a tag or pixel, on your site. This builds audience lists that you can then target with specific ad campaigns across the Google Display Network, YouTube, and in some configurations, Google Search itself.

For a dental practice, this might look like the following in practice:

  • A visitor lands on your dental implants page from a Google search ad, browses for two minutes, then closes the tab.
  • They are added to a remarketing audience list labelled something like "implants page visitors, last 30 days."
  • Over the next week or two, as they browse other websites or watch YouTube videos, they see a well-designed display ad from your practice, featuring your implants offering and a clear call to action.
  • They click through, revisit your site, and this time they enquire.

That is the core mechanic. But the real value lies in how intelligently the campaigns are structured.

The components of an effective dental remarketing campaign

Audience segmentation

One of the most common mistakes practices make when setting up remarketing for the first time is treating all past visitors as a single group. They are not. Someone who spent four minutes on your Invisalign page and visited your pricing section has a very different intent profile from someone who bounced off your homepage after three seconds.

A properly segmented campaign would create separate audience lists for high-intent visitors (those who visited treatment pages, spent a meaningful amount of time on site, or visited the contact page but did not submit a form) and would serve those audiences different messages at different bid levels. Someone who nearly contacted you deserves more aggressive follow-up and a more direct message than someone who briefly skimmed your about page.

Ad creative that reflects the treatment of interest

Generic dental ads in remarketing campaigns are a wasted opportunity. If someone visited your teeth whitening page, showing them a display ad about your full range of treatments is far less effective than showing them an ad specifically about teeth whitening. This level of relevance improves click-through rates and reinforces the sense that your practice understands what they are looking for.

Responsive display ads within Google Ads allow you to provide multiple headlines, descriptions, and images, which Google then tests and combines. For dental practices, it is worth building out separate responsive display ads for each major treatment area, matched to the corresponding audience lists.

Frequency capping and exclusions

Remarketing that is not properly controlled can become counterproductive. If a past visitor sees your ad twenty times in a week, it stops feeling helpful and starts feeling intrusive. Setting sensible frequency caps, typically somewhere in the range of three to five impressions per user per day, keeps your presence consistent without becoming annoying.

Equally important are exclusion lists. People who have already converted, meaning those who have submitted a contact form or called your practice, should be excluded from remarketing audiences immediately. Continuing to show them acquisition ads after they have already enquired is a waste of budget and a poor experience for someone who is now a potential patient.

Bid adjustments and campaign duration

Not all audience members are equally likely to convert at any given moment. Bid adjustments allow you to increase how much you are willing to spend to reach the highest-intent audiences. Someone who visited your contact page and did not submit a form is arguably the most valuable person in your remarketing pool, and it makes sense to bid more aggressively to reach them.

Campaign duration also matters. Remarketing audiences can be set to include visitors from as far back as 540 days, but in practice, the most conversion-ready visitors are usually those from the past 30 days. Beyond that, interest tends to drop off or they have already made a decision elsewhere. Running separate campaigns with different durations and bid strategies lets you focus budget where it is most likely to deliver results.

How a specialist agency approaches this differently

Setting up remarketing is technically accessible to anyone with a Google Ads account, but there is a meaningful difference between a campaign that has been switched on and one that has been built with a clear strategy behind it.

At Dental Marketing Pros, our approach to dental PPC campaigns treats remarketing as an integral layer of the overall paid search strategy, not an afterthought. That means the Google Tag is verified and firing correctly from day one. It means audience lists are structured logically around your actual treatment pages and patient journey. It means ad creative is reviewed and refreshed regularly so that your ads do not go stale or become invisible through familiarity.

It also means the data is being read and acted on. Remarketing campaigns generate useful intelligence about which treatments attract the most engaged visitors, which landing page content holds attention, and where people are dropping off before they enquire. That information feeds back into the broader campaign and, in turn, into your wider marketing decisions.

There is also the question of compliance. Google Ads policies and UK advertising standards apply to dental marketing, and certain claims or imagery can cause ads to be disapproved or accounts to be flagged. An agency with specific dental experience understands where those boundaries sit and builds campaigns that stay within them without sacrificing effectiveness.

Integrating remarketing into your broader paid search activity

Remarketing works best when it sits within a coherent paid search structure. Your primary search campaigns attract new visitors. Your remarketing campaigns recapture those who did not convert. Together, they cover both ends of the decision-making process.

For practices running Google Ads across multiple treatment areas, a layered approach makes sense. High-value treatments like implants and Orthodontics warrant dedicated remarketing campaigns with more carefully crafted creative and higher bids. Lower-value treatments can often be covered by a broader display remarketing campaign at more conservative spend levels.

Practices that currently run Google Ads but have no remarketing in place are effectively paying to drive traffic and then walking away when visitors do not convert immediately. In a competitive market like South Yorkshire and North Derbyshire, where several practices may be bidding on the same treatment keywords, that is a costly gap in your strategy.
