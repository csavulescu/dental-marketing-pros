GEO vs SEO for dentists: what's actually different

By Cristian Savulescu, Founder · Published 9 October 2026

Search is changing fast, and dental practices that rely solely on traditional SEO risk becoming invisible to patients using AI assistants and generative search tools. This article explains what separates GEO from SEO, why both matter, and what to do about it.

If you have spent any time recently looking into digital marketing for your practice, you have probably come across two terms sitting uncomfortably close together: SEO and GEO. They sound similar, they overlap in places, and the advice circulating online tends to conflate them in ways that are more confusing than helpful. This article is a clear-headed attempt to separate them, explain what each one actually does for a dental practice, and give you a practical sense of where your attention should go right now.

The problem: two different versions of "being found online"

For the past decade or more, dental practices have invested in SEO, which is Search Engine Optimisation, to appear in Google's organic results when someone searches for "dentist in Sheffield" or "Invisalign near me." That model is still relevant. But something meaningful has shifted in the way search works, and it has created a second, distinct challenge that traditional SEO does not fully address.

A growing number of people now start their search not with Google, but with AI tools. They ask ChatGPT, Google's AI Overviews, Microsoft Copilot, Gemini, or Perplexity a question like "Which dental practices near Rotherham offer same-day emergency appointments?" and expect a direct, conversational answer rather than a list of ten blue links to click through. The tool reads across a wide range of sources, synthesises an answer, and presents it, sometimes without the user ever visiting a single website.

This is where GEO comes in. Generative Engine Optimisation is the discipline of shaping your practice's online presence so that AI-driven tools can find, understand, and potentially reference your content when answering questions relevant to your services. It is not a replacement for SEO. It is an additional layer that is becoming harder to ignore. You can read more about the fundamentals in our guide, What is GEO? A plain-English guide for dental practice owners.

How traditional SEO works for dental practices

To understand what makes GEO different, it helps to be clear about what SEO actually does. At its core, SEO for a dental practice involves three broad areas.

Technical foundations

Your website needs to load quickly, work properly on mobile devices, and be structured in a way that search engine crawlers can read. This includes clean page hierarchies, proper use of heading tags, and accurate structured data markup, things that tell Google what kind of business you are and what each page is about.

Local search signals

For dental practices, local visibility is everything. This means keeping your Google Business Profile accurate and well-maintained, building consistent citations across directories, and earning genuine reviews from patients. For a closer look at how this plays out in practice, our article on Local SEO for dentists: ranking for 'dentist near me' covers the detail.

Content and authority

Google's algorithms have become increasingly focused on expertise and trustworthiness, particularly in health-related sectors. Publishing accurate, well-attributed clinical content, and making clear that it is written or reviewed by a qualified dental professional, can strengthen your visibility in competitive searches. This is closely tied to what Google calls E-E-A-T, and we have written separately about why dentist authorship matters for SEO.

All of these signals feed into where your website appears in traditional search results pages. It is a well-understood, measurable discipline with a long track record. If your practice is based in a competitive urban area such as Sheffield, Barnsley, or Doncaster, good SEO is non-negotiable.

How GEO works differently

GEO addresses a different question: not "can Google rank this page?" but "can an AI assistant understand who you are, what you offer, and whether your practice is worth referencing in a generated answer?"

The distinction matters because AI tools do not work like search engines. They do not serve up a ranked list of links based on authority scores. They synthesise information from multiple sources and generate a response. The factors that influence whether your practice appears in that response are not identical to the factors that determine your Google ranking, though they do overlap significantly.

Clarity and structure of information

AI systems tend to draw on content that is clearly organised, factually specific, and easy to parse. Vague marketing copy does not help. If your website says "we offer a wide range of treatments in a welcoming environment," that gives an AI tool very little to work with. If it clearly states the treatments you offer, your location, your opening hours, whether you accept NHS or private patients, and what the booking process involves, that is far more useful to both human readers and AI systems.

Consistency across the web

AI tools pull information from many different sources: your website, your Google Business Profile, dental directories, review platforms, local news mentions, and more. If those sources say different things about your practice, an AI tool may struggle to form a confident picture. Consistent, accurate information across every touchpoint can help. This is not unique to GEO, but GEO makes it more important.

Content that directly answers questions

Generative AI tools are built to answer questions. Practices that publish content addressing specific patient questions, such as "how long does a dental implant procedure take?" or "what should I do if I have a dental emergency on a Sunday in Doncaster?", give AI systems material they can actually use when generating a response. This is sometimes called FAQ-style or question-led content, and it sits at the heart of a well-executed GEO strategy for dental practices.

Credibility signals that travel beyond your website

Just as SEO depends partly on links from other reputable sites, GEO depends partly on whether your practice is mentioned or referenced in credible external sources. This could be a local news article about your practice, a listing in a recognised dental directory, or a mention in a professional body's materials. These signals help AI tools make more confident assessments about whether your practice is a legitimate, established provider.

Where GEO and SEO overlap, and where they diverge

The honest answer is that good SEO and good GEO share a common foundation: accurate, well-structured, trustworthy content about a clearly defined practice. If you have invested seriously in SEO over the past few years, you are not starting from scratch with GEO. But there are meaningful differences in emphasis.

  • Rankings vs. references: SEO aims for a specific position in a ranked list. GEO is about being a source that AI tools can draw on, which is harder to measure directly and does not follow a simple ranking model.
  • Keywords vs. questions: SEO content is often built around keyword phrases. GEO content works best when it is built around the actual questions patients ask, in the language they use.
  • Page authority vs. information quality: Traditional SEO places heavy weight on domain authority and backlink profiles. AI tools appear to weight the clarity, specificity, and factual accuracy of information more heavily, though it is important to be honest here: no one outside these companies has full visibility into how their systems make decisions.
  • Single platform vs. whole-web presence: SEO is primarily about Google. GEO requires thinking about how your practice appears across the entire web, because AI tools aggregate from many sources simultaneously.

What this means practically for your practice

If you are a practice owner in South Yorkshire or North Derbyshire trying to decide where to focus your marketing budget, the honest answer is that this is not an either-or decision. The practices that will be best placed over the next few years are those that treat SEO and GEO as complementary, not competing.

Start with a clear-eyed look at where your current online presence stands. Is your Google Business Profile complete and actively managed? Is your website technically sound and well-structured? Do you publish content that clearly explains your treatments, your team's qualifications, and the practical details patients need? If the answer to any of these is uncertain, those are the gaps to address first, because they affect both your SEO and your GEO readiness simultaneously.

If your fundamentals are solid, the next step is auditing your content for question-led relevance and your wider web presence for consistency. This is where a structured GEO strategy becomes useful. For practices in competitive areas, for example those competing for searches across Sheffield, Rotherham, or Chesterfield, the practices investing in this now are likely to be better positioned as AI search becomes more mainstream.

It is also worth being realistic about timelines. Neither SEO nor GEO produces overnight results. Both require consistent, well-planned effort over months rather than weeks. If someone is promising you rapid, guaranteed visibility through either approach, that is a claim worth scrutinising carefully.

How a specialist agency approaches this differently

General digital marketing agencies typically apply the same frameworks across every sector. Dental practices are different. The GDC's guidance on patient communication, the ASA's rules around healthcare advertising, and the specific way patients research and choose dental providers all shape what good dental content looks like.

A specialist dental marketing agency understands that you cannot make sweeping claims about treatment outcomes, that testimonials need to be handled carefully, and that the trust signals patients look for in a dental provider are specific and clinical in nature. These constraints do not make GEO or SEO harder; they make it more important to get the detail right from the start. For a market-level view of how this plays out, our Dental SEO in Wakefield: a market overview for practices article gives a sense of the competitive landscape practices face.

A specialist agency will also be honest about what GEO can and cannot do. AI tools make their own decisions about what sources to reference, and no agency can guarantee that your practice will be cited in a generated response. What can be done is building the kind of well-structured, credible, consistent online presence that gives you the best reasonable chance of being part of that picture.
