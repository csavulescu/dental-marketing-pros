Google Business Profile posts: a monthly plan for practices

By Cristian Savulescu, Founder · Published 25 November 2026

Most dental practices set up a Google Business Profile and then leave it largely untouched. This guide gives you a practical, month-by-month posting plan that keeps your profile active, relevant, and working harder for your practice.

Your Google Business Profile is one of the first things a prospective patient sees when they search for a dentist in your area. Yet for many practices, the profile sits largely dormant after the initial setup. The name, address and phone number are correct. There might be a handful of photos. But the posts section? Either empty, or last updated sometime before a bank holiday that now feels distant. This is a missed opportunity, and a straightforward one to fix with a bit of structure.

This article sets out a practical monthly posting plan for dental practices, covering what to post, how often, and why consistency matters more than occasional bursts of activity. It is written for practice owners and practice managers who want to get more from a channel they already own.

Why Google Business Profile posts matter for dental practices

Google Business Profile posts appear directly on your listing in Google Search and Google Maps. They are visible to anyone who finds your practice through a local search, often before they have visited your website. That makes them unusually valuable real estate, particularly for searches like "dentist near me" or "dental implants Sheffield," where the map pack dominates the results page.

Posts can highlight services, share useful information, promote seasonal offers (within GDC and ASA guidelines), and demonstrate that your practice is active and well-run. A profile that is regularly updated with relevant content signals to both Google and prospective patients that the practice is engaged and trustworthy. For a deeper look at how the whole profile fits together, our article on Google Business Profile optimisation for dental practices covers the foundations you should have in place before you start posting.

It is also worth noting that Google Business Profile content is increasingly being picked up and interpreted by AI-powered search tools. Keeping your posts accurate, specific, and up to date therefore has implications beyond traditional local SEO. Our separate article on your Google Business Profile's role in AI answers explores this in more detail if you want to understand the broader picture.

The problem: why most practices do not post consistently

The honest answer is that Google Business Profile posts are easy to deprioritise. They feel less urgent than a full website update, less visible than a social media campaign, and less measurable than paid advertising. When the diary is full and the team is busy, posting on the profile tends to slip.

There is also a content problem. Even practices with good intentions often run out of ideas after the first few posts, or they worry about saying the wrong thing in a regulated environment. What can you say about treatments? What counts as a misleading claim? These are legitimate concerns, and they sometimes cause paralysis.

The result is a profile that looks inactive, which can subtly undermine confidence in an otherwise well-run practice. A prospective patient comparing two local practices may draw conclusions from a profile that has not been updated in several months.

How often should a dental practice post on Google Business Profile?

Once a week is a sensible target for most practices. That is frequent enough to keep the profile looking current, without creating an unmanageable workload. If weekly is not realistic given your resources, aim for a minimum of two to three posts per month. Fewer than that and the profile starts to look neglected.

Posts on Google Business Profile do not disappear immediately, but they are less prominent after a week or so. Consistency over time matters more than the occasional perfectly crafted post. A steady rhythm of relevant, accurate content will serve you better than sporadic bursts of activity.

A practical monthly content plan for dental practices

The following structure gives you a repeatable framework you can adapt each month. It assumes roughly one post per week, with some flexibility around seasonal or practice-specific events.

Week one: a service spotlight

Choose one treatment or service your practice offers and write a short, factual post about it. Keep it informative rather than promotional. For example, a post explaining what Invisalign involves, who it tends to suit, and what a patient might expect at an initial consultation is genuinely useful. It is not making unsubstantiated claims. It is helping someone understand their options.

Rotate through your treatment menu over time. Composite bonding, dental hygiene appointments, emergency appointments, children's dentistry, and implant consultations all make good subjects. Include a clear and honest call to action, such as inviting the reader to book a consultation or call the practice.

Week two: a team or practice update

People choose a dentist partly on the basis of trust, and trust is built by familiarity. A short post introducing a team member, noting a new piece of equipment, or sharing news about updated opening hours helps humanise the practice. These posts do not need to be long. A photograph, a few sentences, and a relevant detail is enough.

If your practice has recently achieved an award, completed a CQC inspection with a positive outcome, or a team member has completed additional training, that is worth sharing. Factual, verifiable achievements are both compliant and credible.

Week three: a patient education post

Educational content tends to perform well on Google Business Profile because it is genuinely useful rather than self-promotional. Topics might include the importance of regular hygiene appointments, how to manage tooth sensitivity, what to do if a filling is lost, or the link between oral health and general wellbeing.

Keep the tone informative and avoid any language that could be read as making guarantees about outcomes. Phrasing like "regular hygiene appointments can help" or "early treatment may reduce the risk of" is both accurate and appropriate. This is not about being overly cautious; it is about being honest, which the GDC and ASA both expect.

Week four: a seasonal or timely post

Use the fourth post each month to respond to something current. In January, that might be a note about booking a check-up for the new year. In October, it could be a factual post about how acidic Halloween treats affect enamel. Around school holiday periods, you might highlight that the practice has appointments suitable for children.

Promotional offers, if your practice runs them, can also feature here, provided they are compliant with ASA advertising standards and do not make misleading comparisons or unsubstantiated claims. Seasonal and time-limited content adds variety and keeps the profile feeling relevant rather than templated.

Practical tips for writing compliant, effective posts

  • Be specific: Vague posts about "great dental care" say very little. A post about a specific treatment, a named team member, or a concrete piece of information gives the reader something useful.
  • Use photographs where possible: Posts with images attract more attention. Use real photos from your practice wherever you can, as generic stock imagery is less convincing.
  • Avoid superlatives and unverifiable claims: Phrases like "the best dentist in Sheffield" or "guaranteed results" are not compliant with GDC and ASA guidance. Stick to factual, verifiable language.
  • Include a call to action: Every post should give the reader a clear next step, whether that is visiting your website, calling the practice, or booking online.
  • Keep posts relatively concise: Google Business Profile posts are not blog articles. Two to four short paragraphs is usually sufficient. Write for someone who is scanning, not reading in depth.
  • Check your links: If you include a link in a post, make sure it goes to the right page on your website and that the page is up to date.

Tracking what is working

Google Business Profile provides basic performance data through its insights section, including views, clicks to your website, and direction requests. It is worth reviewing these figures monthly to get a broad sense of how your profile is performing. Do not read too much into week-by-week fluctuations, but do look for trends over three to six months.

Getting started

If your Google Business Profile posts have lapsed, the best thing to do is restart with a simple plan rather than trying to catch up. Pick a date, map out four posts for the coming month using the framework above, and commit to publishing them on a regular schedule. The compound effect of consistent, relevant posting builds over time.
