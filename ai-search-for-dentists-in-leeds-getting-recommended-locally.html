AI search for dentists in Leeds: getting recommended locally

By Cristian Savulescu, Founder · Published 8 October 2026

AI assistants like ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews and Perplexity are now answering patient searches for dentists in Leeds, often before a patient visits any website. This article explains why some practices appear in those answers and what you can do to improve your chances.

If a prospective patient in Leeds opens ChatGPT tonight and types "best dentist in Leeds for Invisalign" or "NHS dentist accepting new patients in LS6," your practice may or may not appear in the response. The troubling part is that most practice owners have no idea which outcome is happening, and no clear picture of why. This article explains how AI search works in practical terms for Leeds dental practices, where the gaps tend to appear, and what you can do to improve your chances of being recommended.

Why AI assistants are now part of how patients find a dentist

Search behaviour has been shifting for a while, but the arrival of mainstream AI assistants has accelerated it. Tools like ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Microsoft Copilot, Gemini and Perplexity do not simply return a list of links. They synthesise information from multiple sources and present a direct answer, often including specific practice recommendations with supporting context.

For a patient asking a conversational question, this feels natural and helpful. For a dental practice that has not considered how these tools gather and present information, it represents a significant blind spot. Unlike a Google search, where you can see your position in the results page, AI-generated recommendations are largely invisible to you unless you go looking for them.

Leeds is a particularly competitive market. It is a large, diverse city with a dense concentration of dental practices, a sizeable student population, and strong demand for both NHS and private treatment. AI assistants drawing on publicly available information will surface the practices that have the clearest, most consistent, and most authoritative digital presence. If that is not your practice, a competitor benefits.

How AI assistants actually find and use information about local practices

It is worth being straightforward here: the precise ranking factors used by each AI system are not publicly documented, and anyone claiming otherwise is speculating. What we do know, based on how these systems work, is that they draw on a broad range of publicly available signals when forming responses about local businesses.

These typically include:

  • Your Google Business Profile, including your name, address, phone number, categories, services listed, and the content of patient reviews
  • Your website content, particularly how clearly and specifically you describe your services, location, and the patients you serve
  • Third-party directories and review platforms such as Trustpilot, NHS.uk, Yell, and dental-specific platforms
  • Mentions of your practice across other websites, local news coverage, and professional listings
  • The consistency of your practice information across all of these sources

AI systems are trying to give users a reliable answer. That means they gravitate towards practices whose information is consistent, detailed, and corroborated by multiple independent sources. A practice with a well-maintained Google Business Profile, a clear and informative website, and strong reviews across several platforms is better placed to appear in AI-generated recommendations than one with sparse or inconsistent information.

The most common reasons Leeds practices are not appearing in AI search

Thin or vague website content

Many practice websites describe services in broad strokes: "we offer cosmetic dentistry" or "we accept NHS patients." AI assistants are looking for specificity. Which cosmetic treatments? Which NHS services? What makes your approach distinctive? A page that clearly explains your composite bonding process, the brands of aligner you use, or the experience level of your implant clinician gives an AI system much more to work with when forming a response to a patient's specific question.

Inconsistent or incomplete business listings

If your practice name, address, or phone number differs between your website, your Google Business Profile, and your NHS profile, AI assistants encounter conflicting signals. They default to caution, which can mean your practice is less likely to be cited confidently. This is sometimes called a NAP consistency problem (Name, Address, Phone), and it is more common than most practice owners realise.

Low review volume or poor review diversity

Reviews are one of the clearest signals AI systems can use to assess a practice's reputation and the patient experience it delivers. A practice with a large number of detailed, specific reviews across multiple platforms is more credible to an AI assistant than one with a handful of generic comments on a single platform. The content of reviews matters too: patients who mention specific treatments, staff names, or aspects of the experience they valued provide richer data.

No local content strategy

Practices that produce useful, location-specific content, such as a guide to dental implants for Leeds patients, or information about what to expect from NHS dentistry in the city, build topical authority over time. This content is indexed and used by AI systems as evidence that a practice is a genuine, knowledgeable presence in its area. Without it, practices rely entirely on their core pages and business listings, which are rarely enough in a competitive city.

What you can do to improve your chances

Audit your Google Business Profile thoroughly

Your Google Business Profile is one of the first places AI systems look. Make sure every service you offer is listed, your opening hours are accurate and updated for bank holidays, your practice description is detailed, and your photo library is current. Regularly posting updates through the profile also signals that it is actively maintained.

Build out your service pages with genuine depth

Each core treatment you offer deserves its own page with substantive content: how the treatment works, who it is suitable for, what the process involves, and what patients can expect during recovery or aftercare. This is not about keyword density; it is about providing the kind of thorough, trustworthy information that an AI assistant can draw on when a patient asks a specific question. Understanding which questions patients are actually asking is where keyword research for dental practices becomes directly relevant.

Pursue structured local citation building

Make sure your practice is accurately listed on NHS.uk, Dentist.co.uk, the British Dental Association's find-a-dentist tool, Healthgrades, Yell, and any other relevant directories. Each consistent listing strengthens the signal that your practice is a legitimate, established business in Leeds. Our location-specific marketing pages outline how we approach this for practices across South Yorkshire and North Derbyshire.

Develop a sustainable review strategy

Ask satisfied patients to leave reviews, and make it easy for them to do so. A short follow-up message after an appointment with a direct link to your Google Business Profile or preferred review platform removes the friction. Do not incentivise reviews (this breaches GDC guidance), but do make the request a normal part of your post-appointment communication. Responding to reviews, positive and negative alike, also demonstrates engagement and builds trust.

Consider how AI assistants see your geographic relevance

If you are based in a specific part of Leeds, such as Headingley, Chapel Allerton, or Roundhay, making that clear on your website and in your listings can help AI assistants surface your practice for neighbourhood-specific searches. Patients often search with highly local intent, and a practice that clearly signals its location within Leeds, rather than simply saying "Leeds," may be better placed for those queries.
