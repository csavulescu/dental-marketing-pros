Video testimonials for dentists: filming, consent and impact

Video testimonials are one of the most persuasive tools a dental practice can use, yet most practices either never film them or film them badly. This guide covers everything from patient consent to production basics and how to put the footage to work.

Most dental practices have genuinely happy patients. The problem is that happiness stays in the waiting room and never makes it onto the practice website, social media feed or Google Business Profile. Written reviews help, but dental video testimonials do something a five-star rating cannot: they let a real person look directly into the camera and tell a prospective patient, in their own words, that choosing your practice was the right decision. When it works, it is the closest thing to a personal recommendation that digital marketing can produce. When it goes wrong, practices either end up with unusable footage, a compliance headache or content that sits unwatched because it was never distributed properly. This article addresses each of those failure points in turn.

Why most practice owners do not bother, and why that is a missed opportunity

The most common reason practices skip video testimonials is that the whole process feels complicated. Consent forms, filming equipment, editing, GDC compliance, ASA rules on testimonials: the list of things that could go wrong is long enough that many practice managers file the idea under "maybe later" and move on. The second reason is that dentists underestimate how willing satisfied patients actually are to help. If a patient has just had a smile makeover, composite bonding or a course of Invisalign and they are genuinely pleased with the result, many of them will say yes if you ask clearly, simply and without pressure.

The opportunity cost of inaction is real. A prospective patient comparing two practices in the same postcode will spend time on each website. If one practice has three short, authentic patient videos and the other has a stock photo and a block of text, the decision is influenced before a single phone call is made. That influence is not guaranteed to convert every visitor, but it shifts the balance meaningfully, and it does so without paid spend once the content is live.

Consent: getting it right before you press record

Consent is not optional and it is not a box to tick. The GDC's standards on patient confidentiality apply even when a patient is enthusiastically volunteering to appear on your Instagram page. Before filming, every patient must give explicit, documented consent for each specific use of their footage. That means separate, clear consent for use on your website, your social media channels, paid advertising and any third-party marketing materials. Blanket consent that covers everything in a single vague sentence is poor practice and may not hold up if a patient later objects.

What your consent form must cover

  • Full name of the patient and the date consent was given
  • A plain-language description of what will be filmed and what it will show
  • A specific list of where the footage will appear, for example website, Facebook, Instagram, Google Business Profile, paid social adverts
  • Whether the patient's name or any personal details will be used alongside the footage
  • How long the footage will remain in use, and how the patient can withdraw consent
  • A declaration that the patient was not paid or offered a material benefit in exchange for the testimonial

That last point matters under ASA regulations. Offering a discount, a free appointment or any other incentive in exchange for a positive testimonial creates an obligation to disclose the commercial relationship, and it undermines the authenticity that makes video testimonials effective in the first place. Ask for testimonials because the patient is happy, not because you have made it worth their while financially.

Keep signed consent forms on file and link them to the patient record. If a patient later withdraws consent, remove the footage promptly from all channels and document that you did so.

Filming: practical advice for consistent, usable results

You do not need a production studio. You do need to think about four things: location, light, sound and questions.

Location and light

Film in a clean, quiet area of the practice. A treatment room with the chair out of shot, a modern reception area or a dedicated consultation space all work well. Natural light from a window to the side of the subject is flattering and free. Avoid filming with a window directly behind the patient, which creates a silhouette. If natural light is inconsistent, a basic ring light or two soft box lamps costing well under a hundred pounds will solve the problem permanently.

Sound

Sound quality matters more than picture quality. A viewer will forgive a slightly soft image but will click away from muffled or echoey audio within seconds. A lapel microphone that clips onto the patient's collar and connects to the filming device costs very little and transforms audio quality. Avoid filming near air conditioning units, near reception desks with background calls, or anywhere with a hard floor and no soft furnishings, which causes echo.

Questions to ask

Do not hand the patient a script. It will sound like a script. Instead, brief them conversationally beforehand and ask open questions on camera. Useful prompts include: what made you choose this practice, what were you worried about before treatment, and what would you say to someone who is nervous about booking? The answers to those three questions, spoken naturally in a patient's own words, address exactly the concerns a prospective patient has when they land on your website. Keep each video to between sixty and ninety seconds. Anything longer loses most viewers before the end.

What to do with the footage once you have it

Filming the video is the easy part. The harder discipline is distribution, and this is where most practices leave value on the table. A single patient video can legitimately appear in multiple places without feeling repetitive, because different audiences encounter it in different contexts.

Your website

The most important destination is your website. A testimonial video embedded on your homepage or on a treatment-specific page, for example on your Invisalign page or your dental implants page, gives prospective patients social proof at the exact moment they are making a decision. If your website is not currently structured to display video content well, or if it loads slowly on mobile, that is a design and performance problem worth addressing. A website that buries its best content or takes too long to load will undermine the quality of the videos you worked hard to produce. Our dental website design service covers precisely these considerations.

Social media

Post the video natively to Instagram Reels, Facebook and, where relevant, TikTok. Native uploads consistently outperform links to external video platforms in organic reach on most social networks. Add closed captions, because the majority of social media video is watched without sound. Write a short, plain caption that sets context without overpromising. Avoid language such as "life-changing" or "amazing transformation," both because it is hyperbolic and because testimonials that make implied outcome claims can attract ASA scrutiny.

Google Business Profile

Uploading short video clips directly to your Google Business Profile adds visual richness to a listing that prospective patients view before they even reach your website. It is an underused channel that takes minutes to update.

Paid social advertising

If you run paid social campaigns, video testimonials consistently outperform still image creatives in dental advertising because they combine social proof with storytelling. A sixty-second patient video used as a Facebook or Instagram ad, targeted to people within a ten to fifteen mile radius of the practice, puts authentic peer recommendation in front of a cold audience at relatively low cost. Ensure the consent form specifically covers paid advertising use before you run the footage in this way.

Where practices go wrong, and how a specialist agency helps

The most common failure modes are not technical. They are systemic. The practice films one video in 2022, posts it once, gets a reasonable response, and then nothing happens for two years because no one has ownership of the process. Video testimonials work best when they are collected consistently, edited to a house style, distributed to a plan and refreshed regularly so that the content on your channels reflects current treatments, current patients and current seasons.

A dental marketing agency that handles content production, social media management and website integration can build that system for you rather than leaving it to a busy practice manager who has fifteen other priorities. That means a consistent filming schedule aligned to your busiest treatment categories, consent processes that are reviewed for compliance, edited videos that carry your brand and captions written to maximise reach without breaching advertising standards. The distinction between doing this ad hoc and doing it as part of a managed strategy is the difference between having a handful of dated videos and having a library of current, on-brand social proof that works for the practice around the clock.

A practical starting point

If your practice has never filmed a patient testimonial, start small. Identify three patients whose treatment results you are proud of, whose personalities are warm and open, and who you believe would say yes if asked. Prepare a simple one-page consent form and a filming checklist. Book fifteen minutes after their final appointment to film. Edit in-house or pass the footage to your marketing team. Upload to your website and your social channels within a week so the momentum does not stall.

If you have filmed testimonials before but they are not generating the visibility or enquiries you expected, the issue is almost certainly distribution and integration rather than the videos themselves. Audit where your footage currently lives and whether your website and social channels are set up to make the most of it.
