Conversion tracking setup for dental Google Ads (GA4)

By Cristian Savulescu, Founder · Published 27 August 2026

If your Google Ads campaigns are running but you're not certain which clicks turn into booked appointments, your conversion tracking is almost certainly broken or missing entirely. This guide explains how to fix it properly using GA4.

Running Google Ads without reliable conversion tracking is like fitting new equipment in your surgery and never checking whether it improves patient outcomes. You spend the money, you assume it is working, but you have no way to prove it. For dental practice owners across South Yorkshire and North Derbyshire, this is one of the most common and costly mistakes we see. Budgets are wasted on campaigns that look healthy inside Google Ads but are delivering no measurable return, because the dental Google Ads conversion tracking was never set up correctly in the first place.

This article walks you through exactly what correct conversion tracking looks like, why the setup so often goes wrong, and what a properly configured GA4 and Google Ads account should include. If you have ever looked at your Ads dashboard and felt uncertain whether those clicks are translating into real appointment enquiries, this is the guide you need.

Why Conversion Tracking Matters More Than Click Volume

Google Ads will always give you data. The problem is that without conversion tracking, the data it gives you is largely cosmetic. Impressions, clicks, and click-through rates tell you whether people are seeing and clicking your ads. They tell you almost nothing about whether those people are booking consultations, filling in your contact form, or picking up the phone.

Dental practices typically spend meaningful monthly budgets on paid search. Without knowing which campaigns, ad groups, keywords, and individual ads are actually generating enquiries, you cannot make sensible decisions about where to increase spend or where to cut. You are, in effect, flying blind.

Conversion tracking closes that gap. When it is set up correctly, you can see precisely which search term triggered an ad, which ad the user clicked, and what action they took on your website afterwards. That level of insight changes everything about how you manage and optimise a campaign.

The Most Common Reasons Dental Conversion Tracking Fails

In our experience working with dental practices on Google Ads management, broken or absent conversion tracking almost always comes down to one of four problems.

1. The tracking code was installed incorrectly

The Google tag (formerly known as the global site tag) needs to be present on every page of your website, and the specific event snippet needs to fire only on the correct confirmation or thank-you page. A common mistake is installing the event snippet on the contact page itself rather than the confirmation page that loads after a form is submitted. This means every visit to your contact page is counted as a conversion, whether or not anyone actually got in touch.

2. Phone call conversions are not being tracked

For most dental practices, a significant proportion of new patient enquiries arrive by telephone rather than online form. If your conversion tracking only records form submissions, you are missing a large chunk of your actual results. Google Ads includes a call conversion feature that can track calls made directly from your ad or from a number displayed on your website after a click. Without this, your campaign data is incomplete.

3. GA4 and Google Ads are not linked properly

Google Analytics 4 and Google Ads are separate platforms. Linking them is a deliberate step that many practices skip or configure incorrectly. Without a proper link, you cannot import GA4 goals into Google Ads as conversions, and you lose the ability to use audience data from GA4 for remarketing or smart bidding.

4. All actions are marked as primary conversions

If you have set up tracking but marked every interaction, including page views, PDF downloads, and social media link clicks, as a primary conversion, Google's smart bidding algorithms will optimise towards all of them equally. This dilutes the signal. Your campaigns end up chasing low-value interactions rather than the appointment enquiries and phone calls that actually matter to your business.

How to Set Up GA4 Conversion Tracking for Dental Google Ads

The following is a high-level overview of the correct process. Some steps require technical access to your website and a working knowledge of Google Tag Manager. If your current setup has not been audited recently, it is worth doing that before launching any new campaigns.

Step 1: Install Google Tag Manager on your website

Google Tag Manager (GTM) is a free tool that lets you manage all your tracking tags from a single interface without editing your website code directly for every change. Install the GTM container snippet across all pages of your site. From this point forward, all tracking tags, including GA4 and Google Ads conversion tags, can be deployed and managed through GTM.

Step 2: Configure your GA4 property and key events

Inside GA4, set up the events that matter to your practice. For most dental websites, these will include:

  • Form submission on the contact or appointment request page
  • Clicks on your telephone number
  • Clicks on an email address link
  • Visits to the booking confirmation or thank-you page

Once these events are firing correctly, mark the ones that represent genuine patient enquiries as key events within GA4. These are what GA4 treats as conversions in its reporting.

Step 3: Link GA4 to your Google Ads account

Within GA4, navigate to Admin, then Google Ads Links, and connect your Ads account. Once linked, you can import your GA4 key events directly into Google Ads as conversion actions. This is preferable to using native Google Ads conversion tracking alone because it gives you a single, consistent source of truth across both platforms.

Step 4: Set up call tracking

Inside Google Ads, enable call extensions (now called call assets) on your campaigns and configure call conversion tracking. You can track calls that last longer than a specified duration, typically 60 seconds for a dental practice context, to filter out misdials and short calls that are unlikely to represent genuine enquiries. If you want to track calls from your website number specifically, you will need to configure a Google forwarding number.

Step 5: Assign conversion priorities correctly

In Google Ads, review each conversion action and set the ones tied to direct patient enquiries as primary conversions. Set everything else, such as page views or brochure downloads, to secondary. This ensures that when you use a smart bidding strategy such as Target CPA or Maximise Conversions, the algorithm is optimising for the outcomes that actually grow your practice.

Step 6: Test everything before going live

Use the Tag Assistant Chrome extension and GA4's real-time report to verify that events are firing correctly. Submit a test form, click your phone number link, and confirm that the corresponding events appear in GA4 within a few seconds. Do not rely on historical data to confirm this. Test it yourself, on a fresh browser session, before spending any budget.

What Correct Tracking Unlocks for Your Campaign

Once your dental Google Ads conversion tracking is working accurately, your entire approach to campaign management can become more precise. You will be able to see which services generate the most enquiries, whether that is dental implants, Invisalign, teeth whitening, or emergency appointments, and allocate budget accordingly. You will know which keywords are genuinely profitable and which are consuming spend without delivering results.

Accurate conversion data also unlocks the full potential of Google's smart bidding strategies. These algorithms adjust your bids in real time based on signals like device, location, time of day, and user behaviour. But they are only as good as the conversion data they are trained on. Feed them incomplete or inaccurate data and they will make poor decisions. Feed them clean, consistent data and they become a genuine competitive advantage.

Beyond optimisation, reliable tracking means you can have honest, evidence-based conversations about what your marketing budget is actually delivering. You move from gut feeling to provable results.

How a Specialist Dental Marketing Agency Approaches This Differently

Most dental practices either have no tracking in place, or have tracking that was set up quickly when a campaign launched and has never been audited since. Website changes, platform updates, and new landing pages frequently break existing tags without anyone noticing.

When we onboard a new practice at Dental Marketing Pros, a full conversion tracking audit is one of the first things we carry out. We verify that every key event is firing correctly, that phone call conversions are captured, and that GA4 and Google Ads are exchanging data cleanly. Where tracking is broken, we fix it before any meaningful budget is deployed.

We also structure conversion priorities in a way that aligns with each practice's specific goals. A practice focused on growing its implant list has different priorities from one that wants to fill short-notice appointments. The tracking setup reflects those priorities, so the campaigns optimise towards what actually matters.

Ongoing, we monitor conversion data week to week. If conversion volumes drop unexpectedly, that is often the first signal that a tracking tag has broken due to a website update. Catching this quickly means less wasted spend and faster recovery.
