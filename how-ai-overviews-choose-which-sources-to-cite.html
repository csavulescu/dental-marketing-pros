How AI Overviews choose which sources to cite

By Cristian Savulescu, Founder · Published 13 November 2026

AI tools like Google's AI Overviews, ChatGPT and Perplexity are increasingly shaping how patients find dental practices, yet most practice owners have no idea how these systems decide which sources to cite. This article explains what drives those decisions and what you can do to improve your chances of being included.

If you have searched for a service on Google recently, you will have noticed the AI-generated summary that now appears at the top of many results pages. Google calls this feature AI Overviews. ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot and Perplexity do something similar. These tools pull together information from across the web and present a synthesised answer, often without the user needing to scroll any further. For dental practices, this shift raises an urgent question: how do these AI systems decide which sources to cite, and what can you do to give your practice a better chance of appearing in them?

This is not a niche technical concern. Patients are already using AI assistants to research dentists, compare treatments and decide who to call. If the sources being cited are your competitors and not you, that is a problem worth addressing now, before the gap widens further.

What "being cited" actually means in AI search

When an AI Overview or a generative search tool produces an answer, it draws on content it has either indexed directly or retrieved in real time. In some cases it shows a list of sources; in others it incorporates information silently, without attribution. Either way, the content that gets used tends to share a set of common characteristics.

Being cited does not mean ranking first in traditional organic search, though the two are related. A page that ranks well for a specific query is more likely to be pulled into an AI-generated answer, but ranking alone is not sufficient. The AI also evaluates whether your content is structured clearly, written authoritatively, and relevant to the specific intent behind the search.

Why AI systems favour certain sources over others

No AI company publishes an exact formula for how it selects sources, and anyone who claims otherwise is guessing. What we can observe, based on the content that tends to be cited consistently, is a cluster of factors that appear to carry significant weight.

Clarity of expertise and authorship

AI tools are built to surface trustworthy information. For healthcare content, that means they tend to favour sources that make it clear who wrote the content and what qualifies them to write it. A page written by a named GDC-registered dentist, with a clear author bio, is more credible to both a human reader and an AI system than a page with no attribution at all. This is closely aligned with Google's long-standing emphasis on Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness and Trustworthiness, commonly referred to as E-E-A-T.

Structure and scannability

Generative AI tools work by processing text efficiently. Content that is broken into logical sections with descriptive headings, that uses bullet points where appropriate, and that answers questions in a direct and self-contained way is far easier for an AI to extract and quote accurately. Dense blocks of text with no internal structure are harder to parse and less likely to be pulled into a summary.

Specificity and depth

Thin content, the kind that says very little in many words, rarely gets cited. AI tools look for content that genuinely answers a question. A page that explains what Invisalign treatment involves, how long it takes, what it costs in approximate terms, and what a patient should expect at their first consultation is far more useful than a page that simply announces you offer Invisalign. The more specifically and accurately your content answers likely patient questions, the more usable it is.

Domain authority and backlink quality

Established, well-linked websites tend to be treated as more credible sources. This does not mean you need thousands of backlinks. A modest number of high-quality links from relevant, reputable sources, such as local business directories, dental trade publications, or health organisations, can help signal that your site is a legitimate authority in its field.

Local relevance signals

For location-specific queries, such as "NHS dentist in Sheffield" or "dental implants Chesterfield," AI tools draw on local signals including your Google Business Profile, local citations, and the geographic specificity of your website content. If your site does not clearly and repeatedly establish where you are and what you offer in that location, you are less likely to be surfaced for those searches.

The common mistakes that keep dental practices out of AI citations

Most dental practice websites were built to look attractive and load quickly, which are still important things. But they were rarely built with AI consumption in mind. The most common issues we see include:

  • Generic service pages that describe treatments in broad terms without addressing specific patient questions or concerns.
  • No clear author attribution on clinical content, leaving AI systems unable to assess the credibility of the source.
  • Weak geographic signals, particularly on practices that serve multiple locations but have only a single generic "contact us" page.
  • Outdated content that has not been refreshed to reflect current treatment options, pricing guidance, or NHS availability.
  • No structured FAQ content, which is one of the most direct ways to match the question-based queries that AI tools are designed to answer.
  • Poor mobile performance or slow page speed, which affects how thoroughly search systems crawl and index your content.

These are not obscure technical problems. They are fixable, and fixing them can meaningfully improve how AI systems perceive and use your content.

What a structured approach to GEO looks like in practice

Generative Engine Optimisation, or GEO, is the emerging discipline of optimising content specifically to perform well in AI-driven search environments. It builds on traditional SEO but extends it to account for how language models retrieve and synthesise information. Our GEO services are designed specifically for this environment, combining content strategy, technical optimisation and local authority-building in a way that is tailored to dental practices.

In practical terms, a structured GEO approach for a dental practice typically involves the following.

Content restructuring

Existing service pages are reviewed and rewritten to answer specific patient questions clearly and directly. Headings are restructured to match likely search queries. FAQ sections are added where appropriate. Author attribution is added or strengthened throughout.

Local authority development

Your Google Business Profile is audited and optimised. Local citation consistency is checked across directories. Location-specific content is developed for the areas you serve, ensuring that AI tools can clearly identify your geographic relevance.

Technical health

Page speed, mobile usability and crawlability are assessed. Schema markup, which helps search systems understand what type of content a page contains, is implemented where it is missing. These are signals that both traditional search engines and AI tools use to evaluate content.

Ongoing content development

AI tools favour sources that are regularly updated and that demonstrate ongoing expertise. A content calendar built around patient questions, treatment guides and local relevance keeps your site active and credible over time.

Why specialist dental marketing support makes a difference

GEO is not something that sits neatly within the remit of a general web agency or a practice manager already stretched across administration, compliance and clinical governance. It requires a working knowledge of how AI search systems behave, an understanding of GDC and ASA guidelines as they apply to dental content, and the ability to write and structure content that is both clinically appropriate and genuinely useful to patients.

Getting it wrong, by making unsubstantiated claims, using misleading before-and-after framing, or producing content that falls foul of advertising standards, creates regulatory risk alongside any potential marketing benefit. Specialist dental marketing support manages both sides of that equation.

It is also worth being realistic. No agency can guarantee that your practice will be cited in AI Overviews or recommended by ChatGPT. These systems make their own decisions based on constantly evolving criteria. What specialist support can do is ensure your content, your site structure, your local presence and your authority signals are as strong as they can reasonably be, giving you the best available foundation to be considered.
