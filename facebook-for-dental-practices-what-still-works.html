Facebook for dental practices: what still works

By Cristian Savulescu, Founder · Published 14 October 2026

Facebook remains one of the most cost-effective channels for reaching local dental patients, but only if you use it correctly. This guide cuts through the noise and shows UK practice owners exactly what still delivers results.

If you have been running a Facebook page for your dental practice and quietly wondering whether it is still worth the effort, you are not alone. Organic reach has fallen sharply over the past several years, and many practice owners have concluded that Facebook is dead for healthcare businesses. The reality is more nuanced. Facebook is not dead; it has simply changed. Practices that understand those changes and adapt accordingly are still generating consistent enquiries from it. Those that treat their page as a noticeboard are the ones struggling. This article sets out what still works, why so many practices are getting it wrong, and what a more disciplined approach looks like in practice.

Why most Facebook dental practice pages underperform

The most common pattern we see when we review a practice's social presence is a page that has been updated sporadically, often with stock images, generic oral health tips copied from elsewhere, and the occasional before-and-after photo with no context. Posts go up, receive a handful of likes from existing patients and members of staff, and generate no meaningful enquiries. The practice owner concludes Facebook does not work and either stops posting altogether or carries on with the same low-effort content out of habit.

The problem is rarely the platform. It is the strategy, or rather the absence of one.

Facebook's algorithm has always prioritised content that generates genuine engagement, primarily comments and shares rather than passive likes. When a page posts content that does not prompt a reaction, the algorithm suppresses it. Fewer people see it. Fewer still act on it. Over time, the page's organic reach shrinks to near zero, and the practice is essentially invisible to anyone who has not already found them.

There is also a targeting problem. Even practices that do invest in boosting posts often do so without a clear sense of who they are trying to reach, what action they want that person to take, or how to measure whether any of it is working.

What still works on Facebook in 2025

Locally rooted content that reflects real patient concerns

Generic dental tips, "did you know your enamel is the hardest substance in the body" style posts, perform poorly because they offer no reason for a local person to engage or to choose your practice. Content that performs well tends to be rooted in your specific community and in the genuine questions your existing patients ask you every week.

Think about the conversations that happen in your surgery. Patients ask about the cost of Invisalign compared to traditional braces. They worry about whether a particular treatment will hurt. They want to know what to expect at their first appointment after avoiding the dentist for years. These are the topics that generate comments, shares, and saves, because they reflect real anxieties and decisions rather than trivia.

Video content, even short-form clips recorded on a smartphone, consistently outperforms static images when it shows a real person from the practice speaking naturally. Introductions from associate dentists, short explainers filmed in the waiting room, and behind-the-scenes content all tend to generate meaningfully more engagement than a designed graphic with a tooth on it.

A consistent posting rhythm over a sporadic burst

One of the clearest patterns in page performance data is that consistency matters more than frequency. A page that posts three times a week reliably will almost always outperform one that posts ten times one week and then goes quiet for a month. The algorithm rewards pages that demonstrate consistent activity, and so do potential patients who visit your page to assess whether your practice is active and professional before getting in touch.

The practical implication is that it is better to plan a realistic schedule you can maintain, two or three posts a week including one piece of genuinely useful content, one piece of social or community content, and one post with a clear treatment focus, than to try to post daily and burn out within a fortnight.

Paid advertising targeted properly

Organic reach alone is no longer sufficient for most practices to generate significant new patient volume from Facebook. However, the paid advertising side of the platform remains highly effective when used correctly. The targeting options available, by location, age, household income indicators, and interest profiles, allow a well-configured campaign to put a treatment message in front of exactly the kind of person likely to book.

This is where many practices waste money. Boosting posts without defining a proper audience, running ads with no clear offer or call to action, and failing to track what happens after someone clicks an ad are the three most common errors. If you are spending money on Facebook ads and you cannot tell precisely which ad produced which enquiry, your budget is not working as hard as it should be.

For practices promoting specific treatments, targeted campaigns can be highly effective. For example, a structured approach to Facebook ad targeting for Invisalign patients, step by step looks quite different from a generic awareness campaign, and produces correspondingly better results.

Retargeting: the underused tool

The majority of people who see a Facebook ad for a dental practice will not book on first contact. They may click through to the website, read about a treatment, and then close the tab and forget about it. Retargeting campaigns, which serve ads specifically to people who have already visited your website or engaged with your page, allow you to stay in front of those warm prospects at a fraction of the cost of cold audience advertising.

Very few independent practices use retargeting effectively, which means it represents a genuine competitive advantage for those that do. Setting it up requires a Facebook pixel installed correctly on your website and an understanding of how to create custom audiences, but once in place, it tends to produce a lower cost per enquiry than almost any other Facebook tactic.

Reviews and social proof woven into content

Facebook remains a platform where patients check a practice's credibility before booking. A page with no recent activity, no reviews visible, and no human faces is a deterrent rather than an asset. Sharing patient feedback, where you have appropriate consent and comply with GDC guidance on testimonials, publishing responses to Google reviews via your page, and showcasing treatment outcomes in a transparent, accurate way all contribute to the sense that your practice is active, trustworthy, and worth contacting.

What a specialist agency does differently

When practice owners manage their own Facebook presence, the most common failure mode is not lack of effort. It is lack of time to think strategically between patients, admin, and running a business. Content gets posted reactively rather than as part of a planned funnel. Ad campaigns are set up once and left running without optimisation. Results are checked irregularly, and there is no clear link between what is being spent and what is being generated.

A specialist dental marketing agency approaches Facebook as one channel within a broader patient acquisition strategy. Content is planned around treatment priorities, seasonal patterns, and the competitive landscape in your specific area. Ad campaigns are structured with defined audiences, creative variations that are tested against each other, and clear conversion tracking that shows exactly what each campaign produces. Retargeting is set up from the outset, not added as an afterthought.

It is also worth noting that Facebook is rarely the whole picture. Practices that grow sustainably tend to combine social activity with strong local search visibility. Understanding how long dental SEO takes to work and what realistic timelines look like helps you plan a marketing mix that delivers results at different stages of the patient journey, not just one channel in isolation.

Common objections, answered plainly

"Our patients are older and not on Facebook"

Facebook's user base in the UK skews older than most practice owners expect. It remains the dominant social platform for adults over 35, which is precisely the demographic that books most restorative, cosmetic, and orthodontic treatment. The assumption that Facebook is only for younger audiences is not supported by current usage patterns.

"We tried Facebook ads and wasted money"

This is the most common objection we hear, and in most cases when we look at what was actually run, the campaign was either too broadly targeted, had no clear call to action, ran for too short a period to generate meaningful data, or had no conversion tracking in place. A failed Facebook ad campaign is almost always a targeting or setup problem, not a platform problem.

"We do not have time to create content consistently"

This is the most legitimate objection, and it is the clearest argument for working with a specialist. Content creation, scheduling, ad management, and performance reporting are time-consuming when done properly. Delegating them allows you to focus on clinical work while the marketing runs systematically in the background.

Is Facebook right for your practice?

Facebook is not the right primary channel for every practice in every situation. A practice targeting a largely younger, tech-native demographic might find Instagram or TikTok more effective for certain treatments. A practice whose primary growth priority is local search ranking might invest first in SEO before expanding into paid social. The right answer depends on your current patient profile, your treatment priorities, your local competitive environment, and your budget.

What is rarely the right answer is ignoring Facebook entirely or running a page in a half-hearted way that consumes time without producing results. Done properly, a Facebook dental practice presence can be one of the most cost-effective ways to reach the right local patients at the moment they are considering treatment.
