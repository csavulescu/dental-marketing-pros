Call tracking for dental ads: stop wasting budget on lost calls

Most dental practices running paid ads track clicks and impressions but have no idea how many of those clicks actually result in a phone call or a booked appointment. This article explains how call tracking closes that gap and why it matters for every pound you spend on PPC.

You check your Google Ads dashboard and the numbers look reasonable. Clicks are coming in, the cost-per-click sits within budget, and impressions are healthy. But the appointment book is not filling the way you expected. Something is falling through the gap between a user clicking your ad and that person sitting in your chair. In most cases, the gap is the phone call, and without proper call tracking in place, you have no reliable way to know whether your ads are working at all.

The problem: clicks are not the same as patients

When a prospective patient searches for a dentist in Sheffield, Rotherham, Chesterfield or any of the surrounding towns, there is a good chance they will pick up the phone rather than fill in a form. Dental services are personal decisions. People want to ask about nervous patient appointments, check whether you accept their insurance, or simply hear a friendly voice before they commit to visiting. Forms feel clinical. Phone calls feel human.

This means that a significant portion of conversions from dental PPC campaigns happen by phone, not by web form. If your campaign is only tracking form submissions or website clicks, you are measuring, at best, a fraction of the results your ads are actually producing. Worse, you could be pausing or cutting budgets on campaigns that are quietly generating a steady stream of inbound calls, simply because those calls are invisible in your analytics.

The reverse is equally damaging. You might be spending confidently on a campaign or keyword group that drives plenty of calls, but if those calls are going unanswered, going to voicemail, or being handled poorly at the front desk, you are paying for opportunities you are not converting. Without call data, you cannot distinguish between a campaign that is failing and a front-desk process that is failing. They look identical in a standard Ads report.

Why this happens in most dental practices

Call tracking is not complicated in principle, but it is easy to overlook when you are setting up a PPC campaign quickly. The most common reason practices lack it is simply that no one set it up. Google Ads does offer a basic call reporting feature, but it has meaningful limitations. It counts calls made directly from ads using a forwarding number, but it does not always capture calls that come in after someone clicks through to your website and then dials the number they see there. That second type of call, known as a post-click call, often represents your warmest leads, the people who took the time to read your site before contacting you.

Another reason is that many practices rely on general-purpose web agencies or use off-the-shelf ad templates that are not configured with the dental patient journey in mind. A call-to-action optimised for an e-commerce brand will not behave the same way as one built for a local healthcare provider. The intent is different, the device behaviour is different, and the conversion path is different.

There is also the attribution problem. If a patient searches for "emergency dentist Doncaster," clicks your ad on their mobile, calls the practice, and books, that conversion needs to be attributed correctly back to the exact keyword, ad group, and campaign that triggered it. Without dynamic number insertion (more on this below), that chain breaks, and you lose the ability to make informed decisions about where to direct your budget.

The fix: how call tracking actually works in a dental PPC setup

Dynamic number insertion

The foundation of proper call tracking for dental PPC is dynamic number insertion, usually abbreviated to DNI. The way it works is straightforward: a small JavaScript snippet sits on your website and detects how a visitor arrived. If they came through a paid Google Ads campaign, the phone number displayed on your site automatically swaps to a unique tracking number assigned to that campaign, or even to that specific keyword. When they call that number, it forwards seamlessly to your practice, and the system logs the call against the correct source.

This means you can see, inside your reporting dashboard, exactly how many calls each campaign generated, how long each call lasted, whether it was a first-time caller or a repeat contact, and at what time of day most calls come in. None of this requires any change to the patient's experience. They dial what looks like a normal local number, and they reach you as usual.

Call recording and quality scoring

Duration alone does not tell you much. A two-minute call could be a patient booking a full smile makeover, or it could be someone asking if you do NHS check-ups and being told you are private-only. Recording calls, with clear notice to callers in compliance with relevant data protection requirements, allows you and your agency to review what is actually happening on those calls. You can identify whether high-volume keywords are attracting the right patients, spot recurring questions that suggest a gap in your website content, and assess whether your reception team is converting enquiries effectively.

Some call tracking platforms allow you to tag calls by outcome, so a call can be marked as a booking, an enquiry, a cancellation, or not relevant. Over time this builds a clear picture of true cost per acquisition, not just cost per call, which is a far more meaningful metric when you are trying to justify or scale a PPC budget.

Feeding data back into Google Ads

The most powerful step is importing call conversion data back into your Google Ads account so that the bidding algorithms have accurate information to work with. Google's automated bidding strategies, including Target CPA and Maximise Conversions, are only as good as the conversion data you feed them. If you are only feeding in form submissions, the algorithm will optimise for form submissions. Add qualified phone calls to the mix and the algorithm starts to learn which audiences, times, devices and keywords generate real patient enquiries, and it adjusts bids accordingly.

This feedback loop is where a well-configured call tracking setup pays for itself many times over. The campaign becomes progressively more efficient rather than stagnating at its initial performance level.

What a specialist dental PPC agency does differently

There is a meaningful difference between running Google Ads for a dental practice and running PPC for a general retail or service business. The patient journey, the compliance considerations, and the local competition dynamics all require a specific approach. Our dental PPC service is built around that specificity.

A general agency may install a call tracking tool and consider the job done. A specialist approach goes further. It means mapping the call tracking configuration to the actual treatment types you offer, setting call duration thresholds that reflect the realistic length of a genuine new patient enquiry (a 45-second call is unlikely to be a booking), and building reporting that your practice manager can actually read and act on without needing to interpret raw marketing data.

It also means being alert to compliance. Dental advertising in the UK is regulated by the GDC and the ASA. Any claims made in ad copy, landing pages, or call scripts need to be accurate and not misleading. Recorded calls can help confirm that front-desk language stays within appropriate boundaries, which is a benefit that extends well beyond marketing performance.

Beyond setup, a specialist agency reviews call data as a routine part of campaign management, not as an occasional audit. Patterns in call times can inform bid scheduling adjustments. High call volumes from certain postcodes can support geographic bid increases. Calls that frequently ask the same question can prompt a landing page update that removes friction before the call even happens.

What good reporting looks like

If you are currently running dental PPC without call tracking, your monthly report probably shows impressions, clicks, click-through rate, and maybe form conversions. That is a partial picture at best. A properly instrumented campaign report should also show:

  • Total inbound calls generated by paid campaigns, broken down by campaign and keyword
  • Call duration distribution, so you can distinguish brief misdials from genuine enquiries
  • Time-of-day and day-of-week call patterns, so you can align staffing or bid schedules
  • First-time caller rate, which helps you understand how much of your call volume represents genuinely new patient interest
  • Calls by device type, since mobile and desktop callers often behave differently and may respond to different ad formats
  • Missed call rate, which is one of the most important numbers in the entire report

That last point deserves emphasis. If your ads are generating calls but a meaningful proportion of those calls are going unanswered, you are paying for leads you are actively losing. No amount of optimisation on the ad side will compensate for a systemic problem at the point of first contact. Call tracking makes this visible, and once it is visible, it can be fixed.

A straightforward way to start

If you are not currently running call tracking alongside your dental PPC campaigns, the place to start is an honest audit of what you are and are not measuring. Look at your current conversion setup in Google Ads and ask whether it accounts for phone calls originating from your website, not just clicks on call-only ads. Check whether your reporting gives you any information about call quality or outcome. If the answer to either question is no, you have a gap in your data that is likely costing you budget.

You do not need to overhaul your entire marketing setup to address this. In most cases, implementing dynamic number insertion, connecting it to your Google Ads account, and establishing a basic call tagging process can be done relatively quickly and without disrupting your existing campaigns.
