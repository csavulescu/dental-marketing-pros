Schema markup for dentists: helping Google understand your site

Schema markup is one of the most overlooked technical SEO tools available to dental practices, yet it can significantly improve how Google reads and displays your website. This article explains what it is, why most practice websites get it wrong, and how to fix it properly.

If you have ever searched for a local business on Google and seen a result that displays star ratings, opening hours, an address, and a phone number all directly in the search listing, you have seen schema markup doing its job. For dental practices trying to attract new patients in competitive local markets, this kind of visibility is not a luxury. It is a genuine competitive advantage. Yet the majority of dental websites across South Yorkshire and North Derbyshire are running without any meaningful schema implementation at all, which means Google is making its best guess about what your practice does, who you serve, and where you are located. That guess is often incomplete.

This article walks you through what dental schema markup SEO actually involves, why so many practice websites fall short, and what a properly implemented schema strategy looks like in practice.

What Schema Markup Actually Is

Schema markup is structured data, a specific vocabulary of code added to your website that tells search engines precisely what each piece of content means, not just what it says. It is written in a format called JSON-LD (the format Google recommends) and sits quietly in the background of your web pages, invisible to visitors but highly readable by search engine crawlers.

Without schema, Google reads your homepage and makes inferences. It can usually work out that you are a dental practice, but it may not be certain which services you offer, whether you accept NHS or private patients, what your exact location is relative to a searcher's query, or how your practice is rated by existing patients. Schema removes that ambiguity by giving Google explicit, machine-readable answers.

The vocabulary used for schema markup is maintained at Schema.org, an open standard supported by Google, Bing, and other major search engines. For dental practices, the most relevant schema types include LocalBusiness (and its more specific subtype Dentist), MedicalBusiness, FAQPage, Service, and BreadcrumbList.

Why Dental Websites So Often Get This Wrong

The problem is not usually ignorance of schema markup's existence. Many practice owners have heard the term mentioned in passing. The problem is threefold.

Generic website templates

A large proportion of dental websites are built on generic healthcare or small business templates. These templates may include very basic schema, often just a generic LocalBusiness type, but they rarely use the more specific Dentist schema type, and they almost never include service-level or FAQ schema. If your website was built without a dental-specific technical foundation, the chances are your schema is either absent or unhelpfully vague. This is one of the reasons why investing in purpose-built dental web design matters so much from the outset.

Incomplete or inaccurate data

Even when schema is present, it is frequently incomplete. Common omissions include missing geo-coordinates, incorrect or inconsistent opening hours, no price range indication, absent telephone numbers, and no connection between the schema data and the practice's Google Business Profile. Google cross-references structured data against other signals, so inconsistencies actively undermine the benefit.

Set-and-forget implementation

Schema markup is not a one-time task. When you add a new service, change your opening hours, start offering a new treatment, or update your fees, your schema needs to reflect that. Most practices that do have some schema in place have never revisited it since the website launched.

What Properly Implemented Dental Schema Looks Like

A well-structured dental schema implementation covers several layers. Here is what each layer contributes and why it matters for local search visibility.

Dentist schema on your homepage and contact page

The foundation is a correctly specified Dentist schema block, which is a subtype of MedicalBusiness within the Schema.org hierarchy. This should include your practice's legal name, full address with postcode, telephone number, email address, opening hours for each day of the week, geographic coordinates (latitude and longitude), your website URL, and a link to your Google Business Profile. The sameAs property is particularly useful here because it tells Google that your website, your Google Business Profile, and any other authoritative listings are all the same entity.

Getting this entity information correct and consistent is the backbone of local dental schema markup SEO. It is also directly connected to how Google decides to surface your practice in map pack results for searches like "dentist in Sheffield" or "private dentist Chesterfield".

Service schema for individual treatments

Each service page on your website, whether for dental implants, Invisalign, composite bonding, or routine check-ups, should carry its own Service schema block. This tells Google not just that you offer these treatments but that they are distinct, definable services with their own descriptions. Combined with well-written, unique content on each page, this significantly improves the chance that Google will surface the correct page for treatment-specific queries, rather than defaulting to your homepage for everything.

FAQ schema

FAQ schema is one of the most immediately rewarding schema types to implement because it can produce rich results directly in the search listing, showing expanded questions and answers beneath your URL. For dental practices, this is particularly valuable for treatment pages. Questions such as "How long do dental implants last?" or "Is Invisalign suitable for adults?" are exactly the kind of queries prospective patients use when they are in the research phase, and having those answers visible before someone even clicks your link builds trust and improves click-through rates.

It is worth noting that Google does not guarantee it will display FAQ rich results even when the markup is correctly implemented. It exercises editorial discretion. But correct implementation gives you the opportunity where no implementation gives you none.

Breadcrumb schema

Breadcrumb schema is a smaller but useful addition. It tells Google the hierarchical structure of your site, for example: Home, Treatments, Cosmetic Dentistry, Composite Bonding. When Google displays your pages in search results, it can use this to show a cleaner, more informative URL path beneath your page title, which makes listings look more organised and professional to prospective patients.

Aggregate rating schema

If your website hosts genuine patient reviews, and they are clearly sourced and editorially maintained in line with GDC guidance on testimonials, AggregateRating schema can be added to reflect the overall rating and number of reviews. This can produce star rating displays in search results. One important note: Google's own guidelines explicitly prohibit self-serving review schema where you are marking up reviews you have curated without clear sourcing. Implementation must be compliant and honest.

How to Check Whether Your Practice Has Schema Issues

Google provides a free tool called the Rich Results Test, available at search.google.com/test/rich-results. Enter your website URL and it will show you what structured data Google can detect, and whether any errors or warnings are present. Google Search Console also has a dedicated section for rich results that shows whether your schema is generating impressions.

Common errors include missing required fields, incorrect property names, and schema types that do not match the actual content of the page. These errors do not necessarily cause your site to disappear from search results, but they prevent you from benefiting from the enhanced listings that correct implementation enables.

Why a Specialist Agency Does This More Effectively

Dental schema markup SEO sits at the intersection of technical SEO, local search strategy, and healthcare-specific compliance considerations. A generalist web developer may know how to write JSON-LD but lack the local SEO context to connect your schema strategy to your Google Business Profile, your citation profile, and your service page architecture in a coherent way.

A specialist dental marketing agency brings several specific advantages. First, the schema implementation is designed around the actual search behaviour of patients in your area, not generic best practice. Second, it is built to be consistent with your broader dental SEO strategy, so your structured data reinforces rather than contradicts your keyword targeting and content structure. Third, it is maintained as your practice evolves. New services, changed hours, updated pricing information and seasonal promotions all need to be reflected in your schema, not just your visible website content.

There is also the matter of compliance. The GDC and ASA both govern how dental practices can present themselves and their services, including online. Schema markup that inadvertently makes unsubstantiated claims or displays testimonials in a misleading way can create regulatory risk, not just technical SEO problems. Getting schema right requires someone who understands both the technical requirements and the professional boundaries.

The Bigger Picture

Schema markup is not a magic ranking switch. It is one component of a coherent technical and local SEO foundation, alongside site speed, mobile usability, content quality, link authority, and Google Business Profile management. But it is a component that most dental practices have not addressed, which means implementing it correctly is an area where well-run practices can pull ahead of competitors who are still relying on incomplete or default implementations.

If your practice is investing in paid search, social media, or referral programmes to grow your patient base, it makes sense to ensure that the organic search foundation supporting all of that activity is as strong as it can be. Schema is a meaningful part of that foundation.
